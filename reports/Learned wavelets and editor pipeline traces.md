# Whole-frame decoding erases what wavelets localize

Your own results already answer the central question, and the 2026 literature agrees with them: **in modern generative editors the low-level trace is global, not local**. Your wavelet, lifting and shearlet streams detect regeneration well at image level (0.90 AUC on format-matched OpenSDI) but localize poorly (0.55–0.56 cross-dataset) and sit at chance on GPT-Image-2. Frozen semantic tokens (DINOv2-L6, 0.77 / 0.68) win, and they absorb everything the sparse streams add. The published state of the art on closed MLLM editors is no better than your 0.58. PIXAR-7B reaches **11.7% IoU on GPT-Image-1.5**, humans manage 10.7% on the same benchmark ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)), and the best domain-generalized model averages **0.222 gIoU** over GPT-Image-2, Gemini-3.1, FLUX.2 and Seedream 4.5 ([PIXAR-DG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18230)). The editors share five stages: resample to a model grid, encode, generate, optionally blend the original back in, and decode every pixel through a learned decoder, followed by post-processing. Only two of those stages, the blend and the content the denoiser generates, can leave a trace that differs between edited and unedited regions, and mask-free MLLM editors skip the blend entirely. The best use of your wavelet expertise is therefore not another pooled multiscale statistic. It is wavelets as *instruments*: (a) subband-resolved perturbation probes of frozen ViT tokens; (b) a round-trip idempotence test that separates re-decoded from de-novo content; (c) an image-level "regenerated or pasted back" gate where your 0.90 detection result is real. MLLM internals are a poor bet on 6 GB: their LLM layers carry semantic rather than artifact signal, and their adapters throw away spatial resolution. The single most valuable next step costs almost nothing. Audit the GPT-Image-2 and Nano-Banana-2 masks against pixel-difference maps before treating 0.58 as a property of the edits rather than of the labels.

## Your results show a global decoder trace and a semantic local signal

Read together, your experiments form a controlled demonstration of a mechanism that the literature has so far only shown in pieces. The same multiscale streams that score **0.90 on whole-image OpenSDI detection** score 0.66 at partial-edit localization and 0.55 in transfer. SRM falls from 0.83 to 0.64 over the same gap. A feature that separates real from generated images but not edited from unedited patches within one image is measuring something present across the whole regenerated frame. INP-X demonstrated this from the other direction. When the original pixels outside the mask are restored, so that the decoder trace is confined to the edit, commercial and academic detectors collapse to chance: **Corvi2023 0.942 → 0.554, Hive 0.914 → 0.548, Sightengine 0.926 → 0.550**. They still hold 0.80–0.89 under JPEG q=80 ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). The authors show that VAE reconstruction "induces a subtle but pervasive spectral shift across the entire image, including unedited regions" ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). Your 0.50 for every low-level stream on GPT-Image-2 is what this mechanism predicts for an editor with no paste-back. Both regions come out of the same decoder, so any content-independent statistic of the decoder output is identical in both.

The per-dataset ordering matches pipeline structure. AutoSplice (0.91) and CocoGlide (0.89) are splice-style: generated content is pasted into camera pixels, so a boundary and a regime change exist in the pixels. CocoGlide in particular is GLIDE output pasted into real images, which preserves camera traces outside the edit ([TruFor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957)). DiffSeg30k (0.83) and SID-Set (0.85) come next. OpenSDI (0.70), Nano-Banana-2 (0.69) and GPT-Image-2 (0.58) trail, the last two being mask-free whole-frame regenerators. The same hierarchy appears independently across three groups. GPT-Image reaches about 11.7 IoU ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)) and Nano-Banana 0.216 IoU for FLAME ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02178v1)), against roughly 0.45–0.57 IoU for open DiT editors held out under ICL-Net ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)). Camera-trace localizers behave the same way on TGIF2: TruFor scores 0.75 F1 on spliced inpainting and **0.14 on fully regenerated (FR) inpainting**, and CAT-Net falls from 0.89 to 0.02 ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)).

Your fusion result, where DINOv2-L6 plus all sparse streams stays at 0.77 / 0.68, matches the strongest 2026 ablation. FUSED pairs a sparse-attention forensic branch with a frozen CLIP-ConvNeXt-XXL. Removing the frozen semantic branch drops average localization F1 to **19.1**, and the authors state that low-level cues "tend to generalize poorly across different generators" ([FUSED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28302)). The low-level streams contribute nothing on top of DINOv2 in your experiments. They are either redundant with what early ViT layers already encode, or they carry only the global trace, which a per-patch probe cannot use. Your L6 result also fits the layer literature. Learned linear probes for low-level evidence peak early. TRAIL's training-free perturbation drift peaks late, at normalized depth 0.80–0.94 ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). Whole-image detection signal concentrates in middle blocks and register tokens ([Forensic Reserve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08639)).

Three of your secondary results deserve more attention than they have had. First, **the calibration gap (AUC 0.77, F1 ≈ 0.4 at threshold 0.5) is universal, not a defect of your probe**. FUSED reports ROC-AUC 92.4 but accuracy 62.6 at 0.5 on CocoGlide ([FUSED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28302)). TRAIL's Dice rises from .619 at a fixed threshold to .790 at a per-image oracle threshold ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). Second, **a one-class Gaussian on real patch features scoring *below* chance is a signal, not a failure**. It means edited patches sit *closer* to the real-feature mean than real patches do. That is consistent with Cozzolino et al.'s finding that regenerated content has lower latent variance and lower effective dimensionality ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)), and with ZED's premise that generators sample near high-density modes ([ZED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)). If the inversion is stable across datasets, "too typical" is a usable score. Third, **the inconsistent sign of LiftNet surprisal** (0.61–0.70 on SID-Set and TGIF2 FLUX FR/SP, 0.36 on DiffSeg30k) is the same phenomenon seen through a coder. Generated content looks more or less predictable than its surroundings depending on what the surroundings are: camera pixels, a VAE reconstruction, or output from a different editor. Image-relative normalization cannot fix a sign flip. It rescales the score; it does not tell the probe which side of the background distribution the edit lies on. A two-sided or regime-conditioned score can, and your TGIF2 matched FR/SP subsets can test this directly.

Three limitations recur across this literature and in your galleries. The first is **object bias**. TruFor fine-tuned on FR semantic masks scores 0.50 F1 on semantic masks but 0.23 on random rectangles ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)). GPT-Image-2 alters surrounding, non-target text in 44.2% of receipt edits ([GPT-Image-2 forgery study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13617)), and PIXAR finds relighting halos, colour bleeding and seam removal outside intended masks ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193)). ## Five shared stages, and only two can mark the edit

Reverse-engineering the pipelines gives a simple, testable model. Every editor examined, open or closed, performs at least two stages: it **resamples the input onto a model grid**, and it **produces every output pixel with a learned decoder**. Latent diffusion and flow editors (SD1.5/2/XL inpainting, SD3, FLUX.1 Fill and Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, BAGEL, OmniGen2) all encode with a convolutional KL-VAE at 8× compression. Older models have 4 latent channels; newer ones have 16 behind 16-px transformer tokens ([LDM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752); [FLUX.1 Kontext](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15742v2); [Qwen-Image](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02324v1); [BAGEL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14683)). Discrete autoregressive editors (Janus-Pro, Emu3.5, Show-o, VAR, Infinity) use 16× quantizing tokenizers and a learned or diffusion decoder ([Emu3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26583); [Janus-Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17811)). GPT-Image and Nano-Banana are undisclosed. OpenAI forces output sides to multiples of 16, treats a mask as guidance that "may not follow its exact shape", and always processes inputs at "high fidelity" on gpt-image-2 ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Gemini emits fixed 0.5K/1K/2K/4K buckets with a SynthID watermark ([Gemini image docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). PIXAR had to align Gemini outputs with ORB+RANSAC homography before it could difference them ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193)).

The source image reaches the output through one of three routes, which define three forensic regimes. In **per-step latent blending** (non-inpaint SD checkpoints in diffusers), the pipeline re-noises the original latent and blends it with the 8×-downsampled mask at every step. The background is a VAE reconstruction, and the boundary is quantized to the 8-px latent lattice before decoding ([diffusers inpaint pipeline](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion_inpaint.py)). In **pixel paste-back**, the background is bit-exact camera content with a hard or feathered seam. Paste-back happens only when the pipeline opts in: diffusers `apply_overlay` "at the expense of some more unnatural transitions", `padding_mask_crop`, which crops, upscales, inpaints and overlays, and BrushNet's default blurred-mask blend, justified because VAE encoding "may not ensure complete image reconstruction" ([diffusers inpainting guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/using-diffusers/inpaint); [BrushNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06976)). In **mask-free conditioning** (Kontext's sequence concatenation, Qwen-Image-Edit's dual VLM + VAE tokens, BAGEL, all MLLM editors), there is no pixel path at all. The "unchanged" region is at best a reconstruction, bounded at roughly 31 dB PSNR for the FLUX autoencoder at 256², plus generative drift ([FLUX.1 Kontext](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15742v2)).

|---|---|---|---|---|

Stage 5 is the most studied trace and the least useful for localization. Up-convolution and VAE decoders leave periodic spectral peaks and attenuated high frequencies ([Corvi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00680); [Durall et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01826)). VQ tokenizers leave quantization error and codebook-frequency bias that D³QE exploits at image level ([D³QE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05891)). But a diffusion-autoencoder pass alone makes AI-image detectors "flag most authentic images", and seven zero-shot detectors reach AUROC ≤ 0.745 on processing-matched controls ([processing-matched controls benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26232)). Pooled VFM features are near zero on pure VAE reconstructions, e.g. DINOv3 0.030 on SDXL reconstructions ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)). Generative super-resolution adds a second global decoder layer and "significantly weakens forensic traces" ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)).

Stage 4 explains your easy datasets, and it is where your 24×24 pooling costs most. FLAME's energy map shows edited regions as "low-energy voids bounded by spikes at the boundary". Removing that map halves IoU (0.567 → 0.294), and the same signal drops to 0.216 IoU on Nano-Banana, where there is no boundary to spike ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02178v1)). A BrushNet-style feather band of about 21 px is a fraction of one cell at 1024² on a 24×24 grid. Cell-level statistics then average a two-regime mixture into one moment, which is the worst case for wavelet energy and phase features.

Stage 3 is the only stage that can separate edited from unedited pixels in a GPT-Image or Nano-Banana output, and its evidence is thin but specific. On Qwen-Image-Edit, edited regions show "significantly reduced colorfulness and brightness" ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577v1)). Regenerated content has lower latent variance and dimensionality ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). VAE reconstruction-loss maps correlate only moderately with inpainted pixels (pixel r = 0.45 ± 0.23) and more strongly with local high-frequency content (r = 0.52) ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). Localizers trained on masked VAE-reconstruction data transfer poorly to real inpainting (OOD F1 44.6 vs 61.4) ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20929v1)). This yields the decisive hypothesis for your pending Proposal 2. Re-encoded camera content and de-novo content both lie near the decoder's range, but they got there by different paths. A second encode–decode should therefore be closer to idempotent on de-novo content than on reconstructed content. The nearest published methods use reconstruction only as a single-pass residual or a global token, and an arXiv search for re-editing or regeneration-consistency localizers returned nothing after September 2025 ([ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882); [GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)). One more stage-3 fact undercuts the labels themselves. Editors over-edit, "modifying content beyond the user's intent" ([Agent Banana](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09084)). When GT masks come from object segmentation, real changed pixels outside the mask are labelled authentic and unchanged pixels inside it are labelled edited. PIXAR's pixel-difference GT at τ = 0.05 exists for exactly this reason ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)).

## Learned wavelets earn a place as instruments, not as pooled features

The literature contains **no forensic evaluation of learned lifting or learned wavelet transforms**. iWave, learned-lifting DWT codecs and normalizing-flow wavelets were built for compression and are optimized to remove predictable structure ([learned lifting DWT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03616); [nonlinear wavelet flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11306)). Wavelet backbones such as WaveMix, Wave-ViT and WaveCNet use the DWT as a downsampling operator, not a forensic feature. The 2026 wavelet localizer, SegWave, uses a fixed single-level Haar DWT with learned sub-band attention injected into a SAM-style ViT. It reaches CocoGlide F1 0.69 against SAFIRE's 0.63, but sub-band attention matters little for that score (0.69 vs 0.67 without it). It matters much more for separating multiple sources (pmIoU 0.821 vs 0.695), and SegWave was never tested on an instruction or MLLM editor ([SegWave](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30714)). Your LiftNet is, as far as these notes can establish, the first learned probabilistic lifting transform evaluated for generative-edit localization. Its result, 0.66 / 0.56 and statistically indistinguishable from DT-CWT, is itself a publishable negative: learning the filter bank on real photos did not escape the global-trace ceiling.

That result also lowers the prior on most untested harmonic-analysis paradigms. Your DT-CWT stream already included cross-scale phase coherence, so the strongest candidate from higher-order spectral analysis, a cross-scale phase-coupling map, has partly been tested and landed at 0.66 / 0.55. Bicoherence, multifractal log-cumulants, Gaussian-scale-mixture joint statistics and random-matrix patch-covariance spectra are different *statistics* of the same subband coefficients. Computed per cell on a fully regenerated image, each inherits the same problem: both regions came through the same decoder. They remain worth a cheap screen only because some of them are inherently self-referential. The wavelet-leader surrogate test of Wendt et al. flags pixels inconsistent with a global multifractal model fitted on the same image. It was designed for "local anomaly in a homogeneous field" and has never been applied to forensics ([Wendt et al., ICIP 2022](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03735492)). Lyu et al.'s blind local-noise estimation is the proven dense precedent for this kind of test on splices ([Lyu et al., IJCV 2014](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~siweilyu/papers/ijcv14.pdf)). Be candid with yourself about the prior, though. Your per-image median/MAD normalization, the simplest self-referential transform, gained only +0.01–0.02. These methods should be screened on TGIF2 FR versus SP for a day, and dropped if FR stays below about 0.60.

The paradigms that do have evidence share one property: they **model real images and score deviation**, rather than classifying frequency features. Examples are SPAI's masked spectral learning (+5.5% AUC across 13 generators) ([SPAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19417)), ZED's coding cost ([ZED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)), forensic self-descriptions ([Self-Descriptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21003)) and score-curvature detection across 20 generators ([Brokman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15470)). All four are image-level. Your LiftNet surprisal *is* the dense version of ZED, and it came out weak and sign-unstable, which says the global offset swamps the local contrast. A learned-codec rate map (hyperprior or ELIC bits per latent) would be a stronger coder of the same idea. It carries the same risk, plus a known confound: JPEG AI artifacts "closely resemble" synthesis and splicing artifacts ([JPEG AI forensics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03261)).

Wavelets have a clear role in three places, each grounded in evidence. The first is **wavelets as perturbations of frozen ViTs**. TRAIL applies one global Haar high-frequency attenuation and maps the cosine drift of DINO patch tokens. That alone gives **patch AUROC .903 on CocoGlide against .912 for a mask-supervised model**, and context matters: AUROC drops to .735 with independently encoded crops ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). WaRPAD measures DINO-family embedding sensitivity along Haar high-frequency directions, but only at image level ([WaRPAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14030)). DEAL-300K's frozen VFM with multi-frequency prompt tuning holds the best CocoGlide F1 (80.97) ([DEAL-300K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23377)). None of them resolves the drift by *scale and orientation*. A DT-CWT perturbation bank produces a per-patch sensitivity profile with 6 orientations × 3–4 scales, from early and late ViT layers. That profile is a compact, spatially aligned frequency feature that keeps DINOv2's context, and it uses exactly your expertise. The second role is **wavelets as an image-level gate**. Your 0.90 OpenSDI detection AUC is a genuine capability. Train it to classify the *regime* (paste-back vs regenerated vs pristine) on TGIF2 SP/FR/original, then route or condition the patch probe on it. The third role is **wavelets at the boundary**. Seam and feather bands are local by definition, and multiscale analysis at native resolution, rather than 24×24 cells, is the right tool for a two-regime mixture. This only helps on splice-type and paste-back data, which is already your strongest regime.

Sparse representations offer one cheap, open slot. Patch-level sparse autoencoders on frozen DINOv2/CLIP tokens have public tooling ([saev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06755)). A 32k-concept archetypal SAE on DINOv2 already finds concepts "exaggerated" in generated images ([RA-SAE concept comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19708)). Yet **no paper uses SAE features for generated-image detection or localization**; the closest is interpretability-only ([Deepfake Detective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21670)). An SAE is a near-linear reparametrization, so it will not beat the dense tokens in-domain. Its value lies in identifying and removing object-concept latents, which attacks the object bias in your galleries, and in isolating dataset-agnostic "trace" latents, which could improve transfer. Unrolled sparse-coding backbones (SDNet, CRATE) have ImageNet weights and robustness claims ([SDNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12945)) but no forensic evidence. Expect them below DINOv2.

## MLLM towers and generator internals add little beyond DINOv2 on 6 GB

Your own encoder ranking already tests the core premise. **Language-aligned encoders do worse than self-supervised ones on your task**: SigLIP2-B scores 0.72 / 0.65, CLIP-B/16 0.74 / 0.67, DINOv2-B L6 0.77 / 0.68. MLLM vision towers are close relatives of SigLIP and CLIP, further trained with LLM-alignment objectives: SigLIP-so400m in Gemma-3 and PaliGemma, the ViT in Qwen2.5-VL, InternViT. The only systematic frozen-probe study covers their standalone versions. It finds them strong whole-image detectors (PE-CLIP 0.899, DINOv3 0.940 average in-the-wild accuracy) but near chance on localized edits with pooled features (0.45–0.62 on BR-Gen) and blind to VAE reconstruction ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)). Those numbers come from pooled features under a whole-image label, so they do not bound your patch probe. They do show that MLLM towers bring no special forensic knowledge. One architectural detail argues for a single cheap test. Qwen's ViT runs at native resolution with 2D-RoPE, avoiding the 224/518 resize that destroys high-frequency traces. Tap it *before* the 2×2 patch merger, which halves spatial resolution.

LLM hidden states over image tokens are a weaker source still. The one layer-wise probing study on Qwen2.5-VL-7B finds semantic forgery information converging in early-to-middle LLM layers, while **artifact-forgery accuracy stays flat at about 81% across all depths**. The authors argue the frozen LLM "suppresses high-frequency artifact signals" ([Deep-VRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15880)). A mechanistic study finds the vision-to-LLM adapter causes a segmentation "drop-off" that later LLM layers only partly recover. Causal attention also limits recovery for early raster positions ([MLLM segmentation probe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17228)). IFDL-VLM goes further: VLM priors "hardly benefit" detection and localization and can hurt, because of "inherent biases toward semantic plausibility rather than authenticity" ([IFDL-VLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12930)). The explainable MLLM localizers (SIDA, FakeShield, PIXAR) get their masks from a SAM image encoder prompted by a [SEG] token, not from the LLM's image tokens. Their mIoU sits around 0.15–0.26 on generative edits ([GRPO-VLM study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28464); [PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)). A frozen-LLM-state probe would give you a semantic "what looks implausible" map, which is the signal DINOv2 already supplies, from a 4-bit 3B model at a coarser grid. A head on MLLM hidden states can match token generation for closed decisions (0.98 AUC at 53 ms vs 386 ms) ([MAD-Guard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33683)), but that is detection, after LoRA training. MLLMs are useful on 6 GB only as offline region *proposers* or priors, never as the decision, because their plausibility bias predicts the most misses on exactly the photorealistic GPT-Image-2 edits you care about.

Generator internals split into an expensive part and a cheap part. The expensive part is U-Net or DiT features. An SD1.5 U-Net (about 860M parameters) fits in fp16, and HIDE used its h-space to detect diffusion inpainting ([HIDE, CVPRW 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/SAFE/supplemental/Ji_HIDE_Detecting_Diffusion-Based_CVPRW_2026_supplemental.pdf)). But these features are semantic, the same kind that DIFT and hyperfeatures exploit for correspondence, and they offer no clear advantage over DINOv2 patches. The cheap part is the **VAE encoder and decoder** themselves, used not as features but as an *operator*. Your single-pass SD-VAE reconstruction error (0.62 / 0.53) is weak, as the literature predicts: reconstruction error tracks texture ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). The double round-trip compares x→D(E(x)) with D(E(x))→D(E(D(E(x)))), so it measures *convergence*, not error, and it partly cancels texture dependence. It is the one generator-internal experiment with a mechanistic reason to work. Pick the VAE family to match the generator: 4-channel SD for SD-family sets, 16-channel FLUX for TGIF2 FLUX and Kontext-like data. The closest supervised precedent is DinoLizer. It labels VAE-reconstructed areas outside the mask as a separate class on a DINOv2 backbone with a single linear layer, and gains about 20% IoU over the next model ([DinoLizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20722)).

### Proposal A: a stage-wise trace audit of regenerated edits (highest certainty)

The cleanest publishable contribution is already 70% done. It is a controlled study showing that, across seven datasets and a matched TGIF2 FR/SP/original design, low-level multiscale streams (fixed, learned-lifting, shearlet, SRM, VAE error) detect regeneration at image level (0.83–0.90) but localize only where a blend stage exists. Frozen semantic tokens carry the local signal and absorb the low-level streams. A pixel-difference relabelling of GPT-Image-2 and NB2 then quantifies how much of the "hard editor" gap is label definition. The stage table above is the paper's organizing frame. The research notes found no 2026 paper that measures which low-level statistics survive GPT-Image or Nano-Banana decoding, and none that reports results on both object-mask and pixel-difference GT. PIXAR argues for the latter but evaluates only on its own labels ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)). The main risk is that reviewers read it as a negative-results paper. The defence is the matched-pairs design, the label audit and the stage model, which together tell the field where to stop looking. It is entirely feasible on 6 GB, and fits TIFS, a CVPR/ICCV workshop, or WIFS.

### Proposal B: subband-resolved wavelet sensitivity probing of frozen ViTs (best fit with your record)

Perturb the image by attenuating or phase-rotating individual DT-CWT subbands, one scale and orientation at a time. Record the per-patch cosine drift of DINOv2 tokens at L6 and at a late block, and append the resulting profile of about 24–48 numbers to the token for the linear probe. As a training-free variant, score the profile's deviation from the image's own median profile. This keeps what works (frozen context-mixing tokens, single-pass full-image encoding, per TRAIL's crop result) and adds a frequency axis that is spatially aligned rather than pooled. It also connects directly to your Enhanced Wavelet Scattering work: the scattering transform's invariances become a *perturbation basis*, not a feature. It is new relative to TRAIL (one global Haar perturbation, training-free, late layers only) ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)), WaRPAD (image-level) ([WaRPAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14030)) and SegWave (Haar sub-band attention inside a trained ViT, classic forgeries only) ([SegWave](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30714)). The main risk is that the profile is redundant with L6 tokens, just as your sparse streams were. The decisive control is the NB2/GPT-Image-2 split, where the drift asks the ViT how *context-consistent* each patch is, not what its subband energy is. Cost is about 25 forward passes of ViT-B per image, well within 6 GB. Run it after experiment 4 confirms a gain.

### Proposal C: round-trip idempotence for passive localization inside fully regenerated images (highest upside, gated)

If the pending double round-trip shows FR separation, frame it as a three-class provenance model (pristine / re-decoded / de-novo). It would use banded convergence ratios, per-patch latent effective rank, a wavelet regime gate and DINOv2 tokens, and it would be trained only on self-made VAE round-trips plus random-mask open-model inpainting, so that no object prior is learned. GAP-SAM compresses reconstruction into one global token, and ReGFLoW uses single-pass residuals as weak guidance ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20929v1); [ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)). Neither tests convergence, and no passive method separates the two FR classes. Run the INP-X control in both directions: restoring the background should flip "re-decoded" to "pristine" while "de-novo" stays fixed. That is the evidence a reviewer will demand. The biggest risk is the closed editors. If GPT-Image regenerates texture everywhere through an unknown decoder, the background is no longer a round-trip of camera content and the signal disappears. Reporting which editors leave a passive FR signal is publishable either way. Feasible on 6 GB with SD and FLUX VAEs in fp16 with tiling, using TGIF2's existing FLUX subsets in place of generating FLUX-Fill data.

### Proposal D: SAE-debiased dense probing against object bias (cheap, novel, moderate venue)

Train a patch-level SAE on DINOv2-L6 tokens from real and edited images. Identify latents that predict SAM object boundaries or category, and project them out before the probe. Report the random-mask vs semantic-mask gap as an object-bias index alongside a boundary-adhesion score. The literature has an object-bias diagnosis (TGIF2's 0.50 vs 0.23 F1) ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)) but no SAE-based remedy and no SAE forensic detector ([2512.21670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21670)). The risk is that edits *are* semantic, so removing object latents also removes signal. The rank-ablation curve is the honest answer. All of it trains on cached features in 6 GB. Paired with Proposal C as its debiasing layer, it strengthens a main-conference submission. Alone it is TIFS-level.

### Proposal E: calibrated maps with guarantees on authentic pixels (add-on)

Your F1 of about 0.4 at AUC 0.77 is a thresholding problem. Conformal risk control, calibrated on pristine and VAE-round-trip-only images, bounds the authentic-pixel false-discovery rate with no fake labels and stays valid under editor shift. The earlier novelty search found no conformal method for pixel-level forgery masks; the nearest work certifies image-level decisions or detection boxes ([conformal detection boxes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24038)), while the oracle-vs-fixed threshold gap it would close is well documented ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). It runs on CPU over cached score maps. Attach it to A, B or C rather than publishing it alone.

## Conclusion

Your experiments have quietly falsified the hypothesis that drove much of the earlier plan, namely that a better multiscale representation, fixed or learned, would localize regenerated edits. LiftNet, DT-CWT with phase coherence and shearlets all converge on the same ceiling because they read the decoder, and the decoder is everywhere. That is a result, not a dead end. It relocates the wavelet contribution from *description* to *interrogation*: perturbing a frozen context model subband by subband, iterating the editor's own encode–decode operator, and gating by regime. Those are the places where multiscale analysis can still find a local asymmetry. The inverted one-class score and the sign-flipping surrogate are the first hints of what that asymmetry looks like: edited content is *too typical* relative to its context, not anomalous.

The larger implication concerns GPT-Image-2. Passive pixel forensics has been asked to find a boundary that mask-free whole-frame editors do not draw, and the published state of the art (about 0.12–0.22 IoU, with humans at 0.11) suggests the ceiling is near. Before investing in any method for that editor, find out how much of your 0.58 is label error. If pixel-difference labels raise it substantially, the field has been mismeasuring the problem. If they do not, the defensible output for whole-frame editors is a calibrated, semantic change likelihood, and saying so with matched controls is itself a contribution reviewers have not yet seen.
