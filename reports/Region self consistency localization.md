# Make self-consistency a calibrated conditional test

Yes, localization of modern generative edits should be framed as region-wise self-consistency, but not in the form the question states. **"Each region is an independent sample from one image-wide law" is false for real images**: forensic statistics depend on intensity, texture and scale. Neighbouring cells are positively correlated. And under whole-frame re-decoding (fully regenerated inpainting, GPT-Image, Nano-Banana), the decoder-level statistics really are the same everywhere, so the null hypothesis is *true* for them. The sound version is a test of **conditional exchangeability**: after conditioning on content covariates, residual descriptors should be exchangeable across spatial *blocks* of an authentic image. Candidate regions are unions of small cells, scored with a two-sided distributional statistic. False alarms are controlled by an a-contrario number of false alarms (NFA) or a scan statistic, with tails calibrated on authentic and VAE-round-tripped authentic images. Your earlier colour/texture segments were the right instinct used at the wrong level. Content homogeneity is the correct way to *stratify or weight* samples, but it is the wrong unit of hypothesis, because generative edits coincide with objects. The literature documents the cost of segmentation errors ([Chierchia et al.](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)) and of object bias ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). Descriptor choice decides whether the framework can work at all. Frozen foundation-model *perturbation response* is the best-evidenced descriptor: training-free TRAIL reaches patch AUROC .903 on CocoGlide, against .912 for a mask-supervised model ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). Learned pairwise "same-source" comparators are the natural comparator. Content-normalised local energy and coding cost come next, and round-trip idempotence is the one cue designed for the fully regenerated case. Noise-level functions survive only where camera pixels are pasted back, and bispectrum, multifractal and random-matrix spectra remain unproven bets with high estimator variance. The final section gives thirteen small, falsifiable tests on your cached 24×24 features. Most run on CPU. Each has an explicit null, and they lead into one preregistered confirmatory test.

## Self-consistency is the right decision rule but the wrong signal source for re-decoded edits

The paradigm has three generations, and each succeeded exactly as far as its "one image, one process" assumption held. The first used hand-crafted local statistics compared with an image-wide model: noise variance from band-pass kurtosis ([Lyu et al., IJCV 2014](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~siweilyu/papers/ijcv14.pdf)), JPEG grid votes in ZERO ([IPOL 2021](https://ipol.im/pub/art/2021/390)), and Noisesniffer's background noise model with region growing ([IPOL 2024](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)). The IPOL a-contrario line is the only part of the field with explicit false-alarm control. The second generation learned the comparator: EXIF self-consistency trained on real photos only ([Huh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04096)), Forensic Similarity ([Mayer & Stamm](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684)) and its graph-community extension ([Forensic Similarity Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02861)), and EXIF-as-language with zero-shot patch clustering ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04647)). The third learned a camera fingerprint, Noiseprint++, then read its deviations with a *supervised* head (TruFor), so it is self-consistency-inspired rather than label-free at test time ([TruFor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957)).

Diffusion editing broke the shared assumption. TruFor's own re-measurement shows the unsupervised members falling to about chance on CocoGlide: **EXIF-SC F1 .424 and image AUC .526, Splicebuster .434 / .529, Noiseprint .405 / .520**, against .80–.88 F1 on Columbia ([TruFor tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.10957v3)). Full regeneration is worse. On TGIF, CAT-Net drops from **.87 F1 on spliced SD2 inpainting to .04 on fully regenerated SD2**, and TruFor from .83 to .19 ([TGIF](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11566v2)). DiffusionPrint names the mechanism: full-regeneration pipelines "reconstruct the entire image via a latent decoder", erasing camera-level noise ([DiffusionPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)). INP-X shows the converse. The VAE round trip causes a "pervasive spectral shift across the entire image, including unedited regions", and once the original pixels outside the mask are restored, detectors fall to near chance, for example from 91% to 55% ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). Your numbers match this mechanism. Low-level streams are near 0.50 on GPT-Image-2. Pooled low-level streams detect regeneration at image level (0.90 on format-matched OpenSDI) but localize at 0.62–0.66. **For a decoder statistic, the image-wide null "one process" is literally true in a whole-frame regeneration**, and no test, however sound, can reject a true null.

The newest work moves the self-consistency idea to two places. One is the *training loss*: NCL-IML ([arXiv 2309.14900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14900)), ICL-Net with IoU 62.5% in domain and 41.5% on unseen editors ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)), and DiffusionPrint's same-generator contrastive fingerprint. All of these have supervised test-time heads. The other is the *descriptor*. TRAIL encodes the full canvas with frozen DINOv3, applies one global Haar high-frequency perturbation, and scores each patch by its cosine drift. It is not a region-vs-rest test, yet it nearly matches mask supervision on CocoGlide. Its controls show that much of the raw signal is content: **raw-pixel control .641, aligned-real control .697–.743**, and AUROC drops from .811 to .727 when TGIF2 semantic masks are replaced by random ones ([TRAIL full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18968v1)). Your training-free kNN/prototype distances on DINOv2 tokens (0.55–0.67) sit against a pixel control of 0.62/0.59. That is the same story: **region-vs-rest dissimilarity of raw tokens mostly measures content**. TRAIL's gain comes from a perturbation *difference*, which cancels much of the semantics, from a late layer (normalised depth .80–.94), and from global context (.903 full canvas vs .735 independent crops).

The human analogy should be kept as an idea and dropped as a benchmark. People detect manipulated real-world photos at about 62%, "only slightly better than chance", and often cannot locate the edit even when they detect it ([Nightingale et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514174)). On modern VLM edits, ten annotators reached **localization IoU 10.7%**, on par with specialised MLLM localizers at 8–12% ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193v1)). What humans do well is compare like with like, for example this grass with that grass, or this skin with that skin. That is the statistical principle worth formalising. Their reliance on semantic plausibility is exactly what fails on photorealistic MLLM edits. The answer to the main question is therefore conditional. Self-consistency is the right *decision framework*, because it is label-free, gives per-image false-alarm control and is interpretable. It succeeds only with descriptors whose law depends on *how content was synthesised*, not on *which decoder produced the pixels*.

## Regions should be small cells tested in unions; colour segments were stratification, not hypotheses

Every forensic statistic you compute mixes trace with content. Raw sensor noise is Poisson–Gaussian and intensity-dependent ([Foi et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2008.2001399)). After the camera pipeline it depends on both intensity and frequency ([Colom et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2015.2438537)). Noisesniffer's survey notes that earlier blind noise methods "share the same drawback" of assuming one constant noise level, which later methods replaced with noise level functions ([IPOL 2024, Sec. 2](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)). The established remedy has two parts. First, estimate only on weak-texture patches ([Liu, Tanaka & Okutomi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2013.2283400); [Pyatykh et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2012.2221728)). Second, compare only within strata. Noisesniffer compares each block only with blocks of the same channel and intensity bin, keeps the lowest-energy blocks, and tests whether the lowest-noise subset is spatially uniform under a binomial null ([IPOL 2024, Sec. 3](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)). DT-CWT detail energy, sparsity, residual energy and LiftNet surprisal all carry a signal term that scales with local contrast. Without stratification, a "noise inconsistency" map largely becomes a texture map.

Your 2024 method segmented by colour and compared DT-CWT noise across segments, evaluated on a self-built dataset ([Barglazan & Brad, arXiv 2408.06429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06429)). Judged against the evidence, segmentation as the *unit of comparison* was a mistake, for four reasons. **First, it confounds the edit with the object.** Text-guided inpainting replaces an object, and colour segmentation returns that object as one segment. Any between-segment difference in texture, sharpness or smoothness then "localises" the edit without measuring a trace, and it lights up authentic objects just as readily. TGIF2 introduced random masks precisely because they reveal this object bias ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). COCO-Inpaint was built to emphasise intrinsic inconsistencies over "superficial semantic artifacts such as object shapes" ([COCO-Inpaint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18361)). **Second, segmentation errors propagate.** Chierchia et al. abandoned preliminary segmentation for PRNU localization because "segmentation, however, is itself a source of errors" with "heavy impact" on performance. Their soft, guided-filter weights raised AUC from **0.63 to 0.78 for 48×48 forgeries** ([Chierchia et al. 2014](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)). **Third, boundary mismatch.** When the edit mask cuts a segment (random masks, editor dilation, background inpainting), the segment statistic averages two regimes, the "strongly biased and basically useless" case in the same paper. **Fourth, unequal variance.** Segment sizes span orders of magnitude, so raw differences flag small segments by chance unless the test is size-aware.

The instinct behind segmentation, compare like with like, is correct, and it belongs in the *conditioning*. The design the literature supports, and the one these notes recommend, has four steps. (1) Compute descriptors on small cells, ideally aligned to the 8-px JPEG grid and to the 8- or 16-px latent lattice of the editor. (2) Fit an image-wide conditional model E[descriptor | intensity bin, texture class, subband], robustly and on weak-texture cells. (3) Convert each cell to a standardised residual, or better to a rank within its stratum, which is invariant to monotone content-dependent scaling. (4) Test *candidate regions* built as unions of cells, either multi-scale windows or 4-connected polyominoes grown from seeds as in Noisesniffer, with explicit false-alarm control ([IPOL 2024, Sec. 3.2–3.3](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)). Homogeneity then selects and weights samples, and the hypothesis unit is never a segment.

Region size is a power question with a closed-form first answer. For a variance estimated from N effectively independent samples, the relative standard error is about √(2/N). Comparing log-variance between a region and a much larger rest gives z ≈ δ/√(2/N₁). Detecting a 10% change in noise standard deviation (δ ≈ 0.2 in log-variance) at z = 3 needs N₁ ≈ 2(3/0.2)² ≈ 450 *effective* samples, and a 5% change needs about 1,800. Higher-order statistics cost far more: the sampling variance of excess kurtosis is 24/N under normality ([semTools](https://rdocumentation.org/packages/semTools/versions/0.5-6/topics/kurtosis)). Spatial correlation shrinks effective N further. With separable AR(1) correlation ρ, n_eff ≈ n((1−ρ)/(1+ρ))², so ρ = 0.5 divides N by about 9. The empirical record agrees. PRNU needs windows of about 128×128 and discarded detections below 64×64 ([Chierchia et al.](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)). Forensic Similarity loses accuracy from 256 to 128 px patches (91.83% vs 88.63% at JPEG Q95) ([Mayer & Stamm](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684)). TRAIL fails on edits covering 2–8% of the frame ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18968v1)).

Two consequences follow for your grid. At a native long side of 1024, a 24×24 cell is about 43 px: enough pixels for a stratified variance statistic, but only a fraction of each cell is weak-texture. Per-cell kurtosis, bispectrum or multifractal estimates will be dominated by estimation noise. Second, **the smallest detectable edit is set by how many stratum-matched, effectively independent samples fall inside it, not by its pixel area.** A feather band of about 21 px, or an edit spanning two cells, is invisible to cell-level moments however good the descriptor is.

One caution applies to your running `matched` scorer. A generative edit is content-coherent, so a cell inside it has its nearest content neighbours *inside the same edit*. Excluding only the 3×3 neighbourhood leaves those cells in the reference, and the matched reference then absorbs the edit, which biases large homogeneous edits towards AUC 0.5. The `regress` scorer has the mirror problem: the ridge fit uses edited cells too. Both should be cross-fitted. Draw the reference only from spatially distant blocks, for example the other half of a 2×2 or 3×3 block partition, and fit the regression robustly with Huber or least-trimmed squares. This is an inference from the code, not a published result. It costs nothing to check (Test 3).

## Descriptors must break stationarity through synthesis, not through decoding

Three editing regimes determine which descriptors can work. In **paste-back**, camera pixels survive outside the edit (AutoSplice, CocoGlide, TGIF2 SP). In **latent blending**, the background is a VAE reconstruction and the boundary sits on the latent lattice. In **mask-free whole-frame re-decoding**, the "unchanged" region is itself a reconstruction plus generative drift (GPT-Image, Nano-Banana, Kontext/Qwen-style editors, TGIF2 FR). A descriptor is useful inside a self-consistency framework only if its conditional law differs between edited and unedited cells *within one image* in the regime at hand. The ranking below applies that criterion, together with evidence, content sensitivity and sample cost.

**Perturbation drift ranks first** for three reasons, and none of them is that DINO "understands" forgery. It is a difference of two forward passes, so the semantic component that dominates raw tokens largely cancels. Your raw-token self-consistency sitting at the pixel-control level is the expected result without that cancellation. The full-canvas encode builds context into each token, so drift asks how *context-consistent* a patch is. The cue reacts to interpolated content with no generator involved (Poisson interpolation .855), which is the closest available evidence that it can transfer to editors it has never seen. Its gaps are specific. There are no numbers on GPT-Image or Nano-Banana. Small edits fail. The best block for drift is late, whereas your supervised optimum is layer 6. These are cheap to probe (Test 10). The subband-resolved extension (drift under each DT-CWT scale and orientation) is where your wavelet expertise adds something TRAIL and WaRPAD do not have.

**Learned pairwise similarity ranks second** because it *is* the comparator the framework needs. It replaces a hand-chosen Mahalanobis distance with a learned "same process?" function, and graph spectral methods turn its affinity matrix into a partition. Spectral clustering on Forensic Similarity Graphs reportedly reached 0.86 on Columbia against 0.74 for Splicebuster ([Forensic Similarity Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02861)). The risk is identifiable in advance. A model trained on camera images only learns "same camera pipeline", and under full re-decoding both regions share the decoder. Adding positives in which both crops are VAE round trips, and negatives that pair a round-trip crop with a generated crop, would make it learn "same decoding origin". This breaks the strict real-only rule but needs no specific editor. Fang & Stamm showed that Siamese self-consistency localizers can be fooled by synthesised traces ([arXiv 2211.12314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12314)). That adversarial weakness matters for deployment, not for the scientific question.

Your own results contain a descriptor-agnostic clue that changes *which* statistic to compute. A one-class Gaussian on real foundation-model patches scored **below** chance, and LiftNet surprisal flips sign across datasets (0.36–0.70). Both say that edited cells are often **too typical**, closer to the centre of the descriptor distribution than authentic cells. Regenerated content having lower latent variance and dimensionality fits this ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). A region-vs-rest test on *location* (robust z of the median) cannot see a region whose median matches the image but whose spread is compressed. **The test statistic must be two-sided and distributional**: energy distance or MMD between region and rest, or a pair of statistics combining a location shift with a scale ratio (log of within-region MAD over rest MAD). A one-sided "more anomalous" score will look sign-unstable across datasets even when the signal is consistent.

Bispectrum, multifractal and RMT spectra deserve candour. They are different statistics of the same subband coefficients your DT-CWT stream already summarised (including cross-scale phase coherence, at 0.66/0.55), and per 43-px cell their variance is prohibitive. RMT has a distinct merit inside this framework: its noise-floor estimate comes with a known null, the Marchenko–Pastur bulk with a Tracy–Widom edge, so it upgrades the NLF descriptor rather than adding a new trace. Screen it on paste-back data, where noise cues work. Do not expect it to rescue FR. Treat MDL as part of the decision machinery rather than as a descriptor: a two-region MDL split *is* a penalised likelihood ratio with a perimeter penalty (next section). The per-region coding-cost descriptor is the ZED/LiftNet item already ranked.

## Soundness comes from conditional exchangeability, dependence-aware nulls and counted tests

Write the problem precisely. Cell i has descriptor x_i ∈ ℝ^d and content covariates c_i (intensity, gradient energy, texture class; your `pixels` stream). The model-free null is **H0: the residuals r_i = x_i − m(c_i) are exchangeable across spatial blocks of side ≥ the correlation length L**. The alternative is that a connected set S has a different conditional law. Three choices make this sound. Exchangeability is assumed at the level of *blocks*, not cells, because cells are dependent. The residual is taken after conditioning, because the raw descriptor is not identically distributed. And the reference m(·) is fitted robustly and cross-fitted, because the edit must not contaminate it.

**Two-sample statistics.** For a fixed candidate region against the rest, use energy distance or a characteristic-kernel MMD. These are consistent nonparametric tests, and energy distance is MMD with a distance-induced kernel ([Gretton et al. 2012](https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/gretton12a.html); [Sejdinovic et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-AOS1140)). Their null is a degenerate weighted χ² sum, so calibration needs resampling. A plain permutation is exact only under exchangeability. For dependent data, standard permutation "breaks the temporal dependence" and yields "an elevated number of false positives", which the wild bootstrap fixes ([Chwialkowski, Sejdinovic & Gretton 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.5404)). Its theory covers 1-D mixing sequences, so applying it to 2-D image fields is an inference that should be checked by simulation (Test 2). For scalar descriptors (a noise level, a drift magnitude), KS or Anderson–Darling with a block-corrected effective n is cheap and interpretable. Hotelling's T² has an exact F null only under Gaussian i.i.d. data with n > d. Box's M is too sensitive to heavy tails to use on wavelet residuals. Kernel bandwidth must be chosen on a disjoint split, otherwise the permutation test is invalid.

**A-contrario NFA.** For a candidate e with tail probability p_e under the background model, NFA(e) = N_tests · p_e, and e is accepted if NFA ≤ ε. The guarantee **E[#false detections] ≤ ε** follows from super-uniformity of each p_e and linearity of expectation alone, so it holds under *any* dependence between tests, including overlapping windows, multiple scales and nested polyominoes ([Desolneux, Moisan & Morel 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-74378-3)). It is Bonferroni stated as a per-family error rate. ZERO, Noisesniffer and the a-contrario CFA methods use it ([ZERO](https://ipol.im/pub/art/2021/390); [Bammey et al.](https://ipol.im/pub/art/2021/355)). Noisesniffer's N_tests counts every polyomino up to half the image, with p_n ≈ 0.3169·4.0626ⁿ/n ([IPOL 2024](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)). The weak point is *within*-test dependence. A binomial tail over correlated cell votes underestimates the true tail, and the NFA becomes anti-conservative. The fixes are disjoint or thinned cells at stride ≥ L, tails estimated from per-image or corpus backgrounds, and, without exception, an empirical check that authentic images yield at most ε detections on average.

**Multiplicity and scan statistics.** Decide the error criterion first. For "is there a forged region, and where?", the natural answer is image-level family-wise control: an NFA, or a Kulldorff-style scan statistic that maximises a likelihood ratio over windows and calibrates the maximum by Monte Carlo. For per-cell heatmaps, Benjamini–Hochberg controls the false discovery rate under independence or positive regression dependence (plausible but unproven for smoothed residual maps). Benjamini–Yekutieli is valid under arbitrary dependence at a cost of about ln m. Conformal p-values computed against an authentic calibration set are positively dependent, and BH on them controls FDR ([Bates et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)). fMRI supplies the cautionary precedent. Parametric random-field cluster inference produced **familywise error rates up to 70% at a nominal 5%**, while permutation stayed nominal ([Eklund, Nichols & Knutsson 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4948312)). So calibrate cluster-extent or peak thresholds by block permutation or authentic-corpus Monte Carlo, never by Gaussian-field formulas.

**Robust statistics and the 50% breakdown.** Median and MAD encode "the majority is authentic" with the maximal breakdown point of 50%, and so do least-trimmed-squares and MCD in the multivariate case ([Rousseeuw 1984](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1984.10477105)). Within one image, H1 is label-symmetric: statistics can say *that* two populations exist and where the boundary lies, but not *which* one is authentic. When the edit covers more than half the image, a median reference flags the authentic remainder, and per-image AUC inverts. This is an identifiability limit, not a tuning issue. The sound response has two stages. First, detect inconsistency symmetrically, with a two-community test or MMD between estimated communities. Second, assign authenticity using an asymmetric reference: an authentic-corpus calibration, a VAE-round-trip null, or a JPEG grid aligned with the image origin. Without such a reference, report an "inconsistent pair".

**Change-point, segmentation and MDL.** For Gaussian residuals, the two-region description-length gain ΔL = n log σ̂²_all − n_R log σ̂²_R − n_Rᶜ log σ̂²_Rᶜ − λ(k log n + c|∂R|) is simultaneously an MDL criterion ([Rissanen 1978](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(78)90005-5)), a Potts/Mumford–Shah energy with a perimeter term ([Mumford & Shah 1989](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160420503)), and a penalised likelihood-ratio scan. For binary labels with a per-cell data term, graph cut solves it exactly ([Boykov, Veksler & Zabih 2001](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.969114)). The penalty alone does not control false alarms. Either replace it with an NFA-style log N_tests term or calibrate it by Monte Carlo on authentic images. Spectral partitioning of a pairwise-similarity graph is the learned-comparator analogue. A small Fiedler eigenvalue λ₂ certifies a sparse cut through Cheeger's inequality, λ₂/2 ≤ h(G) ≤ √(2λ₂) ([von Luxburg 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0189)). No closed-form null for λ₂ under a learned similarity exists, so build it by block permutation or from the authentic corpus.

**Random-matrix theory.** For n patches of dimension p of white noise with variance σ², the sample-covariance eigenvalues follow Marchenko–Pastur on [σ²(1−√γ)², σ²(1+√γ)²] with γ = p/n ([Marchenko & Pastur 1967](https://doi.org/10.1070/SM1967v001n04ABEH001994)). The largest eigenvalue has a Tracy–Widom null ([Johnstone 2001](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1009210544)), and a population spike becomes visible only above σ²(1+√γ) (the BBP transition, [Baik, Ben Arous & Péché 2005](https://doi.org/10.1214/009117905000000233)). That threshold is a hard detectability limit for any covariance-based regional cue. For forensics, the bulk gives a per-region noise estimate whose null is known, but only for i.i.d. white rows. Overlapping patches, demosaicing and JPEG colour the noise and move the edges. So the edges must be recalibrated by simulation at the actual (p, n), with noise matched to the image's power spectrum. The calibration principle that unifies this section is that **the null corpus must match the regime**. For re-decoded edits the authentic reference is not a camera photo but a *VAE-round-tripped* camera photo. You can produce one by passing the 1,300-image real pool through the SD and Qwen-Image VAEs you already run.

**Where a Lean proof adds value.** The finite, distribution-free guarantees are short proofs that a proof assistant certifies well. They are the NFA expectation bound, Bonferroni, super-uniformity of permutation and conformal p-values, exactness of *block* permutation under block exchangeability, BY under arbitrary dependence, and the 50% breakdown of the median. The most useful formal target is not any single lemma but the **composition**. A theorem would state that the full pipeline (cross-fitted reference, kernel chosen on a split, block-permutation p-values, NFA over a counted family) yields per-image E[false detections] ≤ ε, given block exchangeability of residuals. Composition is where informal arguments silently break, for example when a data-dependent bandwidth or a reference fitted on the test cells is reused. Asymptotic laws (KS limit, Wilks, Marchenko–Pastur, Tracy–Widom, random-field Euler-characteristic formulas) are far harder to formalise. The CLT itself had only standalone Lean formalisations as of 2025 ([Degenne, clt](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@RemyDegenne/clt)). These laws are better checked by Monte Carlo at the actual sample sizes. No proof can certify that real decoder output satisfies block exchangeability. **Only empirical false-alarm counts on authentic and round-tripped authentic images can do that.** Since Lean is not installed locally, write the composition theorem as a precise statement in the paper's appendix now. Formalise it later in a cloud Lean environment if a reviewer or venue values it. Every distributional assumption gets a Monte Carlo test below.

## Thirteen small tests, then one confirmatory experiment

The tests are ordered so that early, near-free diagnostics decide whether later ones are interpretable. Costs assume your cached 24×24 features, the 1,300-image real pool, and one 6 GB GPU. Per-image AUC is invariant to any per-image monotone transformation, so it says nothing about calibration. Every test therefore also reports false alarms on authentic images. "Authentic set A" means the real pool plus the TGIF2 originals. "Authentic set A-RT" means the same images after one SD-VAE or Qwen-Image-VAE round trip. None of the tests presupposes the outcome of your running region-consistency experiment. Tests 3, 5 and 7 consume its output file directly.

### Test 1: Do the current scorers produce false regions on authentic images at a known rate?

**Hypothesis:** the raw, regress and matched robust-z maps are not calibrated, and their image-level maximum on authentic images depends on content, not just on chance. **Procedure:** run the three scorers on every image of A and A-RT, for each stream. Record two image-level statistics: the maximum cell score M, and the largest connected component above the cell-level 99th percentile of A. **Statistic and null:** the empirical distribution of M over A defines a conformal image-level p-value, p = (1 + #{M_auth ≥ M})/(n + 1), exact under exchangeability of images. Apply it to the edited images. Repeat with A-RT as the calibration set. **Success:** detection rate on edited images at p ≤ 0.05 significantly exceeds 5% (one-sided binomial test) on at least the paste-back datasets. Calibrating on A-RT should lower false detections on regenerated sets relative to calibrating on A. **Stop:** if the A-calibrated rate on A-RT itself exceeds 20%, the camera-photo null is the wrong null for regenerated data, and later tests must use A-RT. **Cost:** CPU, under an hour once the pool features exist. Extracting the streams for 1,300 + 1,300 round-tripped images takes a few GPU-hours at most.

### Test 2: How dependent are cell residuals, and which resampling scheme keeps nominal size?

**Hypothesis:** residual descriptors are positively autocorrelated over at least two cells, so cell-level permutation is anti-conservative and block permutation is not. **Procedure:** on A, compute the regress residual for each stream. Estimate Moran's I at lags 1–4 and an empirical variogram, and fit a correlation length L in cells. Then, for 2,000 random rectangular "fake regions" per stream on authentic images, compute the energy-distance p-value of region vs rest three ways: cell permutation, block permutation with block side 2L, and a thinned grid at stride L with cell permutation. **Statistic and null:** under H0 these p-values should be uniform. Test with a KS test against U(0,1), and report the empirical rejection rate at α = 0.05 with a binomial confidence interval. **Success:** block or thinned schemes give a rate inside [0.03, 0.07], while cell permutation exceeds it. That result fixes L and the scheme for every later test. **Stop:** if no scheme achieves nominal size at any block side up to 6 cells, within-image resampling is unusable for that stream, and calibration must come from the authentic corpus alone (Test 1). **Cost:** CPU, about an hour.

### Test 3: Does content conditioning actually remove content, and does cross-fitting matter?

**Hypothesis:** the current regress and matched references leave measurable dependence between residual and content, and they absorb large edits through same-edit neighbours. **Procedure:** two negative controls. (a) Use the `pixels` stream itself as the descriptor: after conditioning on `pixels`, its per-image AUC must be near 0.5. (b) Use a random linear projection of the covariates plus independent noise as the descriptor: this must also give 0.5. Then compute distance correlation between residuals and covariates on A, with a block-permutation null. Finally, re-run regress and matched with cross-fitting: references drawn only from the other blocks of a 3×3 block partition, and a Huber regression. Compare per-image AUCs against the non-cross-fitted versions, paired over images, stratified by mask fraction. **Success:** controls within 0.50 ± 0.02. Residual-covariate distance correlation non-significant (p > 0.05) on at least 90% of authentic images. Cross-fitting raises AUC on images with mask fraction > 0.15 (paired Wilcoxon, p < 0.01). **Stop:** if the pixel control stays above 0.55 after conditioning, the normalisation is insufficient and any positive descriptor result is confounded. **Cost:** CPU, about an hour.

### Test 4: Were colour segments helping through the object confound?

**Hypothesis:** aggregating cell scores over colour/texture segments raises AUC on object-mask datasets (CocoGlide, AutoSplice, OpenSDI, SID-Set) but not on TGIF2 random masks, and it raises the *pixel-control* AUC just as much. **Procedure:** recompute your 2024 colour segmentation, or SLIC at matched segment counts, on each image. For each stream, assign each cell the mean score of its segment, of an area-matched square window centred on it, and of a random partition with the same segment-size distribution. **Statistic and null:** Δ = AUC_segment − AUC_window per image. Test the interaction (object-mask datasets vs TGIF2 random masks) with a paired bootstrap over images. Repeat for the pixel-control stream. **Success (confound confirmed):** the interaction CI excludes zero, and the pixel-control Δ is at least as large as the trace streams' Δ. That would turn the "segments were a mistake" verdict into a measured result and a paragraph for the paper. **Stop:** if segments help equally on random masks and do not help the pixel control, segmentation carried genuine variance reduction, and keeping it as a soft weight is justified. **Cost:** CPU, a few hours, mainly segmentation.

### Test 5: Are edits "too typical"? Location versus scale versus distributional statistics

**Hypothesis:** for several streams the edit differs from the rest in dispersion rather than in median, so location-only robust z is weaker and sign-unstable, while scale and distributional statistics are stable in sign. **Procedure:** per cell, compute three scores over a 3×3 neighbourhood of residuals: location, |median − image median|/MAD; signed scale, log(local MAD / image median of local MADs); and local energy distance to a cross-fitted reference. Report each one-sided in both directions and two-sided. **Statistic and null:** per-image AUC; the fraction of images with AUC < 0.5; and the across-dataset sign consistency of mean AUC − 0.5. Under no signal, the fraction below 0.5 is ½ (binomial test). **Success:** a signed scale score gives AUC − 0.5 of the same sign on at least 5 of 7 non-TGIF2 datasets, and the inverted-image fraction falls compared with location. That would convert the below-chance one-class result into a principled "low-dispersion" score. **Stop:** if all three behave alike, the inversion is not a dispersion effect, and only two-sided distributional scores are kept. **Cost:** CPU, minutes; it reuses the region-consistency row output.

### Test 6: Does the TGIF2 matched design separate decoder traces from synthesis traces?

**Hypothesis:** on the same source images and the same random masks, low-level streams are above chance on spliced (SP) and at chance on fully regenerated (FR) subsets, while drift and pairwise descriptors are above chance on both. **Procedure:** for every stream and scorer, compute per-image AUC on matched FLUX.1-dev FR/SP and SD2 FR/SP pairs, plus SDXL FR. **Statistic and null:** paired difference AUC_SP − AUC_FR per source image, with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test. For each subset separately, a one-sample test of AUC − 0.5. **Success:** low-level streams show SP > FR (p < 0.01) with FR not different from 0.5. Any stream with FR AUC ≥ 0.60 and a CI above 0.55 becomes a candidate synthesis-trace descriptor. **Stop:** if every stream is at chance on FR, self-consistency on current descriptors cannot address FR, and Tests 10, 12 and 13 become decisive. **Cost:** cached features, minutes.

### Test 7: Where does the majority-authentic assumption break, and does a symmetric test recover detection?

**Hypothesis:** per-image AUC of median-referenced scorers declines and inverts as the edited fraction approaches 0.5, while a symmetric two-community statistic keeps detection power. **Procedure:** using the `mask_frac` already logged per row, bin images by edited fraction (0–0.05, 0.05–0.15, 0.15–0.3, 0.3–0.5, > 0.5). For each image also compute a symmetric statistic: 2-means on cell residuals with energy distance between the two clusters, calibrated against the same statistic on A. **Statistic and null:** logistic regression of 1{AUC < 0.5} on edited fraction. For the symmetric statistic, the conformal image-level p-value from A. **Success:** a significant positive slope for the median-referenced scorers, and symmetric detection power that stays above 50% in the 0.3–0.5 and > 0.5 bins. **Stop:** none; this test measures a boundary for reporting. **Cost:** CPU, minutes.

### Test 8: Does detection power follow the sample-size theory?

**Hypothesis:** the power to detect a controlled edit follows z ≈ δ/√(2/N_eff), with N_eff from Test 2, so the minimal detectable size can be predicted for each descriptor. **Procedure:** semi-synthetic edits on authentic images. Insert square regions of side 32, 64, 96, 128 and 192 px that are (a) the same region after one SD-VAE round trip (paste-back of re-decoded content), (b) the region with Gaussian noise σ scaled by 0.9 or 1.1, and (c) the region with a 0.5-px Gaussian blur. Re-extract the affected cell features. **Statistic and null:** detection at conformal image p ≤ 0.05 (calibrated on A), and per-image AUC. Fit the empirical power curve and compare it with the predicted curve. **Success:** for noise-type descriptors, the observed size at 80% power agrees with prediction within a factor of 2 in area. That validates the power arithmetic used to choose grid size. **Stop:** if the observed power is far below prediction, the real effective N is smaller than Test 2 estimates, which points to unmodelled non-stationarity. **Cost:** feature re-extraction for about 300 images × 15 conditions, a few GPU-hours on SD-VAE in fp16, otherwise CPU.

### Test 9: Is an a-contrario region-growing detector calibrated on authentic images?

**Hypothesis:** a Noisesniffer-style NFA on stratified residual ranks is calibrated (E[detections] ≤ ε) on a thinned grid but anti-conservative on the full grid. **Procedure:** within strata (intensity tercile × texture tercile from `pixels`), convert each cell's residual to a rank. Mark cells in the top m fraction (two-sided: both tails) as "votes". Grow 4-connected polyominoes greedily from vote seeds. Compute NFA = N_polyominoes(size) × binomial tail with p = m, counting polyominoes as Noisesniffer does. Run on the full 24×24 grid and on the stride-L thinned grid from Test 2. **Statistic and null:** mean detections per authentic image with NFA ≤ 1, using a Poisson upper 95% bound. **Success:** the thinned grid gives a mean ≤ 1 on both A and A-RT. Detection power on edited images then follows, together with pixel-level F1 at this principled threshold, compared with F1 at 0.5 and at the oracle threshold. **Stop:** if the full grid already gives a mean ≤ 1, dependence is weaker than feared; if even the thinned grid fails, the binomial tail is invalid and the tails must be estimated from the authentic corpus. **Cost:** CPU, a few hours.

### Test 10: Does perturbation drift beat raw tokens inside the same framework, and at which depth?

**Hypothesis:** TRAIL-style drift is less content-driven than raw DINOv2 tokens, so self-consistency on drift beats raw-token self-consistency and the pixel control, especially on TGIF2 random masks. The best depth for drift is later than layer 6. **Procedure:** for 150 images per dataset, encode the full canvas with DINOv2 (ViT-B or ViT-L, fp16) on x and on T(x) = x − 0.2·HF₂(x) (the Haar construction of [TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18968v1)). Record per-patch cosine drift at layers {6, 12, 18, last}, pooled to your 24×24 grid. Score raw drift and drift passed through the cross-fitted regress scorer. **Statistic and null:** per-image AUC, compared paired with the raw-token matched scorer and with the pixel control. As a null, apply the same pipeline to authentic images with a random rectangle as the "mask", which should give 0.5. **Success:** on TGIF2 random-mask subsets and on at least 4 of the other 7 datasets, drift AUC ≥ pixel control + 0.05, with a paired p < 0.01 against raw tokens. Any GPT-Image-2 or Nano-Banana-2 AUC above 0.60 is a headline result. **Stop:** if drift fails to beat the pixel control on random masks, its CocoGlide performance is object-driven, and drift drops in rank. **Cost:** two forward passes per image. About 1,000 images is roughly an hour on 6 GB for ViT-B/L in fp16 (estimate). The subband-resolved extension (about 24 DT-CWT perturbations) multiplies the cost by roughly 12; run it only if the single-Haar version passes.

### Test 11: Is a Marchenko–Pastur noise estimator a better NLF descriptor than MAD on paste-back data?

**Hypothesis:** in textured regions, an MP-bulk noise estimate has lower bias than the wavelet MAD estimate, which raises SP localization without helping FR. **Procedure:** first, a Monte Carlo validation. Add Gaussian noise of known σ ∈ {1, 2, 4} to 200 low-noise real-pool images. For 3×3-cell windows (≈130 px, n ≈ 340 non-overlapping 7×7 high-pass patches, γ ≈ 0.14), estimate σ² by MAD, by Liu's weak-texture PCA, and by fitting MP to the lower bulk after iterative spike removal. Compute relative RMSE by texture tercile, and compare the empirical bulk edge with the theoretical one at this (p, n). Second, on TGIF2 SP/FR, compute an intensity-binned NLF-residual robust z for each estimator. **Statistic and null:** relative RMSE with bootstrap CIs. For the edge, the empirical exceedance rate of the largest eigenvalue over the TW-predicted 95% quantile, under pure noise, should be 5%. Localization is assessed by per-image AUC, paired across estimators. **Success:** the MP estimator cuts RMSE by ≥ 30% in the top texture tercile and improves SP AUC by ≥ 0.03 over MAD. **Stop:** if the edge is miscalibrated by more than a factor of 2 at this (p, n), or the SP gain is < 0.02, drop RMT as a descriptor and keep MAD. **Cost:** CPU, about half a day.

### Test 12: Does round-trip idempotence give a regime-specific cue with a flat null?

**Hypothesis:** on FR images the double-round-trip convergence ratio differs between re-decoded and de-novo cells, and on A-RT images it is spatially flat. **Procedure:** from your rt2 extraction (SD and Qwen-Image VAEs), compute per cell r = ‖y₂ − y₁‖/‖y₁ − x‖ in DT-CWT subbands, where y₁ = D(E(x)) and y₂ = D(E(y₁)). Condition on content with the cross-fitted regress scorer. Score it two-sided. **Statistic and null:** per-image AUC on TGIF2 FR (matched VAE family: FLUX for FLUX subsets if available, otherwise report the mismatch), paired against single-pass VAE-rec. Image-level false-alarm rate on A-RT using the conformal procedure of Test 1. An INP-X-style control: restore the original pixels outside the mask, after which the map should invert or vanish in the background. **Success:** FR AUC ≥ 0.62 with a CI above 0.55, a false-alarm rate on A-RT within nominal, and the control behaving as predicted. **Stop:** FR AUC < 0.58 for both VAE families, which closes the passive FR route for low-level operators. **Cost:** already being extracted; analysis on CPU.

### Test 13: Does a learned pairwise comparator beat hand distances, with a calibrated spectral test?

**Hypothesis:** a small same-source head trained on cached features from real images (plus VAE round trips) separates edited from authentic cells better than the matched scorer on the same features, and the Fiedler eigenvalue λ₂ of its affinity graph detects edits at a calibrated rate. **Procedure:** on cached DT-CWT + LiftNet + SRM cell features (and DINOv2-L6 separately), train a two-layer MLP Siamese head with a Barlow-Twins or contrastive loss. Positives are two content-distant cells of the same real image, and two cells of the same A-RT image. Negatives are cells from different images, and a real cell versus its round-tripped counterpart. No edited data is used. On test images, build the cell affinity matrix, compute λ₂ of the normalised Laplacian, and sweep-cut the Fiedler vector into a two-region map. **Statistic and null:** the λ₂ null comes from A and from A-RT (conformal image-level p-value). Per-image AUC of the Fiedler map, paired against the matched scorer on the same features. **Success:** ≥ +0.03 AUC over matched on at least 4 datasets, *in cross-dataset evaluation* (the head is trained on the real pool only, so every dataset is out-of-distribution), with λ₂ false alarms within nominal on A-RT. **Stop:** no gain over matched, meaning a learned comparator adds nothing on 43-px cached cells and would need native-resolution residual crops (a larger project). **Cost:** minutes to an hour on GPU, entirely on cached features.

### From small tests to one confirmatory experiment

The tests form a gate. Tests 1–3 decide whether any number is interpretable: the null corpus (A or A-RT), the block size L, and whether conditioning removes content. Tests 4–7 fix the design: grid units, not segments; a two-sided distributional statistic; regime-specific reporting; symmetric detection for large edits. Tests 8–13 choose descriptors, and each has a stop rule, so the descriptor set shrinks before anything expensive is built. Only descriptors that pass their own test enter the confirmatory study, and the decision rule should be written down before any confirmatory data are touched.

The confirmatory experiment should be **one preregistered pipeline evaluated on held-out data**. The pipeline has five steps. (1) Compute the surviving descriptors at a cell size chosen from Test 8, preferably finer than 24×24 and aligned to the latent lattice. (2) Fit a cross-fitted robust conditional reference on content strata. (3) Turn residuals into two-sided stratified ranks. (4) Run a polyomino NFA with tails validated on a thinned grid. (5) Calibrate against a regime-matched authentic corpus (A for paste-back, A-RT for regenerated). Hold out entire datasets: GPT-Image-2 and Nano-Banana-2, ideally re-labelled with pixel-difference maps as PIXAR recommends ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)), plus the TGIF2 FLUX subsets. The co-primary endpoints are (i) the empirical per-image false-detection rate on held-out authentic images, which must stay ≤ ε, and (ii) paired per-image AUC against two baselines: TRAIL drift alone, and your supervised DINOv2-L6 probe. Secondary endpoints are pixel F1 at the NFA threshold (no oracle), detection power by edited-fraction bin, and an object-bias index defined as the AUC gap between random and semantic masks. For sample size, if the per-image AUC difference between methods has a standard deviation of about 0.15, which Tests 6 and 10 will estimate, then 400 images per held-out set give a standard error of about 0.0075 and roughly 80% power to detect a 0.02 paired difference at α = 0.05. All of this fits in a few GPU-days on 6 GB, because the expensive parts are already cached. The result is publishable either way. A calibrated, label-free localizer that matches a supervised probe on held-out editors is a strong positive. A demonstration that *no* calibrated self-consistency test rejects the null on fully regenerated edits, while the same pipeline succeeds on matched spliced pairs, gives a quantitative boundary the field has so far only asserted.

## Conclusion

The question "should localization be region-wise self-consistency?" has a sharper form: **which conditional law is broken, and can a calibrated test see it inside one image?** For paste-back edits, almost any well-normalised low-level trace breaks it, and the gain from statistical soundness is mainly calibration (thresholds that mean something, F1 without an oracle). For whole-frame regeneration, decoder statistics obey the null by construction. Only descriptors that respond to *synthesis*, such as context-dependent perturbation drift, round-trip convergence and learned decoding-origin similarity, can reject it. The most useful reframing to come out of your own data is that edited content is often *too typical* rather than anomalous. Two-sided, distributional and scale-aware statistics therefore matter as much as the choice of descriptor, and the apparent sign instability of surprisal and one-class scores is likely an artefact of one-sided tests.

Methodologically, the forensic literature has used a-contrario counting for years but has almost never checked its nulls on large authentic corpora. It has also not borrowed the dependence-aware resampling that fMRI learned the hard way. Your matched TGIF2 FR/SP design, the real-only pool, and a round-tripped version of that pool are exactly the ingredients for doing so. That means a localizer whose false-alarm rate is *measured* on regime-matched authentic images, not assumed. A Lean proof of the pipeline's composed error guarantee would make the claim airtight on paper. It is worth doing only once the Monte Carlo checks show that the assumptions it rests on actually hold for decoder output.
