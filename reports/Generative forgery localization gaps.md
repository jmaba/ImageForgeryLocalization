# Regenerated edits break localizers: six ways forward

Current forgery localizers handle spliced generative edits well, where the edit is pasted back into otherwise camera-original pixels. They have not solved fully regenerated (FR) edits, instruction edits or MLLM edits, where every output pixel passes through a decoder. On closed-source MLLM editors the best verified zero-shot result is below 0.25 IoU: **Nano-Banana held out scores 18.95 IoU for ICL-Net and 0.216 for FLAME** ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386); [FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)). Off-the-shelf TruFor drops from **0.75 F1 on spliced inpainting to 0.14 on FR inpainting** ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). The underlying problem is the evidence, not the architecture. Camera traces, the reference that classical localizers rely on, are erased by the VAE round-trip. Most 2025–2026 decoders (SAM, CLIP, MLLMs) are semantic, so they learn "where the named object is" instead of "where pixels were synthesized". Benchmarks whose masks come from SAM or object segmentation reward that shortcut. The literature also lacks four things: any localizer trained only on real images and evaluated on generative edits, any passive method that separates "VAE-reconstructed" from "generated de novo" inside an FR image, any calibrated map with guarantees, and any physics-based localizer tested on modern editors. Below I propose six approaches aimed at these gaps, each with a falsifiable hypothesis and a protocol built around INP-X background restoration, TGIF2 random masks and leave-one-editor-out splits. Four of them, including the dense real-only predictive pipeline that extends your frozen-latent plan, can be developed and evaluated on a single 6 GB GPU. Two need larger compute for some ablations.

## Generative pipelines erased the pristine reference localizers depend on

The defining fact of the new threat model is that, in most deployed pipelines, the region outside the edit is no longer authentic. In latent inpainting, the latent is composited at every denoising step, but the final image is a full VAE decode of the whole canvas. Pixels outside the mask are therefore reconstructions, not camera pixels ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02178)). The diffusers documentation states that inpaint checkpoints "are more likely to change your unmasked area". Bit-exact preservation requires an explicit `apply_overlay()` step, which the docs say produces "more unnatural transitions" ([diffusers inpainting guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/using-diffusers/inpaint)). Paste-back (SP) occurs only when a pipeline adds it on purpose. BrushNet does so with a Gaussian-blurred mask whose kernel is 21 px ([BrushNet app](https://huggingface.co/spaces/TencentARC/BrushNet/blame/main/app.py)), and Photoshop/Firefly does so with an automatic gradient border ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)). Instruction editors such as FLUX.1 Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, Step1X-Edit, BAGEL, GPT-Image and Nano-Banana generate the entire output, and some crop or shift it. PromptForge-350k had to apply ORB+RANSAC affine alignment before it could derive any mask, because editors "internally apply pixel cropping" ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29386)). Community reports describe roughly 8 px shifts per Qwen-Image-Edit pass caused by silent rescaling. That evidence is non-peer-reviewed ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/qwen-image-edit-pixel-perfect-referencelatent)).

A localizer therefore has to distinguish at least four pixel classes, not two. The first is authentic camera pixels, found in SP backgrounds. The second is VAE-reconstructed authentic content, found in FR backgrounds. The third is de-novo generated content. The fourth is a feathered blend band where the output is α·generated + (1−α)·original. **DinoLizer** is the only method found that formalizes even three of these classes (Real / VAE / Inpainted). Its authors argue that labelling FR backgrounds "real" creates "conflicting supervisory signals". With three classes on a DINOv2 + LoRA backbone it reaches about 0.55 IoU and is at least 20 F1 points ahead of other detectors ([DinoLizer](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20722)). Most methods and benchmarks still use binary labels.

The trace that best survives this setting is a low-pass signature. The VAE bottleneck attenuates high-frequency sensor noise, and generated content has lower local energy than optical-imaging noise. INP-X turned this into an experiment. It restored the original pixels outside the mask while keeping the generated region, and commercial and academic detectors collapsed: **Hive 91.4% → 54.8%, Sightengine 92.6% → 55.0%, Corvi2023 94.2% → 55.4%** ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). Image-level detectors were therefore reading the global VAE trace, not the edit. The same study found that image-level VAE loss correlates with high-frequency content at r = 0.941. Soft alpha blending of seams did not restore detection, so detectors were not using seams either ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). FLAME exploits the local version of this trace with a hollow-3×3 "local absolute difference" energy map. It derives an interior energy gap and a boundary spike from a spectral-energy inequality ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02178)). The same property makes the cue fragile. Under Gaussian noise of variance 5, FLAME's IoU falls to 0.270, and under JPEG-75 it falls to 0.329 ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02178)). An adversary can re-inject sensor-like noise.

Conversions between SP and FR are cheap and defeat each detector family in turn. A whole-image VAE round-trip turns SP into FR, and classical localizers fall from **0.64–0.89 F1 to at most about 0.14** unless retrained ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)). Background restoration turns FR into SP and defeats image-level detectors ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). Generative super-resolution is a third laundering route. One reading of TGIF2's Real-ESRGAN ×2 experiment has CAT-Net falling from 0.87 to 0.06 F1 and TruFor from 0.78 to 0.21, against small losses under bicubic upscaling ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613)). TGIF2's own text and tables disagree on which column is which, however, so the exact magnitude needs checking in the PDF ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613v1)). Whichever reading is correct, generative post-processing is the one robustness axis that standard JPEG/blur augmentation does not cover.

## Six method families converge on semantic encoders, and semantic encoders converge on object bias

The 2023–2026 literature falls into six families. The first is dual-stream RGB+noise localizers from classical IML: TruFor, PIM, IML-ViT, SparseViT, Mesorch and SAFIRE. The second is foundation-model adapters on SAM3, DINOv2/v3, CLIP or MAE: MaskCLIP, FLAME, GAP-SAM, ForenScope and FASA. The third uses a diffusion model as the mask decoder: InpDiffusion, SDiFL, CBDiff and DiffIML. The fourth is MLLM localizers: FakeShield, SIDA, So-Fake-R1, ForensicsTok and DeFakerOne. The fifth uses reconstruction residuals: ReGFLoW and GAP-SAM's VAE token. The sixth learns AIGC-specific traces: DiffusionPrint, NFA-ViT, ICL-Net and PANet ([TruFor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957); [GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929); [DiffIML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16879); [SIDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04292); [ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882); [DiffusionPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)). The 2026 leaders are hybrids. Each injects a hand-designed generative-trace prior into a frozen foundation segmenter: LAD energy in FLAME, a VAE-reconstruction token in GAP-SAM, local monogenic phase in PANet, and DiffusionPrint features in place of Noiseprint++ in TruFor. That convergence suggests a generation-pipeline prior matters more than the decoder. Diffusion-as-decoder and MLLM families mostly change how the mask is produced, not what evidence it rests on, so their transfer to new editors depends on a semantic encoder.

Three patterns stand out. First, in-domain instruction-edit localization is largely ordinary semantic segmentation. On LocateEdit-Bench, a vanilla SegFormer reaches 87.0 F1 and outperforms Mesorch, PIM-Net and IML-ViT ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577)). The forensic contribution only becomes visible across editors. Second, few-shot adaptation is very effective: 500 samples from a single new editor nearly double FLAME's Nano-Banana IoU, from 0.216 to 0.391 ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)). Third, MLLM localizers are not ahead on modern edits. SIDA's OOD IoU is below TruFor's ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)). DeFakerOne trains on 12.5M samples, yet it is *worse* than Mesorch on CASIAv1 and Columbia, and its GPT-Image-2 result is detection on only 71 samples, with no localization ([DeFakerOne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14091)). No paper reports pixel-level localization on GPT-Image(-1/-2), Seedream or SeedEdit, apart from PIXAR's GPT-image-1.5 test split.

Failure modes recur across independent groups. The most consequential is **object bias**, which GAP-SAM calls "boundary adhesion": fine-tuned segmenters snap their predictions to object contours rather than to manipulation boundaries ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)). TGIF2 measured it directly. TruFor fine-tuned on FR semantic masks scores **0.50 F1 on semantic masks but 0.23 on random rectangles** ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613)). TRAIL's training-free DINO drift shows the same confound. Its advantage over an aligned-real control is +0.164 AUROC on TGIF2 random masks but only +0.068 on semantic masks, so the frozen features respond to objects before any edit has been made ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). Background edits form a second weak spot. PANet reaches 0.786 IoU on Impostor but only 0.327 on BR-Gen ([PANet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04545)), and TruFor reaches .865 F1 on Columbia but .085 on BR-Gen ([GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)). A third weak spot is removal and small edits. A splicing-trained SparseViT scores about 0.92 on splicing and about 0.44 on removal, and its errors are dominated by false negatives ([GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01778)). FLAME lists very small edits, object removal and false positives on smooth authentic regions among its limitations ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)). The fourth is ground truth that does not match what the editor changed. ReGFLoW argues that VAE mapping and blending alter pixels outside the mask, which are then mislabeled authentic ([ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)), and TGIF2 shows a random-box FR edit that "made notable changes to the sign" outside the box ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613)).

## Benchmarks reward object bias and hide false positives

Benchmarks have gone through three generations. The first was masked-inpainting sets with masks known by construction: CocoGlide, AutoSplice, TGIF/TGIF2, COCO-Inpaint, BR-Gen, OpenSDID and SID-Set. The second was instruction-edit sets whose masks are inferred after editing: DEAL-300K, LocateEdit-Bench, PIXAR, PromptForge-350k and EditStream. The third is diagnostic sets: DiffSeg30k (up to three sequential edits, with per-pixel editor-ID labels) and GIFTBench (4 sources × 3 targets × 3 operations × 3 compositions) ([DiffSeg30k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19111); [GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)). For instruction editors, four incompatible ground-truth paradigms coexist. LocateEdit-Bench has ChatGPT name the edited object and SAM3 segment it, with confidence above 0.5 and no pixel-difference check ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577)). PIXAR thresholds |I_orig − I_gen| at τ = 0.05 after a homography alignment, and falls back to the unaligned image when alignment fails ([PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193)). PromptForge combines keypoint alignment with DINOv3 similarity. A 200-sample volunteer check of its masks found 8 significant errors, including failures on pure colour edits ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29386)). DEAL-300K uses active-learning change detection ([DEAL-300K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23377)). No paper validates its masks against human annotation quantitatively.

These choices have consequences. A SAM object mask rewards a model for segmenting the object named in the prompt, and that is exactly the shortcut TGIF2's random masks expose. A pixel-difference mask on an FR editor labels global colour drift as "edited" whenever the drift exceeds τ. A failed alignment produces an almost full-image mask without any warning. A model trained under one paradigm is therefore penalized under another for reasons of label definition, not detection. No paper has measured this, so it is an open and cheap contribution.

Metric practice makes the problem worse. The de-facto standard is per-image pixel F1 at a fixed 0.5 threshold. GIFTBench **excludes real images from localization metrics** ([GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)), so a model that paints every image red is never penalized for doing so. Pixel AUC is inflated by the dominant authentic class: on LocateEdit, PSCC-Net reaches 95.1 AUC but only 75.5 F1 and 61.9 mIoU ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577)). Thresholding alone accounts for a large share of error, as TRAIL's Dice rises from .619 at a fixed threshold to .790 at a per-image oracle threshold ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). None of the benchmarks reviewed reports calibration, area-stratified scores or false-positive area on pristine images. Format bias, where real images are JPEG and fakes PNG, is documented for detection ([Fake or JPEG?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)) but has not been audited for localization sets. COCO supplies the source images for CocoGlide, TGIF/TGIF2, COCO-Inpaint, PIXAR, DiffSeg30k and MagicBrush, yet no paper checks whether image IDs overlap across these sets. Release status is also poor. LocateEdit-Bench will be open-sourced "upon acceptance", GIFTBench offers only a showcase page, and the SID-Set test split is withheld ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577); [SID-Set](https://huggingface.co/datasets/saberzl/SID_Set)). An undocumented Hugging Face bundle does contain GPT-Image-2 and NanoBanana2 test folders, but it has no card, no licence and no description of how its masks were made ([AIGC-Loc-Testsets](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/youqiwong/AIGC-Loc-Testsets/tree/main)).

## Frozen latents already encode edits; pooling and semantics hide them

Your pipeline of frozen self-supervised latents with a simple discriminator has strong image-level support, and it also has a specific, documented blind spot for localization. A linear probe on frozen DINOv3 reaches **94.0% average accuracy on in-the-wild sets**, about 10 points above DDA. LoRA *hurts* generalization: PE with r = 8 drops from 95.9% to 63.5% on Chameleon ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)). The same paper finds these probes "largely blind" to pure VAE reconstruction and reaching only about 50–60% on BR-Gen local edits. Its authors attribute this to global pooling washing out local traces ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)). The capability also depends on pretraining data: DINOv3 pretrained only on satellite images detects 12.1% of Chameleon fakes, against 91.4% for the web-pretrained model ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)). Part of what frozen VFMs "know" is therefore a memory of the web's synthetic distribution. That is a risk for future editors. Cozzolino et al. find that foundation-model detectors respond to low-to-mid-frequency, non-semantic discrepancies, and that regenerated images have **lower latent variance and lower effective dimensionality** ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). That second finding is a usable per-patch statistic.

The patch tokens contain the spatial signal that pooling discards. TRAIL needs no training: it measures how much frozen DINOv3-7B tokens drift under a Haar high-frequency attenuation, and reaches 0.903 patch AUROC on CocoGlide. The best block always lies at normalized depth 0.80–0.94. Global context matters (0.903 against 0.735 for independently encoded crops), and the edit-specific increment grows with model size, from 0.089 at ViT-S to 0.206 at 7B ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). Under supervision, DINOv3 with LoRA and a 9.1M-parameter decoder improves pixel-F1 by 17.0 points under the CAT-Net protocol ([DINOv3-IML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16083)). Real-only SSL has also matured at the image level. Forensic Twins applies redundancy reduction to pixel-disjoint crops of a residual stream and, with a GMM on real embeddings, reaches **97.99% AUC across 27 unseen generators** ([Forensic Twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31514)). Forensic Self-Descriptions and EXIF-supervised SSL reach the same conclusion by other routes ([Self-Descriptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21003); [camera-metadata SSL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05651)). No paper turns any of these real-only encoders into a localization map for generative edits. That missing step is the opening that proposal 1 below targets.

## A shared protocol every proposal must pass

Every proposal below should be judged against one protocol, because reviewers will otherwise attribute any gain to object bias or to the global VAE trace. **Training data** should be a single, documented source. Natural choices are TGIF2 SD2 SP+FR with semantic and random masks, OpenSDID SD1.5, or, for real-only methods, the authentic images alone. **Test sets** should include TGIF2 FLUX subsets (SP/FR × semantic/random), OpenSDID SD2.1/SDXL/SD3/FLUX, CocoGlide, AutoSplice, BR-Gen, DiffSeg30k, PIXAR's per-editor splits (Qwen-Image, FLUX.2, Gemini 2.5/3, GPT-image-1.5, Seedream 4.5), and GIFTBench or LocateEdit if they have been released ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613); [PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193)). The undocumented GPT-Image-2 and NanoBanana2 folders should be used only with an explicit caveat. **Leave-one-editor-out** should be reported over the instruction editors, with Nano-Banana and BAGEL as the critical held-out cases ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)).

Five controls are mandatory. The first is an INP-X restoration control: restore the original pixels outside the mask and report the change in performance, to prove the method localizes the edit rather than the global decode ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). The second is a TGIF2-style **random-rectangle mask split** with an empty prompt, reported next to semantic masks. The gap between the two is an object-bias index ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). The third is an aligned-real control: score the unedited source inside the same mask, following TRAIL. The fourth is a set of VAE-round-trip-only negatives, re-decoded images with no edit, together with pristine images, for which false-positive *area* is reported. The fifth is format and resolution normalization, re-encoding every image identically. **Metrics** should be F1/IoU at a threshold chosen on a validation set from a *different* generator, plus oracle-threshold F1 as an upper bound, area-stratified scores (<1%, 1–10%, >10%), a boundary-adhesion score, and robustness to JPEG, resize, Gaussian noise (the FLAME failure case), Real-ESRGAN and a second editor pass. One boundary-adhesion score is the fraction of predicted boundary within *k* px of a SAM object contour but more than *k* px from the true mask boundary; no published metric exists ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)). **Baselines** should cover each family: TruFor, Mesorch, SparseViT, SAFIRE, MaskCLIP, DinoLizer, DINOv3-IML, TRAIL, FLAME (code released), DiffusionPrint-in-TruFor (code released), ReGFLoW and SIDA/FakeShield. GAP-SAM, ICL-Net and PANet code was not confirmed released, so they may have to be cited by their reported numbers.

## Six proposals that target the evidence, not the decoder

### 1. Dense real-only predictive forensics: localization as unpredictability under the camera model

**Hypothesis.** A dense, masked-prediction (JEPA-style) model trained only on real photographs, operating on a forensic residual stream, learns the joint statistics of acquisition traces across neighbouring patches. Generated regions and VAE-reconstructed regions both violate those statistics, in different ways. The per-patch prediction error is therefore a localization map that needs no edited training data, and so it has no editor-specific overfitting to lose under leave-one-editor-out.

**Method.** Freeze a residual extractor such as Noiseprint++ or a learned high-pass bank, and patchify its output at 16–32 px. Train a small context encoder and predictor to predict the embeddings of masked target patches from visible context patches. Use Forensic Twins' recipe of pixel-disjoint targets and no micro-statistic-destroying augmentations, so that the shared signal cannot be content ([Forensic Twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31514)). The anomaly score per patch is the prediction error, normalized by a per-image robust z-score. Concatenate the frozen DINOv3 intermediate tokens as a second, semantic stream for context, but never as a prediction target, so the scorer cannot reduce to semantic novelty. Add **per-image test-time fitting**: run a few gradient steps of the same real-only pretext loss on the suspect image, reweighting patches by their current confidence. The edited region is a minority distribution and should remain unpredictable after fitting, while benign content variation is absorbed. Finally, add a "simple discriminator" head that is trained only on real images and *self-made* VAE round-trips: two-class one-class density, real vs. reconstructed, with no edited images. This handles FR backgrounds explicitly, which is the conflicting-supervision problem DinoLizer identifies ([DinoLizer](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20722)).

**Why it should beat current methods.** Supervised localizers lose 20–40 IoU points on unseen editors because they learn editor-specific traces ([PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)). A real-only model has nothing editor-specific to learn. Its out-of-distribution test is simply "anything not camera-like", which is the regime in which Forensic Twins reaches 98% image-level AUC over 27 generators. The disjoint-target design attacks object bias at its source, because a predictor that cannot see the target's content cannot use semantics to explain it away.

**Closest prior work and novelty.** The closest works are EXIF-as-language and Huh et al. (real-only self-consistency, but for splicing) ([EXIF-as-language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04647)), Forensic Twins (real-only, image-level only), ZED (per-pixel surprisal, image-level evaluation) ([ZED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)), TRAIL (training-free but a semantic VFM drift, with a 7B model) and ForgeryTTT (per-image TTT, supervised pretext, classic benchmarks) ([ForgeryTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04032)). Three things here are new: a *dense predictive* objective on a forensic residual stream, trained on real images only, and evaluated zero-shot on diffusion and instruction-edit localization with FR handled explicitly. The novelty check found no published combination of these ([novelty searches summarized in the TRAIL and Forensic Twins positioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)).

**Experimental protocol.** Use the shared protocol above, plus three additional experiments. The first is an ablation of the prediction target: residual embedding vs. DINO token vs. raw pixel. The second is a test-time fitting curve of IoU against step count. The third is a stratified report across SP and FR. **Predicted signature:** strong on SP and TGIF2 random masks, weaker on FR unless the round-trip head is used. Report a small drop under the INP-X control, because the method does not depend on the global decode.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** The central risk is FR. When every pixel is decoded, the residual stream is globally non-camera, so the whole image may look anomalous. That is the very argument DiffusionPrint makes ([DiffusionPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)). The round-trip head is the mitigation, but it must be shown to work. A second risk is false positives from edges, textures and saturated regions. Reviewers will ask for TRAIL and a GMM on Forensic Twins patch features as baselines, and for evidence that the predictor is not a disguised high-frequency energy detector, which would make it as fragile to noise as FLAME. Include a "noise-matched laundering" attack in which sensor-like noise is added to the edit.

**Feasibility.** **6 GB is sufficient.** The residual extractor and a ViT-S/B predictor train in mixed precision at batch 16–32 on 256² crops. DINOv3 ViT-B/L tokens can be pre-extracted and cached. Test-time fitting of a small predictor takes seconds per image. Pretraining on 100–300k real images is a few GPU-days.

### 2. Round-trip versus de-novo: passive localization inside fully regenerated images

**Hypothesis.** Inside an FR image, background pixels are a VAE encode-decode of camera content, while edited pixels were sampled by the denoiser and then decoded. Both lie close to the decoder manifold, but they reached it along different paths. De-novo content has lower latent variance and lower local effective dimensionality ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). Reconstructed camera content keeps attenuated but structured high-frequency residue ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00192v1)). Repeated round-trips through a VAE should therefore converge at different rates on the two classes, and the rate is measurable per patch without the original image. This is an analogue of double-JPEG quantization idempotence.

**Method.** For a bank of open VAEs (SD1/2 4-channel, SDXL, SD3/FLUX 16-channel), compute x₀ = x, x₁ = D(E(x₀)) and x₂ = D(E(x₁)). Per patch, extract four feature groups. The first is the error ratio ‖x₂ − x₁‖ / ‖x₁ − x₀‖ across frequency bands. The second is latent statistics: per-patch latent variance, local PCA effective rank, and the latent-norm deviation from the encoder's real-image prior. The third is LAD energy before and after the round-trip. The fourth is the cosine drift of frozen DINOv3 intermediate tokens between x₀ and x₁, a TRAIL-style drift that uses a *generative* perturbation in place of a Haar perturbation. Feed these maps, together with frozen DINOv3 tokens, into a light discriminator that outputs the three classes Real / Reconstructed / Generated. Training data is synthetic and editor-free. Take real images, make FR backgrounds by VAE round-trip, and make "generated" patches by SDEdit or inpainting with *open* models under **random masks**, so that no object prior is learned. Because the VAE bank includes several decoders, the method can also report which decoder best explains the image, giving decoder attribution as a by-product.

**Why it should beat current methods.** No current passive method separates the two FR classes. Classical localizers have no camera reference, and image-level detectors respond to the global decode. GAP-SAM compresses the reconstruction into one *global* token, and ReGFLoW uses a single-step residual as a feature ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929); [ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)). The one FR success reported, APT's 0.92 IoU, is proactive and requires embedding a watermark before distribution ([APT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30656)). A second-order, convergence-based statistic adds information that single-pass reconstruction error lacks, because a single pass sees both classes as already on the manifold.

**Closest prior work and novelty.** AEROBLADE (training-free, image-level reconstruction error) ([AEROBLADE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17879)), DinoLizer's three classes (supervised, with no reconstruction physics), ReGFLoW and GAP-SAM are the nearest. The novelty is the iterated round-trip convergence statistic, the per-patch latent-dimensionality map, and an explicit three-class FR formulation trained without any editor outputs. The novelty check found no "generation-count" or double-round-trip localizer.

**Experimental protocol.** The headline numbers are TGIF2 FR (SD2, SDXL, FLUX dev/schnell/Fill) under both mask types. Then add OpenSDID, DiffSeg30k and PIXAR per editor, with leave-one-*VAE*-out (for example, train with SD-VAE features only and test on FLUX). Run the INP-X control in both directions: restore the background, which should change the three-class prediction from "Reconstructed" to "Real" while keeping "Generated" fixed. Report a laundering matrix covering an extra round-trip through a *different* VAE, Real-ESRGAN, and SDEdit at strength 0.1–0.3. No paper has attacked generative-inpainting localizers with cross-VAE laundering, so this matrix is a contribution in itself.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** Closed MLLM editors may re-synthesize texture everywhere, through a VQ tokenizer or a diffusion decoder with unknown internals. In that case the background is no longer a "round-trip of camera content" and the distinction disappears. Treat this as a falsifiable outcome and report it per editor, because it is publishable either way: it identifies which editors leave a passive FR signal. A deliberate whole-image round-trip after editing makes both regions "Reconstructed" plus extra. The second-order ratio may survive this, but it has to be shown. Reconstruction-based detectors are also known to be adversarially fragile ([2608.16646](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16646)).

**Feasibility.** **6 GB is sufficient** for SD1/2/XL VAEs and the FLUX/SD3 VAE (around 84M parameters each) at 512–1024² in fp16 with tiled decoding, and the discriminator is small. Generating random-mask training data with SD2-inpainting or SDXL-inpainting fits in 6 GB at fp16 with CPU offload, but slowly. FLUX-Fill data generation needs a 24 GB-class GPU or the existing TGIF2 FLUX subsets, which already provide it.

### 3. Editability fixed points: localizing by asking an editor to re-edit

**Hypothesis.** A region that an editor E has just synthesized lies near a fixed point of "mask this region and re-inpaint it with E". Re-inpainting it changes little, because the content is already E's preferred completion given its context. An authentic region is not a fixed point: re-inpainting it replaces camera detail with E's prior. The asymmetry d(x, E(x, M_r)) against the expected change for authentic content, computed over a set of probe masks, localizes the edit. It holds for a bank of open proxy editors even when the true editor is closed.

**Method.** Cover the image with a randomized set of probe masks: superpixel-agnostic rectangles at several scales, so the method does not inherit object bias. For each mask, run a cheap re-inpaint using few steps, a low strength and a fixed seed, with SD2-inpaint, SDXL-inpaint and, where compute allows, FLUX-Fill. Measure the change in perceptual and residual space. Aggregate a per-pixel "editability" score by mask inversion: the pixel score is a weighted average of the change over the masks that cover it, normalized by a per-image null estimated on the mask complement. Compare editors to get a bank-level map and an attribution to the most consistent proxy. The method is too expensive to run at deployment, so **distill** it: run it offline on 20–50k images, then train a light student on frozen DINOv3 and residual features to predict the editability map. The student is the deployable localizer, and the fixed-point map acts as label-free supervision.

**Why it should beat current methods.** It is the only proposal that uses the *generator itself* as the forensic model. The question it asks is "would this editor have produced this?", which is closer to the semantics of an edit than any low-level statistic. That makes it a candidate for regimes where traces have been laundered, such as SR or an extra round-trip, since re-inpainting tests content preference and not noise. Distillation turns an expensive oracle into a cheap student, removes the need for any human or SAM mask, and therefore removes the ground-truth paradigm conflict described earlier.

**Closest prior work and novelty.** ReGFLoW uses diffusion reconstruction error as dense guidance in multiple-instance learning ([ReGFLoW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)). Training-free conditional-versus-unconditional diffusion reconstruction maps exist, and TRAIL's authors describe them ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). The novelty check found no mask-conditioned re-inpainting or idempotence test used as a forensic statistic, and no distillation of one. The theoretical framing should argue that the editor's denoise-decode map, restricted to its own outputs, is approximately contractive. This is the main defence against the expected objection that the method is merely "reconstruction error plus mask search".

**Experimental protocol.** Use the shared protocol, with emphasis on closed-editor transfer: the proxy bank uses only open models, and the tests are PIXAR's Gemini, GPT-image-1.5 and Seedream splits plus PromptForge's Nano-Banana split if it becomes available. Ablate the size of the editor bank (1, 2 or 3 editors), the number of probe masks, the number of steps, and the gap between oracle and student. Report the **cost per image** honestly.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** Cost (editors × masks × steps) is the main risk. Closed editors may have completion preferences unlike any open proxy. Low-texture authentic regions, such as sky or walls, may also behave like fixed points, which repeats FLAME's smooth-region false positives ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)). Reviewers will cite GAP-SAM's finding that VAE-reconstruction cues transfer poorly to local edits ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)). The reply is that this method tests the denoiser's prior, not the VAE, which can be shown directly by an ablation that uses VAE-only reconstruction.

**Feasibility.** **Partly feasible on 6 GB.** SD1.5/SD2-inpaint at 512² in fp16 (around 3–4 GB) runs, and SDXL-inpaint runs with offloading and attention slicing at roughly 10–30 s per call. The full oracle over 50k images × dozens of masks is weeks of compute on one small GPU. Generating the distillation set and adding FLUX-Fill (12B) to the bank need 24–80 GB GPUs or cloud time. The student trains on 6 GB.

### 4. Semantic-nullspace probing: removing object bias from frozen dense features

**Hypothesis.** The "boundary adhesion" of frozen-feature localizers is mostly a low-dimensional semantic component of the patch representation, the directions that encode objectness and category. That component can be estimated from *aligned* pairs that share semantics but differ in provenance. Projecting it out before the discriminator should improve random-mask, background and removal localization without hurting semantic-mask performance.

**Method.** Build aligned pairs following GAP-SAM's recipe, regenerating the same COCO scene with Canny/depth ControlNet ([GAP-SAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)), and Cozzolino et al.'s DDIM-inversion depth sweep, which holds semantics fixed while varying the provenance signal ([Cozzolino et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). From frozen multi-layer DINOv3/PE tokens, estimate two subspaces. The *semantic* subspace S is the top principal components of the variation in patch features across different objects within real images, weighted by SAM object-boundary proximity. The *provenance* subspace P comes from the difference between paired real and regenerated patches at identical locations. Train the simple discriminator (linear or a two-layer MLP per patch, plus a tiny upsampler) on features projected onto P ∩ S⊥. Add an adversarial term that penalizes predictability of SAM contours from the discriminator's logits, and train with TGIF2-style random masks only. Learn layer weights per depth, which settles the open question of whether dense signal lives in late layers (as TRAIL finds) or intermediate layers (as RINE finds) ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968); [RINE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19091)).

**Why it should beat current methods.** The largest measured generalization gaps are semantic. They are semantic versus random masks (0.50 vs 0.23), object versus background (GIFTBench, trained on Background: .905 vs .369), and add versus remove (.855 vs .390) ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28613); [GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)). Data alignment helped GAP-SAM. Making the alignment explicit in feature space attacks the same confound for *any* backbone, at linear-probe cost. It preserves the property that simple probes on frozen VFMs generalize better than LoRA ([Simplicity Prevails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01738)).

**Closest prior work and novelty.** The closest works are GAP-SAM (alignment at the data level, a SAM3 adapter), Forensic Twins (disjoint crops), TRAIL's aligned-real control used as a diagnostic, and token ranking for DINOv3 ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22471)). The novelty has three parts: an explicit semantic-nullspace projection for *dense* forensic probing, the first quantitative boundary-adhesion metric, and the first dense layer-selection study across PE, MetaCLIP-2 and DINOv3 for generative-edit localization. The notes found no such study.

**Experimental protocol.** The primary outcome is the object-bias index (semantic F1 − random F1) on TGIF2, together with BR-Gen and GIFTBench removal and background splits. The secondary outcome is the shared-protocol table. Run three controls. The first projects out a *random* subspace of equal dimension, to show that the specific subspace matters. The second checks performance on aligned-real sources, where false-positive area should fall. The third is a leave-one-editor-out check that the projection, estimated with FLUX ControlNet pairs, transfers to Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit and the Gemini splits.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** Semantic and provenance directions may not be separable, because edits *are* semantic. Projection could then remove signal along with bias, and the ablation over subspace rank has to show the trade-off. Reviewers may call this "INLP/nullspace projection applied to forensics". Lead with the measured bias reduction and the new metric rather than the projection algebra. The method is likely to be TIFS/TIP-strength on its own. Paired with proposal 1 or 2, as the debiasing layer for any dense discriminator, it becomes CVPR-strength.

**Feasibility.** **Fully feasible on 6 GB.** Features are pre-extracted once with ViT-L in fp16, and subspace estimation and probe training run on cached tensors. The aligned-pair set can be reused from public data such as OpenSDID and TGIF2 FR pairs with their sources, or generated slowly with SD-based ControlNet.

### 5. Physical-consistency localization that survives regeneration and laundering

**Hypothesis.** A local edit must agree with lighting, shadow, perspective and reflections that are fixed by its surroundings, yet the editor sees that context only through attention. Inserted objects should therefore show measurable disagreement between physically predicted and observed shading or shadow geometry, and removals should leave orphan shadows or reflections. These cues are mid-level and physical rather than low-level traces, so they should survive FR decoding, generative SR, recompression and noise injection, the very conditions that defeat trace-based localizers.

**Method.** Run off-the-shelf monocular estimators for depth, normals, intrinsic albedo/shading and shadows. From the normals and shading, fit a low-order global lighting model (spherical harmonics, robustly, with RANSAC over superpixels). Compute per-region residual maps: shading residual under the global light, shadow-direction consistency between detected shadow–caster pairs and the estimated light, and vanishing-line consistency. Fuse these maps with a trace-based localizer, such as proposal 1 or 2 or TruFor, through a small gating network whose weight is learned to *increase* when trace evidence is weak, for example under FR or after SR. Classical forensic tests of shadow consistency and perspective (Kee, O'Brien and Farid; Farid's paint-by-text studies) supply the physical constraints ([Kee et al.](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=108529); [Perspective inconsistency](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/2206.14617); [Lighting inconsistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13744v2)). Generated images show measurable projective and shadow violations ([Shadows Don't Lie](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)).

**Why it should beat current methods.** It does not beat them in raw IoU on clean data. Its value is complementarity in exactly the regime where everything else collapses: FR plus laundering. If physics residuals keep even a modest AUROC after Real-ESRGAN and an extra VAE round-trip, while trace methods fall to chance, the fused system's worst-case performance improves. Worst-case performance is the deployment-relevant number.

**Closest prior work and novelty.** The closest works are a counterfactual physical-violation benchmark, which is rendered and synthetic, with VLM baselines at 64–68% macro-F1 ([2610.09205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09205)); a lighting-knowledge study of editors that does no detection ([2606.26738](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26738)); and semantic manipulation localization via TRACE ([2604.10132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10132)). The novelty check found no method that localizes real 2026 editor outputs with physics residuals. Measuring *how often* FLUX-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, GPT-Image and Nano-Banana violate physics would be a first in itself.

**Experimental protocol.** Begin with a prevalence study: annotate physical violations (missing or misdirected shadow, orphan shadow, inconsistent reflection, perspective) on about 1–2k edits drawn from PIXAR, TGIF2 FLUX and the closed-editor splits, stratified by edit type (add, remove, replace, attribute). Then evaluate localization on the shared protocol, reporting results per edit type and the **robustness delta**, meaning performance after Real-ESRGAN, an extra round-trip and noise injection, compared with trace baselines. Include whole-frame instruction editors as a predicted *negative* case, since they may re-light the entire scene consistently.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** Recall will be low: attribute changes, texture edits and careful removals are physically plausible. Monocular estimators err on exactly the unusual content that edits produce, so a residual may reflect estimator failure rather than physics. Mitigate this by ensembling estimators and reporting agreement between them. There is no labelled benchmark yet, and building one is part of the contribution. Present this as the "robust-but-low-recall" branch of a fused system, not as a stand-alone SOTA claim.

**Feasibility.** **Feasible on 6 GB with small estimators.** Base-size Depth-Anything-class models, normal and intrinsic estimators fit in fp16. The lighting fit and the residuals run on the CPU. Large estimators, and VLM-based shadow reasoning above about 7B parameters, need more memory or cloud inference.

### 6. Conformal risk control for localization maps, calibrated on authentic pixels

**Hypothesis.** Threshold choice is a first-order error source. TRAIL loses 0.17 Dice between its oracle and fixed thresholds ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)), and benchmarks disagree on thresholds (fixed 0.5 vs EER) ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577)). Conformal risk control can choose the threshold for any score map so as to bound a pixel-level risk with finite-sample guarantees. Calibrating the authentic-pixel false-discovery rate on *real images only* needs no fake labels and remains valid under any editor shift, because the guarantee concerns authentic content.

**Method.** Given any localizer's score map, apply conformal risk control ([CRC](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2208.02814)) to choose λ such that the expected fraction of authentic pixels flagged is at most α. Calibrate on pristine and VAE-round-trip-only images, so the guarantee holds for authentic content regardless of which editor is in use. For the dual objective, bounding the missed fraction of the edited region, use a labelled calibration set with **Mondrian conditioning** on editor family and on post-processing type, and use weighted conformal prediction with density-ratio weights estimated from frozen-feature image embeddings for covariate shift. Output a three-level map (certain-edited, uncertain, certain-authentic) and a risk–coverage curve.

**Why it should beat current methods.** It does not compete on IoU. It replaces an arbitrary threshold with a guarantee, adds a pristine-image false-positive bound that current metrics ignore ([GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)), and gives forensic examiners a quantity they can defend. Applied on top of proposals 1–4, it should close much of the gap between fixed and oracle thresholds.

**Closest prior work and novelty.** The closest works are EG-Mamba's uncertainty-driven updates without guarantees ([EG-Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29156)), image-level calibrated certification ([2610.05870](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.05870)), and conformal methods for detection boxes ([2505.24038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24038)). The novelty check found no conformal method for pixel-level forgery masks.

**Experimental protocol.** Report empirical authentic-FDR coverage against nominal α on in-distribution data, under leave-one-editor-out, under each laundering condition, and on the INP-X control. Report the resulting F1 and IoU against fixed 0.5 and oracle thresholds, and the size of the uncertain region. The critical experiment is the coverage failure under shift for the *edited-region* guarantee, and how much weighted or Mondrian conformal recovers.

**Risks and reviewer objections.** Exchangeability fails under a new editor, so the edited-region guarantee breaks exactly where it matters. Reporting that honestly is itself a finding, and the real-only authentic-pixel guarantee is unaffected by editor shift but not by shift in authentic content, such as new cameras or platforms. Alone this is a TIFS-level methodological transfer. It is strongest as the calibration layer of proposal 1 or 2.

**Feasibility.** **Trivial on 6 GB.** Conformal calibration runs on the CPU over cached score maps.

## Conclusion

The field has been optimizing the wrong variable. Since 2023, most architectural work has changed how masks are *decoded*: diffusion decoders, MLLM decoders, SAM adapters. The decisive variable is which *evidence* survives a generation pipeline that re-decodes every pixel. Two controls show how fragile current evidence is. INP-X shows that image-level detectors read the global decode, and random masks show that localizers read objects. Any claim of progress that skips these two controls is uninterpretable. The most promising direction for your frozen-latent pipeline is therefore not a better decoder. It is to make the discriminator's evidence *provenance-specific*: real-only dense prediction to model "camera-like", a round-trip convergence statistic to separate "reconstructed" from "generated", and a semantic nullspace to stop the simple head from falling back on objectness. Proposals 1, 2, 4 and 6 together form one coherent, 6 GB-feasible TPAMI-scale system with a single story: localization as calibrated provenance estimation.

One uncomfortable result should be planned for rather than hidden. For whole-frame MLLM editors, the boundary between "edited" and "regenerated" may not exist in the pixels at all. In that case passive pixel forensics reaches a ceiling, and the right output is a graded, semantic change map with calibrated uncertainty, supported by physics cues that survive regeneration. A paper that measures *where* that ceiling sits, per editor, under a fixed protocol with dual ground truth (semantic intent and regenerated-pixel maps), would be valuable regardless of which proposal wins. Reviewers do not yet have that map of the problem.
