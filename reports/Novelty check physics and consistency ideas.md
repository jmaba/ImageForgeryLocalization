# Physics residuals and relighting remain unclaimed territory

Two of the seven ideas look unpublished. An explicit image-formation test for localization (foundation normals and intrinsic shading, a global spherical-harmonics (SH) lighting fit, per-region shading and illuminant residuals) has not been published. Neither has generative relighting used as a forensic instrument. A thin-lens defocus-versus-depth residual is unpublished too. As of 4 November 2026, the searches found no paper doing any of the three. The other four ideas sit in crowded neighbourhoods, and novelty there depends on the exact combination and the evaluation setting. Real-only pairwise consistency has a dense lineage, from Huh et al. 2018 through Forensic Similarity, Noiseprint and EXIF-as-language. In April 2026 DiffusionPrint added the closest competitor: a self-supervised generative fingerprint fused into TruFor. Fusion has precedents at the feature level (MMFusion, DiffusionPrint) and in per-image expert selection (RiSE, limited to document images). Evaluation critiques are partly anticipated. TGIF2's random masks expose object bias, and TRAIL's "aligned-real" control already scores the unedited source inside the edit mask, which comes close to the proposed matched transplant null. Reference-based localization rests on 2017-2019 DARPA MediFor provenance infrastructure, and SIGMA (2026) uses original-versus-edited feature differencing only to *annotate* masks. The new threat for the physics ideas is **GFrame (arXiv:2607.18040, July 2026)**. It feeds monocular depth and normals into a supervised localizer, so "foundation geometry helps localization" is no longer new. What remains new is the explicit, training-free physical hypothesis test. No published evidence yet shows that any real-only or physics method *wins* on fully regenerated or LLM-edited images, so that remains an open empirical claim, not a citable fact.

## Lighting physics was automated for classification, never for per-region localization

The classical physics-forensics line (Johnson & Farid, Kee & Farid, Riess & Angelopoulou, de Carvalho) produced manual or semi-automatic tests. Since 2016 these have been automated only in narrow ways. **Pomari, Ruppert, Rezende, Rocha and Carvalho, "Image Splicing Detection Through Illumination Inconsistencies and Deep Learning" (IEEE ICIP 2018)** fed de Carvalho illuminant maps to a transfer-learned CNN for splicing detection on DSO-1/DSI-1 ([FAPESP record](https://bv.fapesp.br/en/publicacao/221244); [SigPort poster](https://sigport.org/node/3588)). **Hadwiger and Riess, "Deep Metric Color Embeddings for Splicing Localization in Severely Degraded Images" (arXiv:2206.10737, 2022)** is the closest Riess-lab deep successor. It learns colour/illuminant-related embeddings for splicing *localization* under strong compression, but fits no lighting model ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10737)). On the generative side, **Farid, "Lighting (In)consistency of Paint by Text" (arXiv:2207.13744, 2022)** fitted SH lighting to DALL-E 2 images. Global lighting was plausible, but lighting **within** an image "can be highly variable". The analysis was exploratory and not automated ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13744)). Its companion, **Farid, "Perspective (In)consistency of Paint by Text" (arXiv:2206.14617, 2022)**, did the same for vanishing points, shadows and reflections ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14617)). Farid's 2025 Content Authenticity Initiative posts restate the argument and warn that the advantage may be temporary ([CAI lighting blog](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/photo-forensics-from-lighting-environments); [CAI shadows/reflections](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/photo-forensics-from-lighting-shadows-and-reflections)).

Where explicit lighting models do appear inside learned detectors, they are face-specific and image-level. **"Face Forgery Detection by 3D Decomposition" (arXiv:2011.09737, 2020)** splits faces into shape, texture, ambient light and direct light ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.09737)). **Fei, Jia, Huang, Zhang and Zhou, "Exploring Specular Reflection Inconsistency for Generalizable Face Forgery Detection" (SRI-Net, arXiv:2602.06452, 2026)** combines Retinex texture, SH ambient/direct light and a Phong specular term to classify traditional and diffusion deepfakes ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06452)). **Ciamarra et al., "SurFake" (arXiv:2310.20621, 2023)** builds a surface-geometry and illumination descriptor for FF++ deepfake classification ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20621)). None of these produces an edit map for natural scenes.

The closest localizer is **Yu, Guo, Ma and Yang, "When 2D Cues Fail: Improving Image Manipulation Localization with Reliable 3D Geometry" (GFrame, arXiv:2607.18040, July 2026)**. It runs MoGe-2 (or Metric3D) to get depth and normals. It then gates them with hand-designed reliability cues: Laplacian depth confidence, normal-norm confidence and depth–normal agreement. The gated features are fused with RGB features in a supervised segmenter trained on CASIAv2 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)). **It reaches in-distribution F1 0.592–0.621 against 0.565 for Mesorch, and 0.490 out of distribution.** It has no albedo/shading split, no SH or light-direction fit, no illuminant-colour check and no training-free residual. Physical cues such as lighting appear only in its related-work section ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)). Its JPEG table matters for idea 1's robustness pitch. **Under JPEG the geometry stream buys nothing: CASIAv1 F1 falls from 0.537 at Q100 to 0.225 at Q50, averaging 0.372 against 0.347 for Mesorch** ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)). The authors also stress that reconstructed geometry is noisy on manipulated images. That is the central confound for any residual method: the estimators run on the edited image itself.

Two 2026 benchmarks bracket the problem without solving it. **Esfahani, Salem, Alser and Calhoun, "Do Vision Models Learn Physical Constraints or Rendering Shortcuts?" (arXiv:2610.09205, Oct 2026)** renders 5,500 Mitsuba-3 images, each scene family having one clean image and ten violations (shadow, reflection, support, surface response, occlusion), with masks. **VLM baselines reach 64.0–67.8% macro-F1, and LLaVA falls from 64.0% to 40.8% under intervention shift**, which shows shortcut learning ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09205)). The data is synthetic, and no lighting model is fitted. **"Do Image Editing Models Understand Lighting?" (arXiv:2606.26738, June 2026)** probes editors' lighting knowledge but does no detection ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26738)). Searches for intrinsic decomposition combined with forgery or AIGC detection returned nothing. The tools exist, for example Careaga & Aksoy's ordinal-shading intrinsics ([compphoto/Intrinsic](https://github.com/compphoto/Intrinsic)), but no forensic use of them turned up.

### Generative relighting appears only as an attack

No paper uses IC-Light, LightLab, DiffusionRenderer, Neural Gaffer, DiLightNet or neural inverse rendering as a forensic instrument. An arXiv query pairing relighting with forensics, fake detection or forgery returned four hits, none of them a detector ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26232)). The nearest work treats relighting as a **manipulation**. **Uluirmak and Kurban, "Image Forgery Detection under Generative and Composited Background Replacement" (BG-REAL, arXiv:2607.26232, 2026)** built 25,624 images that include foreground-conditioned relighting, each with processing-matched authentic controls. **Seven 2022-2026 detectors reach zero-shot AUROC ≤ 0.745, localizers mark the preserved subject rather than the replaced background, and an AIGC detector flags most authentic images after a diffusion-autoencoder pass** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26232)). Inverse-rendering queries returned only detectors *of* neural-rendered or 3DGS content (arXiv:2411.08642; Fake3DGS, arXiv:2604.27590) ([arXiv 2411.08642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08642); [arXiv 2604.27590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27590)). The conceptual relatives are reconstruction-error detectors such as AEROBLADE and DiffCoR ([DiffCoR](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/oj/2025/01/11018794/278Rb188aek)). Meanwhile, shadow-aware relighting editors (arXiv:2602.21820; PixHt-Lab, arXiv:2303.00137) are built to remove the very cues idea 1 measures ([arXiv 2602.21820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21820); [arXiv 2303.00137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00137)). MirrorVerse concedes that diffusion editors "often fail to fully adhere to physical laws" for shadows and reflections ([arXiv 2504.15397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15397)).

No study measures physics cues on GPT-Image, Nano-Banana or FLUX Kontext instruction edits, and none publishes JPEG/resize curves for a lighting-based detector. The claim that low-frequency physics residuals survive re-sharing is plausible, but untested in the literature. GFrame's collapse shows that "uses geometry" does not imply "robust". Instruction editors that regenerate the whole frame may also re-harmonize lighting globally. That would shrink the shading signal while leaving shadow and reflection errors, an open question that should be stated as a hypothesis.

## Optics and perspective cues stop at image-level detection

Blur forensics is old, but every variant found lacks a depth model. An IEEE paper from around 2008, "Digital Image Forgery Detection Based on the Consistency of Defocus Blur", compares blur only between patches *assumed* to be at equal depth ([IEEE Xplore 4604037](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4604037/)). **Bahrami, Kot, Li and Li, "Blurred Image Splicing Localization by Exposing Blur Type Inconsistency" (IEEE TIFS, 2015)** tests blur *type* (defocus vs. motion), not magnitude against depth ([DR-NTU](https://dr.ntu.edu.sg/handle/10220/38453)). A 2017 IJICA follow-up ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1504/IJICA.2017.082495)) and a 2020/21 Springer chapter, "Defocused Image Splicing Localization by Distinguishing Multiple Cues between Raw Naturally Blur and Artificial Blur" ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-69449-4_12)), separate optical from artificial blur, again without optics. The only generative-era paper is **Jeon and Woo, "Seeing Through the Blur: Unlocking Defocus Maps for Deepfake Detection" (arXiv:2509.23289, Sept 2025)**. It feeds a defocus map to a learned real-vs-fake classifier. It does no localization and no thin-lens fit ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23289)). The thin-lens blur–depth relation is standard outside forensics. The ICCV 2023 work by Piché-Meunier et al. explicitly enforces consistency between defocus, lens parameters and depth, but for rendering ([ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Piche-Meunier_Lens_Parameter_Estimation_for_Realistic_Depth_of_Field_Modeling_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)). An arXiv boolean query combining defocus, forgery and depth over 2016-2026 returned 16 hits; Jeon & Woo was the only forensic one ([arXiv 2509.23289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23289)).

Depth enters localizers only as a learned stream. GFrame is one example. **DR-Net, "Depth-assisted refinement network for image manipulation localization" (Neurocomputing, 2026)** runs parallel RGB and depth transformers. Its authors and numbers could not be retrieved ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925231226011732)). The face-video paper arXiv:2411.18572 adds depth attention ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18572v1)). None of them couples depth to an independent optical measurement. GFrame's depth–normal agreement checks the estimator against itself.

Chromatic-aberration forensics has had no deep-learning-era revival on generated content. **Mayer and Stamm, "Accurate and Efficient Image Forgery Detection Using Lateral Chromatic Aberration" (IEEE TIFS, 2018)** is the last substantive statistical model, testing local against global lateral CA ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322779654_Accurate_and_Efficient_Image_Forgery_Detection_Using_Lateral_Chromatic_Aberration)). The same authors showed that CA can be forged deliberately ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282379864_Anti-forensics_of_chromatic_aberration)). A 2025 FCN detects CA itself, not forgeries ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-6008-7_16)), and CHROMA (arXiv:2606.08864) uses inter-channel colour statistics rather than a radial lens model ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08864v1)). Nobody has measured whether a diffusion-inpainted region breaks the local CA field. The likely reason is practical: CA is sub-pixel, so resizing, JPEG below about Q90 and in-camera correction erase it. This is an inference, but reviewers will raise it.

Perspective has the most automation. **Sarkar, Mai, Mahapatra, Lazebnik, Forsyth and Bhattad, "Shadows Don't Lie and Lines Can't Bend! Generative Models don't know Projective Geometry...for now" (CVPR 2024, arXiv:2311.17138)** trains pixel-blind classifiers on perspective fields, DeepLSD lines and object–shadow masks. **It reaches AUC 0.72–0.97 overall, and 0.84 (outdoor) and 0.82 (indoor) on images that a signal detector cannot separate.** Localization is Grad-CAM only, the images are whole generations, and there is no robustness test ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)). Generators are being patched. **ControlVP (Okumura, Shiohara, Yamasaki, arXiv:2512.07504, 2025)** corrects vanishing points ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07504)), and Upadhyay et al. add perspective constraints ([arXiv 2312.00944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00944)). GeoEdit (arXiv:2606.30003) still concedes that 2D editors "produce perspective violations" when objects are moved or scaled ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30003)). That concession supports a per-object test for inserted and moved objects, which nobody has built.

## Real-only consistency has a dense lineage, but no proof that it wins on regeneration

The pairwise "same source?" idea is well established. **Huh, Liu, Owens and Efros, "Fighting Fake News: Image Splice Detection via Learned Self-Consistency" (ECCV 2018, arXiv:1805.04096)** learned EXIF-agreement between patch pairs from real photos only and localized splices from consistency maps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04096)). **Mayer and Stamm, "Forensic Similarity for Digital Images" (arXiv:1902.04684; TIFS 2020, venue unverified)** is the closest comparator precedent: a learned similarity network on patch pairs that generalizes to unseen traces ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684)). "Exposing Fake Images with Forensic Similarity Graphs" (arXiv:1912.02861) replaces majority voting with community detection ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02861)). **Cozzolino and Verdoliva, "Noiseprint" (arXiv:1808.08396)** is a real-only Siamese camera fingerprint ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08396)). SISL (arXiv:2203.07824) is a self-supervised splice localizer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07824)). **Zheng, Shrivastava and Owens, "EXIF as Language" (CVPR 2023, arXiv:2301.04647)** localizes zero-shot by clustering real-only patch embeddings ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04647)). TruFor's Noiseprint++ is trained only on real data, but the TruFor localizer is supervised ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957)). FOCAL (arXiv:2308.09307) and SAFIRE (arXiv:2412.08197) adopt per-image relative definitions of "forged", but both are trained with forged masks ([FOCAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09307); [SAFIRE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08197)). Fang and Stamm show that Siamese consistency localizers can be fooled with synthetic traces ([arXiv 2211.12314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12314)).

**Giakoumoglou and Papadopoulos, "DiffusionPrint: Learning Generative Fingerprints for Diffusion-Based Inpainting Localization" (arXiv:2604.12443, April 2026)** is the main threat to idea 4. It starts from the same premise: full regeneration re-decodes the image and erases camera noise. It learns patch-level MoCo fingerprints from the signal "same inpainting model, same fingerprint". **Used as an extra modality in TruFor and MMFusion, it gains up to +28% on unseen mask types** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)). It differs from idea 4 in four ways: it trains on generator-labelled inpaints, uses a CNN rather than frozen foundation features, applies no majority/reference pairwise scoring, and fuses at the feature level inside a supervised model. The training-data recipe also has precedents. **B-Free (Guillaro et al., arXiv:2412.17671)** makes fakes from real images through Stable Diffusion conditioning, but only for image-level detection ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17671)). **AEROBLADE (Ricker, Lukovnikov, Fischer, arXiv:2401.17879)** uses autoencoder reconstruction error and only qualitatively notes that inpainted regions show up ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17879)). ZED (arXiv:2409.15875) is a real-only image-level detector ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)). Cozzolino, Poggi and Verdoliva (arXiv:2608.12155) show that regenerated images have lower latent variance and effective dimensionality in foundation features, a cell-level statistic idea 4 could exploit ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)). TRAIL (arXiv:2608.18968) is a training-free frozen-DINOv3 perturbation-drift localizer. **It reaches 0.903 patch AUROC on CocoGlide against 0.912 for mask-supervised Detective SAM** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). It is the strongest training-free baseline idea 4 must beat, although it is not pairwise and not trained.

The record on fully regenerated (FR) and instruction edits is unanimous: supervised trace localizers collapse. **TGIF (Mareen et al., arXiv:2407.11566)** found that IFL methods localize spliced inpainting but "fail" on regenerated images ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11566)). **TGIF2 (arXiv:2603.28613, March 2026)** found that fine-tuning on FR helps, but random non-semantic masks reveal "object bias" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). **AIForge-Doc v2 (Wu et al., arXiv:2604.25213) gives the cleanest contrast: TruFor AUC falls from 0.962 on cross-camera splicing to 0.599 on GPT-Image-2 inpainting** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25213)). LocateEdit-Bench (arXiv:2602.05577) calls inpainting-focused localizers "ineffective" against instruction edits ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577)). **PromptForge-350k's ICL-Net reaches 62.5% IoU in-domain but only 41.5% on unseen editors** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)). The FR localizers that succeed are supervised on FR-like data: FLAME (arXiv:2606.02178), ICL-Net, MLLM localizers such as FakeShield, SIDA and LEGION, and Venus-DeFakerOne. Proactive watermarking (APT, arXiv:2608.30656) also succeeds ([FLAME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178); [APT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30656)). **No paper shows a real-only or consistency method winning at FR localization.** The obstacle is structural. Under true full regeneration every pixel passes through the decoder, so a "majority is real" comparator on decoder statistics faces a null that is true. The usable signal then has to be the difference between re-encoded and de-novo content, not camera versus non-camera.

### Fusion with real-only or physics cues is unbenchmarked

Map-level fusion of localizer outputs dates to **Charitidis, Kordopatis-Zilos, Papadopoulos and Kompatsiaris, "Operation-wise Attention Network for Tampering Localization Fusion" (arXiv:2105.05515, 2021)** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05515)). **MMFusion (Triaridis, Tsigos, Mezaris, arXiv:2312.01790)** compares early and late fusion of forensic filters and adopts early fusion ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01790)). Per-sample routing exists inside Omni-IML's modal gate (arXiv:2411.14823) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14823)). **RiSE (arXiv:2610.24480, Oct 2026)** selects one of nine supervised residual experts per image by latent-perturbation confidence. **It reports +35.8% on real-world forgeries with nine experts**, but only for text-centric images ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24480)). Shuai et al. warn that naive fusion of local and global branches "can amplify noise and errors" ([arXiv 2509.13776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13776)). BG-REAL supplies the motivation for fusing physics cues, since signal localizers mark the wrong region on background and relighting edits ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26232)). No paper fuses TruFor or Mesorch outputs per image with a real-only comparator or a physics residual, or routes on whether an image looks spliced or regenerated.

## Evaluation critiques are partly anticipated; reference-based localization is unframed

Shortcut analyses are mature for *image-level* detection. **Grommelt, Weiss, Pfreundt and Keuper, "Fake or JPEG? Revealing Common Biases in Generated Image Detection Datasets" (arXiv:2403.17608)** show that GenImage detectors learn JPEG and size. Removing the bias lifts cross-generator accuracy by more than 11 points ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17608)). **Mandala (arXiv:2607.06615, 2026)** shows that a trivial `is_tiff` classifier reaches **0.80 AUC on CASIA v2** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06615)). Localization-side probes are fewer. **TGIF2's random-mask split** tests whether a localizer needs objects ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)). Kong et al.'s patch shuffling (arXiv:2310.00234) suppresses semantics ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00234)). The most important precedent for the matched transplant null appeared in this project's earlier research, not in the new notes. **TRAIL reports an "aligned-real control": it scores the unedited source image inside the edit mask and gets 0.697 AUROC, against 0.903 for the edited image (a +0.206 increment). On TGIF2 the increment shrinks to +0.068 with semantic masks, against +0.164 with random ones** ([TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18968)). That is the transplant null in all but name: same mask, edit removed. Idea 5 can claim only its systematic, cross-method, metric-level use, for example a reported null-AUC column for every baseline. It cannot claim the concept. No colour or texture-statistics control scorer for localization surfaced, and no paper argues for interior-only (boundary-excluded) scoring. NIST MFC scoring is believed to use no-score zones around mask boundaries, which may anticipate interior-only scoring, but this was not verified ([MFC datasets paper](https://mig.nist.gov/MFC/Web/Papers/MFC_Dataset_WACV2019.pdf)).

Re-sharing robustness is established territory. **Wu, Zhou, Tian and Liu, "Robust Image Forgery Detection over Online Social Network Shared Images" (CVPR 2022)** set the OSN baseline ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Wu_Robust_Image_Forgery_Detection_Over_Online_Social_Network_Shared_Images_CVPR_2022_paper.html)). TGIF covers JPEG and WebP, TGIF2 shows that generative super-resolution "significantly weakens forensic traces", and Mandelli, Bestagini and Tubaro (arXiv:2407.10736) analyse SD-autoencoder laundering ([TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613); [arXiv 2407.10736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10736)). A re-sharing sweep is therefore an extension, and new only when crossed with the nulls. Pseudo-labelled masks are now a design choice nobody audits. LocateEdit-Bench has an LLM name the object and SAM3 segment it, with no pixel-difference check. PIXAR thresholds aligned pixel differences. **A 200-sample check of PromptForge masks found 8 significant errors**, including failures on pure colour edits ([LocateEdit-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05577); [PIXAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20193); [PromptForge-350k](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29386)). PIXAR itself argues that object masks misalign with the true edit signal ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)). SIGMA admits that instruction-grounded masks miss "unintended editor side-effects" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27924)). GIFTBench excludes real images from its localization metrics ([GIFTBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01778)). A cross-paradigm mask audit has not been published.

For reference-based localization, the infrastructure is old and the framing is missing. MediFor provenance work by Pinto et al. (arXiv:1706.00447), Bharati et al. (arXiv:1705.11187) and **Moreira et al., "Image Provenance Analysis at Scale" (arXiv:1801.06510)** built retrieval and provenance graphs ([arXiv 1706.00447](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00447); [arXiv 1705.11187](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11187); [arXiv 1801.06510](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06510)). Chen et al. (arXiv:1905.11903) retrieve splice donors instead of detecting artifacts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11903)). Wu, AbdAlmageed and Natarajan's DMVN (arXiv:1705.09765) localizes given a donor/host pair ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09765)). **SIGMA (Zhuang et al., arXiv:2605.27924, 2026)** differences original and edited images in foundation-feature space to create about 1.1M pseudo-masks. It states that pixel differencing is overwhelmed by diffusion perturbations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27924)). It annotates training data and is not deployed as a forensic localizer. X-Edit, EditSleuth and LocateEdit-Bench ship source–edit pairs but evaluate blind localizers ([X-Edit](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/XAI4CV/html/Bazyleva_X-Edit_Detecting_and_Localizing_Edits_in_Images_Altered_by_Text-Guided_CVPRW_2025_paper.html); [EditSleuth](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08695)). Empirically, FR destroys local trace contrast (TGIF, TGIF2). GPT-ImgEval reports that GPT-4o regenerates the whole image even for brushed edits, a claim seen only through a summary ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02782v1)). INP-X shows the converse: restoring original background pixels drops image detectors from about 91% to 55% ([INP-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00192)). **No paper argues formally that blind trace localization is ill-posed for FR images.** That argument is open. In its strongest form, a decoder statistic yields an image-wide null that is true under whole-frame re-decoding, so only semantic, physical or reference evidence can localize.

The strongest publishable unit combines ideas 1, 3a and 7. Training-free physics residuals (shading under a global SH fit, illuminant chromaticity, defocus against depth) would be fused per image with TruFor/Mesorch, with the fusion weight learned to rise when trace evidence fails. It would be evaluated with the TRAIL-style aligned-real null, TGIF2 random masks and a JPEG/resize/Real-ESRGAN sweep. GFrame is the baseline that must be beaten or shown to be complementary. A first prevalence study (how often 2026 editors violate shading, shadow, perspective or defocus consistency) would be a contribution in itself, because none exists. Idea 2 is the riskiest and most original. Present it as an exploratory instrument, and pair it with an attack analysis of whether IC-Light-style relighting erases idea 1's cues.

## Where the search could have missed prior art

The coverage has clear blind spots, and every "not found" above should be read in that light. The searches leaned on the arXiv API and general web search. Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar and IEEE Xplore were not queried directly. Non-arXiv work in IEEE TIFS, WIFS, ICASSP, IH&MMSec and CVPR Media Forensics workshops (2024-2026) is therefore under-covered, and those are the venues most likely to hold a short paper on lighting residuals or blur–depth consistency. Most papers were read at abstract level. The quantitative results of Pomari 2018, SRI-Net, Jeon & Woo, LocateEdit-Bench, TGIF2 per-method tables and PromptForge baselines were not extracted. GFrame and Sarkar et al. were read in full. DR-Net, the 2008 defocus paper and the 2020/21 Springer blur chapter were paywalled, so their authors and numbers are unverified. Several venue labels (Forensic Similarity in TIFS, FOCAL, SISL, MMFusion, B-Free at CVPR 2025) come from memory. Nobody checked whether the IC-Light, LightLab, DiffusionRenderer or Neural Gaffer papers discuss forensic use in their limitations sections. The NIST MFC scoring documentation (no-score zones), C2PA/Content Credentials and RAW-provenance literature were not searched, and neither were Chinese-language venues (CNKI), where blur-forensics variants are common. The TRAIL aligned-real precedent came from this project's earlier research rather than the present search. Similar near-misses for the colour-control scorer and interior-only scoring could exist as passing remarks inside benchmark papers that were only read at abstract level.

## Conclusion

The field has inverted the classical physics-forensics programme. Farid-style physical tests became interpretable *image-level* classifiers (Sarkar et al., SRI-Net, Jeon & Woo), while *localization* absorbed geometry only as another supervised feature stream (GFrame, DR-Net). The space between them is the unpublished contribution: physical laws used as calibrated per-region hypothesis tests, fused with trace localizers. The timing works because the evidence now shows that trace localizers fail exactly where regeneration and relighting happen (AIForge-Doc v2, BG-REAL, TGIF2). It also works against the idea, because generator research is actively closing the geometry gap (ControlVP, GeoEdit, shadow-aware relighting), so a physics-cue paper should present itself as a robustness branch with a measured shelf life, not as a permanent solution.

For the consistency and evaluation ideas, the honest framing differs from the original pitch. A real-only comparator should not claim to win on fully regenerated images. The decoder argument predicts that it cannot on trace statistics alone. Its credible claim is complementarity on spliced and partially regenerated edits, plus a falsifiable test on FR images. The ill-posedness argument, stated formally, is the most leveraged unclaimed idea in the set. It would justify reference-based localization, physics cues and the matched-null protocol together, and it reframes the evaluation critiques as consequences of a theorem rather than a list of complaints.
