# Real-only / self-supervised region-consistency forgery localization, and fusion of localizers (focus: fully regenerated and instruction-edited images)

Search date: 2026-11-04. Sources: arXiv API (titles, authors, abstracts, and submission dates checked for every arXiv ID below). Venue labels marked "(venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked)" were NOT confirmed against a proceedings page in this session; the arXiv ID, title, authors, and date WERE confirmed. Numbers are taken from the arXiv abstracts unless stated otherwise.

Target idea being checked: (a) a pairwise "same-source?" comparator over frozen foundation-model + noise-residual cell features, trained ONLY on self-made splices of real photos (VAE round-trips through several diffusion autoencoders, real inpainting, processing), scored by a majority/reference assumption, with no real manipulated image or mask used in training; (b) per-image fusion of supervised localizers (TruFor, Mesorch) with such real-only or physics cues.

---

## Q1. Successors of Huh et al. (2018): which are trained only on real or self-generated data, and do they localize?

### Takeaway
There is a clear line of work that learns "same source / same trace" from real or self-made data and localizes by consistency or clustering: Huh 2018, then Forensic Similarity and Forensic Similarity Graphs, Noiseprint, SISL, EXIF-as-language, TruFor's Noiseprint++, FOCAL, SAFIRE, and in 2026 DiffusionPrint. None of these combines all of the following: (i) frozen foundation-model plus noise-residual cell features, (ii) negatives built from VAE round-trips through several diffusion autoencoders plus real inpainting, (iii) a pairwise comparator with majority/reference scoring, and (iv) zero real manipulated images or masks used anywhere. The real-only detectors (ZED, AEROBLADE, B-Free) are whole-image detectors. Only AEROBLADE mentions qualitative inpainting localization.

### Cited Findings
**Pairwise / consistency lineage (real or self-supervised training)**
- Huh, Liu, Owens, Efros. "Fighting Fake News: Image Splice Detection via Learned Self-Consistency." ECCV 2018. arXiv:1805.04096 (May 2018). Trained only on real photos to predict whether two patches share EXIF attributes. It localizes splices by patch-pair consistency (a "consistency map"), with no manipulated training data. This is the direct ancestor of (a). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04096)
- Mayer & Stamm. "Forensic Similarity for Digital Images." arXiv:1902.04684 (Feb 2019). IEEE TIFS 2020 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). A CNN feature extractor plus a 3-layer "similarity network" maps a patch PAIR to same/different forensic trace. Training uses camera-model and editing-operation labels. It works on "unknown" traces not seen in training, more than halves error rates versus prior art, and is applied to forgery detection and localization. This is the closest pairwise-comparator precedent. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684)
- Mayer & Stamm. "Exposing Fake Images with Forensic Similarity Graphs." arXiv:1912.02861 (Dec 2019). IEEE JSTSP 2020 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Patches are graph vertices and pairwise forensic similarity gives the edges. Localization is done by community detection, with communities corresponding to tampered and unaltered regions. This is a graph and partition alternative to majority voting. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02861)
- Fang & Stamm. "Attacking Image Splicing Detection and Localization Algorithms Using Synthetic Traces." arXiv:2211.12314 (Nov 2022). Shows that Siamese-consistency splice localizers can be fooled by synthetic traces. This is a limitation for any comparator approach. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12314)
- Cozzolino & Verdoliva. "Noiseprint: a CNN-based camera model fingerprint." arXiv:1808.08396 (Aug 2018). IEEE TIFS 2020 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). A Siamese network trained on pairs of patches from same versus different cameras, using real images only. It localizes forgeries as noiseprint inconsistencies. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08396)
- Agrawal et al. "SISL: Self-Supervised Image Signature Learning for Splicing Detection and Localization." arXiv:2203.07824 (Mar 2022). A self-supervised splice localizer that the abstract says avoids pixel masks, camera IDs, and metadata. Venue not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07824)
- Zheng, Shrivastava, Owens. "EXIF as Language: Learning Cross-Modal Associations Between Images and Camera Metadata." arXiv:2301.04647 (Jan 2023). CVPR 2023 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Contrastive patch–EXIF-text embedding trained on real photos. It localizes spliced regions "zero shot" by clustering the patch embeddings within an image, so it is real-only and localizes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04647)
- Guillaro, Cozzolino, Sud, Dufour, Verdoliva. "TruFor: Leveraging all-round clues for trustworthy image forgery detection and localization." arXiv:2212.10957 (Dec 2022). CVPR 2023 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Its Noiseprint++ fingerprint is "trained only on real data in a self-supervised manner." The full TruFor model is a transformer that fuses RGB and Noiseprint++ and is trained with manipulated data and masks. It outputs a localization map, an integrity score, and a reliability map. So TruFor is hybrid: the real-only component is the fingerprint, not the localizer. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957)
- Wu, Chen, Zhou, Li. "Rethinking Image Forgery Detection via Soft Contrastive Learning and Unsupervised Clustering" (FOCAL). arXiv:2308.09307 (Aug 2023). Venue not verified in this session. It argues that forged/pristine is only defined relative to a single image. It uses per-image soft contrastive learning, then on-the-fly unsupervised clustering at test time. Reported IoU gains over SOTA: +24.8% Coverage, +18.9% Columbia, +17.3% FF++, +15.3% MISD, +15.0% CASIA, +10.5% NIST. FOCAL is still TRAINED WITH forged images and masks, which define the contrastive pairs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09307)
- Kwon, Lee, Nam, Son, Kim. "SAFIRE: Segment Any Forged Image Region." arXiv:2412.08197 (Dec 2024). AAAI 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Each prompt point segments the source region that contains it, which partitions an image into multiple source regions ("for the first time"). It focuses on "uniform characteristics within each source region." It is supervised on forged datasets and is not real-only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08197)
- Giakoumoglou & Papadopoulos. "DiffusionPrint: Learning Generative Fingerprints for Diffusion-Based Inpainting Localization." arXiv:2604.12443 (14 Apr 2026). **Closest prior work to (a)+(b).** Motivation: full-regeneration inpainting re-decodes the whole image through a latent decoder and destroys camera noise. Method: patch-level MoCo contrastive learning, using "inpainted regions generated by the same model share a consistent generative fingerprint" as the self-supervisory signal, with cross-category hard negatives and a generator-aware classification head. It is used as a secondary modality in TruFor, MMFusion, and a lightweight fusion baseline, with gains of up to +28% on unseen mask types and generalization to unseen generators. Differences from (a): it trains on generator-inpainted data with generator labels, uses a CNN backbone rather than frozen foundation features, uses no majority/reference pairwise scoring, and fuses at the feature level inside a supervised IFL model rather than per image at the score level. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)

**Real-only or self-generated-fake detectors (mostly image-level)**
- Cozzolino, Poggi, Nießner, Verdoliva. "Zero-Shot Detection of AI-Generated Images" (ZED). arXiv:2409.15875 (Sep 2024). ECCV 2024 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). A lossless-coding model of real images measures per-pixel "surprise" (entropy). It needs only real images and averages more than 3% accuracy gain over SOTA. It is an image-level detector. Per-pixel surprisal could in principle be mapped, but the abstract does not claim localization. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15875)
- Ricker, Lukovnikov, Fischer. "AEROBLADE: Training-Free Detection of Latent Diffusion Images Using Autoencoder Reconstruction Error." arXiv:2401.17879 (Jan 2024). CVPR 2024 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). LDM-generated images are reconstructed by the AE more accurately than real images. It is training-free, and the abstract says the error map "can be leveraged for identifying inpainted regions" (qualitative only). This is the closest precedent for using VAE round-trips as a cue. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17879)
- Guillaro, Zingarini, Usman, Sud, Cozzolino, Verdoliva. "A Bias-Free Training Paradigm for More General AI-generated Image Detection" (B-Free). arXiv:2412.17671 (Dec 2024). CVPR 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Fakes are generated FROM real images through Stable Diffusion conditioning (self-conditioned inpainting/regeneration), so real and fake are semantically aligned. It uses content augmentation and is evaluated on 27 generators including FLUX and SD3.5. It is a detector, not a localizer. Its data recipe (self-made fakes from real images through SD autoencoders) is the closest training-data analogue to (a). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17671)
- Park & Owens. "Community Forensics: Using Thousands of Generators to Train Fake Image Detectors." arXiv:2411.04125 (Nov 2024). CVPR 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). 2.7M images from 4,803 generators. Detection improves with the number and diversity of generators. It trains on generated images, is image-level, and does not localize. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04125)
- Cozzolino, Poggi, Verdoliva. "Understanding Why Foundation Models Work for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection." arXiv:2608.12155 (12 Aug 2026). DDIM-inversion copies of real images show that foundation-model detectors use non-semantic low-to-mid-frequency cues, and that regenerated images have lower latent variance and effective dimensionality. This supports using frozen foundation features to detect regenerated cells. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12155)
- Zhao et al. "Learning Universal Features for Generalizable Image Forgery Localization" (GIFL). arXiv:2504.07462 (Apr 2025). Learns "general features from the pristine content rather than traces of specific forgeries" to localize unseen forgeries, and builds a dataset of deep-generative edits. From the abstract it is unclear whether training avoids masks; it appears supervised. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07462)
- Not checked this session: LaRE^2 (CVPR 2024) and DRCT (ICML 2024). From prior knowledge both are supervised AIGI detectors, and DRCT uses diffusion reconstructions of real images as hard negatives. Neither localizes. Their IDs and venues were not verified here.

### Inferences
- The core elements of (a) all have precedents individually: patch-pair same/different learning (Huh 2018, Forensic Similarity 2019), real-only training (Noiseprint, EXIF-as-language), clustering or community partitioning at test time (EXIF-as-language, FOCAL, Forensic Similarity Graphs), self-made fakes from real images via SD autoencoders (B-Free, and DRCT per prior knowledge), and VAE reconstruction as a regeneration cue (AEROBLADE). The novelty claim therefore has to rest on the specific COMBINATION and on the target setting (full regeneration and instruction edits), not on any one ingredient.
- DiffusionPrint (Apr 2026) is the main threat to novelty. It already argues that camera-noise methods fail under full regeneration, learns a self-supervised generative fingerprint, and fuses it into TruFor and MMFusion. (a) has to be positioned as follows: no generator-labelled inpainting data, frozen foundation plus residual features, a comparator with majority/reference scoring, and score-level per-image fusion.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains a pairwise same-source comparator using only VAE round-trips through MULTIPLE diffusion autoencoders as the "different source" class. This was not exhaustively searched beyond arXiv.
- Venues were not re-verified for FOCAL, SISL, or MMFusion. LaRE^2 and DRCT were not verified at all.

## Q2. Which papers evaluate localization on fully regenerated or instruction/LLM-edited images, and how do supervised SOTA localizers perform there? Any evidence that real-only or consistency methods win?

### Takeaway
Several 2024-2026 benchmarks report that classic supervised IFL methods (TruFor and others) collapse on fully regenerated (FR) and instruction-edited images. Examples: TGIF (2024), TGIF2 (Mar 2026), AIForge-Doc v1/v2 (2026), LocateEdit-Bench (Feb 2026), PromptForge-350k (Mar 2026), the PIXAR taxonomy benchmark (Mar 2026), and a GPT-Image-2 / Nano-Banana-2 detection benchmark (Aug 2026). No paper found shows a real-only or consistency method WINNING at localization on full regeneration. The successful FR localizers are either supervised on FR data (fine-tuned IFL, FLAME, ICL-Net, MLLM-based) or proactive watermarks (APT).

### Cited Findings
- Mareen, Karageorgiou, Van Wallendael, Lambert, Papadopoulos. "TGIF: Text-Guided Inpainting Forgery Dataset." arXiv:2407.11566 (Jul 2024). About 75k forgeries from SD2, SDXL, and Adobe Firefly, in both spliced and fully regenerated variants. Finding: traditional IFL "can detect spliced images, [but] fail to detect regenerated inpainted images." SID methods detect FR images but cannot localize. Both fail under stronger compression and WEBP. Venue not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11566)
- Mareen, Karageorgiou, Giakoumoglou, Lambert, Papadopoulos, Van Wallendael. "TGIF2: Extended Text-Guided Inpainting Forgery Dataset & Benchmark." arXiv:2603.28613 (30 Mar 2026). Adds FLUX.1 edits and random non-semantic masks. Findings: both IFL and SID degrade on FLUX.1. Fine-tuning IFL on FR images improves FR localization, but the random masks reveal "object bias" (models learn object priors, not traces). Generative super-resolution significantly weakens forensic traces. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- Ha, Kim, Jeon, Son, Yoon. "APT: Anchor-aligned Perturbations for Tamper Localization in Fully Regenerated Images." arXiv:2608.30656 (31 Aug 2026). Proactive approach (a latent perturbation embedded before distribution). Its FR IoU is 0.92 versus 0.84 for WAM, and existing proactive methods "collapse to near-random performance (AUC 0.5)" under FR. It is proactive, not passive, so it is not comparable to (a), but it confirms that FR destroys background signals. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30656)
- Wu, Zhou, Xu, Liang, Ren et al. "AIForge-Doc: A Benchmark for Detecting AI-Forged Tampering in Financial and Form Documents." arXiv:2602.20569 (24 Feb 2026). 4,061 documents edited with Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano-Banana) and Ideogram v2 Edit. TruFor AUC is 0.751 zero-shot, against 0.96 on NIST16. DocTamper AUC is 0.563 with pixel IoU 0.020. A GPT-4o judge scores 0.509. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20569)
- Wu, Zhou, Ng et al. "When the Forger Is the Judge: GPT-Image-2 Cannot Recognize Its Own Faked Documents" (AIForge-Doc v2). arXiv:2604.25213 (28 Apr 2026). 3,066 GPT-Image-2 document forgeries with masks. TruFor AUC falls from 0.962 on same-domain cross-camera splicing to 0.599 on GPT-Image-2 inpainting. DocTamper falls from 0.852 to 0.585. Humans score 0.501 in 2AFC, and the GPT-Image-2 self-judge's AUC never exceeds 0.59. This is the cleanest controlled evidence that TruFor's noise cues work for splicing but fail for regeneration. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25213)
- Wu, Li, Li, Liu, Wang. "LocateEdit-Bench: A Benchmark for Instruction-Based Editing Localization." arXiv:2602.05577 (5 Feb 2026). 231K images edited by four instruction-editing models across three edit types, with two multi-metric protocols. It states that existing AIGC localization methods "primarily focus on inpainting-based manipulations, making them ineffective against" instruction-based editing. Per-method numbers are not in the abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577)
- Wang, Wang, Chen, Zeng, Qin, Yang, Ba. "PromptForge-350k: A Large-Scale Dataset and Contrastive Framework for Prompt-Based AI Image Forgery Localization." arXiv:2603.29386 (31 Mar 2026). Covers four prompt-based editors, with masks auto-annotated by keypoint alignment and semantic similarity. ICL-Net (triple-stream backbone plus intra-image contrastive learning) reaches 62.5% IoU, 5.1 points above SOTA, with an IoU drop of less than 1% under degradations. On unseen editors it averages only 41.5% IoU. It is supervised but uses an intra-image contrastive (consistency) loss. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
- Shang, Tang, Cui et al. "From Masks to Pixels and Meaning: A New Taxonomy, Benchmark, and Metrics for VLM Image Tampering" (PIXAR). arXiv:2603.20193 (20 Mar 2026). Argues that object masks misalign with the true edit signal: many in-mask pixels are untouched, while off-mask changes count as "natural." It provides per-pixel tamper maps and finds that mask-only metrics over- and under-score strong detectors, with failures on micro-edits and off-mask changes. This matters for scoring instruction edits, which regenerate the whole image. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20193)
- Wang, Wu, Li, Chen, Zhang, Ji. "Order within Chaos: Capturing Intrinsic Energy Anomalies for AI-Manipulated Image Forgery Localization" (FLAME). arXiv:2606.02178 (1 Jun 2026). Argues that diffusion suppresses local high-frequency variance, creating an "energy gap" against optical imaging. It uses a LAD map, a SAM adapter, and an EditStream instruction-based training-data pipeline. SOTA on AI-generated forgery sets. It is supervised but its cue is physics-like. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)
- Zhang, Yu, Guan, Tian, Zhang, Zhu. "A Benchmark Dataset for MLLM-Generated Image Detection: GPT Image2 & Nano Banana2." arXiv:2608.01258 (2 Aug 2026). Covers direct generation, reference-based reconstruction (full regeneration of a real image), and local editing. Shows existing detectors degrade, and proposes the SAP-DSP baseline. This is image-level detection, not localization. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01258)
- Cai et al. "Zooming In on Fakes: ... BR-Gen ... NFA-ViT." arXiv:2504.11922 (Apr 2025). 150k locally forged images including scene edits (sky, ground). NFA-ViT uses noise fingerprints to find heterogeneous regions. Supervised. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11922)
- GuangJian Team. "Venus-DeFakerOne: Unified Fake Image Detection & Localization." arXiv:2605.14091 (13 May 2026). Based on InternVL2 and SAM2. Reports SOTA on 39 detection and 9 localization benchmarks and robustness to GPT-Image-2. Supervised and data-centric. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14091)
- MLLM-based localizers (all supervised with masks): FakeShield, Xu et al., arXiv:2410.02761 (Oct 2024), ICLR 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02761). SIDA / SID-Set, Huang et al., arXiv:2412.04292 (Dec 2024), with 300K images including fully synthetic and tampered, CVPR 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04292). LEGION / SynthScars, Kang et al., arXiv:2503.15264 (Mar 2025), with 12,236 fully synthetic images carrying artifact masks; it beats the second-best expert by +3.31 mIoU and +7.75 F1 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15264).
- Edit-corpus resources that could serve as test sets (no forensic masks): Pico-Banana-400K, built with Nano-Banana edits of real OpenImages photos, arXiv:2510.19808 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19808); GPT-IMAGE-EDIT-1.5M, arXiv:2507.21033 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21033).

### Inferences
- The failure is consistently documented: noise-trace localizers such as TruFor work on spliced and pasted-back edits but fall toward chance on FR and instruction edits. The AIForge-Doc v2 controlled numbers give AUC 0.96 to 0.60.
- A real-only comparator assumes some regions differ in source. Under true full regeneration every pixel passes through the VAE, so the "majority = real" premise is weakened. The usable signal is then generated-content versus re-encoded-content differences: AEROBLADE-style error, ZED surprisal, and the FLAME energy gap. TGIF2's object-bias finding suggests that a trace-based (non-semantic) real-only method could have an advantage on random non-semantic masks. This is untested.
- No evidence found that real-only or consistency methods beat supervised ones on FR localization. This is an open empirical question and a potential contribution.

### Gaps
- Per-method localization numbers (F1/IoU for TruFor, Mesorch, etc.) on LocateEdit-Bench, TGIF2, and PromptForge were not extracted; only the abstracts were read.
- No paper found that evaluates Huh/EXIF-SC, FOCAL, SAFIRE, or EXIF-as-language specifically on TGIF-FR or instruction-edit sets.
- No dedicated localization benchmark for FLUX.1 Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit, or Nano-Banana natural-photo edits was confirmed by name. LocateEdit-Bench and PromptForge do not name their four editors in their abstracts.

## Q3. Fusion and ensembling of forgery localizers: reported gains, and is fusion with real-only cues studied?

### Takeaway
Fusion is studied mainly at two levels. Within-model feature fusion of forensic filters or modalities is common (MMFusion, TruFor's RGB+Noiseprint++, Mesorch's CNN+Transformer, DiffusionPrint as an added modality). Learned fusion of the OUTPUT maps of several localizers is rarer (Charitidis et al. 2021). Expert selection by confidence appears in 2026 (RiSE, for text images). No paper found does per-image score-level fusion of modern supervised localizers (TruFor, Mesorch) with a real-only comparator or physics cue for FR or instruction edits. DiffusionPrint is the closest, but it does feature-level fusion of a self-supervised generative cue.

### Cited Findings
- Charitidis, Kordopatis-Zilos, Papadopoulos, Kompatsiaris. "Operation-wise Attention Network for Tampering Localization Fusion." arXiv:2105.05515 (May 2021). A deep network that combines the OUTPUT maps of multiple forensic algorithms into one fused map, with no expert knowledge needed. This is the closest precedent for map-level fusion of localizers. It predates TruFor and Mesorch. Venue not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05515)
- Siopi, Kordopatis-Zilos, Charitidis, Kompatsiaris, Papadopoulos. "A Multi-Stream Fusion Network for Image Splicing Localization." arXiv:2212.01128 (Dec 2022). Processes RGB in parallel with handcrafted forensic signals. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01128)
- Triaridis, Tsigos, Mezaris. "MMFusion: Combining Image Forensic Filters for Visual Manipulation Detection and Localization." arXiv:2312.01790 (Dec 2023). Shows that SRM, Bayar, Noiseprint++, and other filters reveal complementary manipulation types. Compares late fusion with early fusion and adopts early fusion plus feature re-weighting. Outperforms SOTA across image and video datasets. Explainability analysis gives per-filter contributions by manipulation type. Venue not verified (an earlier version appeared in a multimedia conference per prior knowledge). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01790)
- Zhu, Ma, Su et al. "Mesoscopic Insights: Orchestrating Multi-scale & Hybrid Architecture for Image Manipulation Localization" (Mesorch). arXiv:2412.13753 (Dec 2024). AAAI 2025 (venue from prior knowledge, not re-checked). Parallel Transformer (macro/semantic) and CNN (micro/low-level) branches across scales. SOTA on four datasets in performance, compute, and robustness. Supervised. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13753)
- Shuai et al. "Morphology-optimized Multi-Scale Fusion: Combining Local Artifacts and Mesoscopic Semantics for Deepfake Detection and Localization." arXiv:2509.13776 (Sep 2025). Separate local and global mask predictions fused by morphological operations. It notes that naive fusion of the two branches "can amplify noise and errors." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13776)
- Giakoumoglou & Papadopoulos. DiffusionPrint, arXiv:2604.12443 (Apr 2026). The self-supervised generative fingerprint is added as a secondary modality to TruFor, MMFusion, and a light fusion baseline, giving up to +28% on unseen mask types. This is the only found case of fusing a self-supervised (non-mask-trained) regeneration cue with supervised IFL. It is feature-level, not per-image score-level. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)
- Wong, Li, Chen, Wu, Zhou. "RiSE: Residual Subspace Expert for Generalizable Text-Centric Image Forgery Localization." arXiv:2610.24480 (17 Oct 2026). Uses forgery-type-specific residual experts, each trained on one forgery type. Latent Perturbation Confidence picks the most confident expert per image in one forward pass. One expert gives +20.4% on real-world forgeries, and nine experts give +35.8%. Cross-domain localization improves as experts are added. This is per-image expert selection, which is closely related to (b), but it is limited to document/text images and uses supervised experts only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24480)
- Qu, Zhong, Guo, Jin. "Omni-IML: Towards Unified Image Manipulation Localization." arXiv:2411.14823 (Nov 2024). A Modal Gate Encoder "adaptively selects the optimal encoding modality per sample," which is per-sample gating inside one model. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14823)
- Benchmarks: IMDL-BenCo, Ma et al., arXiv:2406.10580 (Jun 2024), with 8 models, 15 metrics, and 3 robustness tests — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10580). ForensicHub, Du et al., arXiv:2505.11003 (May 2025), unifying Deepfake, IMDL, AIGC, and Doc with 10 baselines — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11003). The abstracts mention no ensemble or late-fusion baselines. Whether their full papers report ensembles was not checked.

### Inferences
- Reported fusion gains are mostly within-architecture: MMFusion relative to single filters, and DiffusionPrint's +28% on unseen masks. Output-level fusion of independently trained SOTA localizers has not been benchmarked on modern AIGC or instruction-edit sets in any work found.
- Per-image routing or selection exists (RiSE confidence-based expert selection; Omni-IML modal gate), but nothing found routes between supervised localizers and real-only/physics cues according to whether the image looks spliced or fully regenerated.

### Gaps
- Did not find a paper that ensembles TruFor with Mesorch (or other public checkpoints) at score level and reports gains. This may exist in challenge reports or workshops not indexed by these searches.
- Dempster–Shafer and other decision-fusion frameworks in forensics before 2018 are out of scope and were not covered.

## Q4. What remains open or novel?

### Takeaway
Plausible novelty lies in the combination and the setting: (1) a pairwise same-source comparator on FROZEN foundation plus noise-residual cell features, trained only on self-made splices of real photos where the "other source" is VAE round-trips through MULTIPLE diffusion autoencoders plus real inpainting and processing, with no real manipulated image or mask; (2) majority/reference scoring for localization; (3) evaluation on FR and instruction-edited sets (TGIF2, LocateEdit-Bench, PromptForge, AIForge-Doc v2, PIXAR) against TruFor and Mesorch; (4) per-image score-level fusion or routing between supervised localizers and the real-only/physics cue. Each ingredient has prior art; the specific assembly was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Real-only localizers exist, but only for camera/EXIF traces, which full regeneration destroys: Huh 2018, Noiseprint, EXIF-as-language. — [Huh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04096); [EXIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04647); [Noiseprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08396)
- Self-made fakes from real images via SD autoencoders have been used only for image-level detection (B-Free). — [B-Free](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17671)
- The closest 2026 competitor is DiffusionPrint: a self-supervised generative fingerprint fused into TruFor and MMFusion. — [DiffusionPrint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)
- Supervised SOTA is documented to fail on FR and AI edits (TruFor 0.962 → 0.599 AUC). — [AIForge-Doc v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25213); [TGIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11566); [TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- Fine-tuning on FR introduces object bias, which motivates trace-based cues free of semantic priors. — [TGIF2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)

### Inferences
- The strongest novelty claims: (i) no mask or manipulated-image supervision, yet localization on FR and instruction edits; (ii) using the multi-autoencoder VAE round-trip as the "foreign source" for pair training, which differs from DiffusionPrint's generator-labelled inpaints and from AEROBLADE's training-free error; (iii) showing complementarity with TruFor and Mesorch through per-image fusion, measured on random non-semantic masks (TGIF2) so that object bias is controlled.
- Main risks: (1) the majority assumption breaks when edits cover large areas, and under FR the "real" majority is itself VAE-decoded; (2) Siamese comparators are vulnerable to synthetic-trace attacks (Fang & Stamm 2022); (3) reviewers will cite DiffusionPrint, B-Free, AEROBLADE, Forensic Similarity, and EXIF-as-language as partial anticipations.
