# Optics- and Geometry-Based Forgery Detection/Localization (2016-2026): Novelty Check for a Thin-Lens Defocus-vs-Depth Consistency Test

Scope note: the searches used the arXiv API (title/abstract boolean queries, 2016-2026, cs.CV/eess.IV), web search, and full-text reads of the key papers (GFrame arXiv 2607.18040 HTML; Sarkar et al. arXiv 2311.17138 via ar5iv). The ScienceDirect and Springer pages were paywalled or returned 403, so details for those papers come only from search snippets and are marked as such. About 20 tool calls in total. "Not found" means not found by these searches, not proof that no such work exists.

## Q1. Blur/defocus inconsistency and depth-of-field consistency for forgery localization since 2016. Does any work explicitly fit a thin-lens blur-depth relation?

### Takeaway
Blur-based forensics is a long-standing line of work. It covers defocus-blur consistency (about 2008), blur-type inconsistency (Bahrami, Kot et al. 2013/2015), and natural-vs-artificial defocus blur (about 2020/21). In the generative era there is one defocus-map deepfake detector (Jeon & Woo, arXiv 2025). None of the works found fits an explicit thin-lens relation (blur radius ~ a|1/d - 1/d_f| + b) against depth from a monocular depth foundation model to localize edits. On that specific formulation the idea appears unpublished.

### Cited Findings
- **Classical predecessor (pre-2016, context):** the IEEE paper "Digital Image Forgery Detection Based on the Consistency of Defocus Blur" (about 2008, IIH-MSP era) assumes that image patches at similar distances from the lens have similar blur kernel sizes. It estimates local blur at edge pixels to expose inconsistencies, tested on tampered images from real legal cases. This is the closest conceptual ancestor, but it has no depth estimate, so it can only compare blur between patches that are assumed to be at equal depth. — [IEEE Xplore 4604037](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4604037/) (authors not verified in this session)
- **Blur-type inconsistency (context, 2015):** K. Bahrami, A. C. Kot, L. Li, H. Li, "Blurred Image Splicing Localization by Exposing Blur Type Inconsistency," IEEE TIFS, 2015. The method splits the image into blocks, estimates local blur kernels, classifies blocks as out-of-focus vs motion blur, and refines region boundaries. It tests blur *type*, not blur *magnitude vs depth*. — [NTU DR-NTU record](https://dr.ntu.edu.sg/handle/10220/38453); [CityU Scholars](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/blurred-image-splicing-localization-by-exposing-blur-type-inconsi/); earlier ChinaSIP 2015 version "Image splicing localization based on blur type inconsistency" — [IEEE 7168815](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/7168815)
- A citing paper reports that the blur-type method reached 94% accuracy on 1200 samples. This figure is second-hand and was not checked against the original. — [search snippet via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275102588_Blurred_Image_Splicing_Localization_by_Exposing_Blur_Type_Inconsistency)
- **Blur-type follow-up (2017):** "Detecting blurred image splicing using blur type inconsistency," Int. J. Innovative Computing and Applications (IJICA), 2017, DOI 10.1504/IJICA.2017.082495. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1504/IJICA.2017.082495)
- **Natural vs artificial defocus blur (Springer LNCS, about 2020/21):** "Defocused Image Splicing Localization by Distinguishing Multiple Cues between Raw Naturally Blur and Artificial Blur," DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-69449-4_12 (a 2020-21 Springer volume; IWDW 2020 proceedings is likely but not verified). According to search snippets it argues that existing methods fail on images that already contain defocus inconsistency, and proposes a per-pixel classifier separating raw (optical) blur from artificial blur. It uses no depth or optics model. — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-69449-4_12) (paywalled; details from snippet only)
- Prior work on artificially blurred splice boundaries notes that forgers typically blur splice edges with a radius of 2-3 px. — [ScienceDirect, "Detect image splicing with artificial blurred boundary"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0895717711003670) (2011, context)
- **Generative era:** Minsun Jeon, Simon S. Woo, "Seeing Through the Blur: Unlocking Defocus Maps for Deepfake Detection," arXiv:2509.23289 (Sept 2025; venue not stated on arXiv). The method builds a defocus blur map and uses it as a discriminative feature for real vs synthetic or face-manipulated content. Its argument is that synthetic images lack realistic depth-of-field characteristics, and that defocus blur is depth-dependent and physically interpretable. It is image-level detection with a learned classifier on the defocus map. It does not localize edits and does not fit a thin-lens depth relation. Code: github.com/irissun9602/Defocus-Deepfake-Detection. — [arXiv 2509.23289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23289)
- A search summary reports that Jeon & Woo find fake defocus maps show "significantly higher local variance" than real ones. This is a preprint claim and was not checked in the PDF. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23289)
- The thin-lens relation itself (circle of confusion depends only on depth D and focus distance F) is standard in depth-from-defocus work, not forensics. Examples are CVPRW 2021 "Uniting Stereo and Depth-from-Defocus: A Thin Lens-based Variational Framework" and ICCV 2023 "Lens Parameter Estimation for Realistic Depth of Field Modeling," which explicitly enforces consistency between estimated defocus blur, lens parameters and a depth map, but for rendering/estimation rather than forensics. — [CVPRW 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/DiffCVML/papers/Friedlander_Uniting_Stereo_and_Depth-From-Defocus_A_Thin_Lens-Based_Variational_Framework_for_CVPRW_2021_paper.pdf); [ICCV 2023, Piché-Meunier et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Piche-Meunier_Lens_Parameter_Estimation_for_Realistic_Depth_of_Field_Modeling_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- A US patent describes a "focal stack camera as secure imaging device and image manipulation detection method." This is an active-capture route, not passive single-image analysis. — [USPTO 12190533](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12190533)
- An arXiv boolean query (defocus OR depth-of-field OR blur) AND (forgery OR splicing OR manipulation) AND depth, 2016-2026, returned 16 hits. The only forensic one was Jeon & Woo 2025; the rest were refocusing, bokeh generation and DoF-NeRF. — [arXiv search](https://arxiv.org/a/) (query run via arXiv API; representative hits: [2509.23289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23289), [2509.26599 DiffCamera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26599), [2306.08251 GBSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08251))

### Inferences
- The proposed contribution (per-region test of whether measured defocus agrees with a thin-lens prediction from monocular depth, with a and d_f fitted robustly per image, and residuals giving a localization map) appears novel. Its closest relatives are the 2008 defocus-consistency paper (no depth), Bahrami/Kot (blur type, no depth), and Jeon & Woo 2025 (learned defocus-map classifier, detection only).
- Generative tools that synthesize bokeh (GBSD, DiffCamera) produce DoF that a learned detector may not flag. An explicit thin-lens residual is the cue that such tools would need to model correctly, which is a useful framing for motivation and for adversarial evaluation.
- Practical risk: a single thin-lens fit per image breaks down for all-in-focus phone images (a ≈ 0, no signal), synthetic portrait-mode blur, and tilt-shift. The test only carries information when there is measurable DoF.

### Gaps
- The full text of the 2008 defocus-consistency paper and the 2020/21 Springer chapter could not be read (paywalled), so quantitative results and exact author lists are unverified.
- Jeon & Woo's datasets, AUC numbers and venue were not extracted.
- No dedicated search was run on CNKI or Chinese-language journals, where blur-forensics variants are common.

## Q2. Monocular depth/normal foundation models as auxiliary inputs to forgery localizers, and how this differs from an explicit physical-consistency test

### Takeaway
Since 2025-26 a few papers have fed monocular depth (and normals) as an extra learned stream into IML networks. GFrame (arXiv 2607.18040, 2026) uses MoGe-2/Metric3D depth and normals with reliability gating, and DR-Net (Neurocomputing 2026) uses a dual-backbone RGB plus depth design. Face-video work uses depth attention. None of these tests a physical law. Depth is just another input channel for a supervised segmenter, and none couples depth with defocus or any other optical measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **GFrame:** Guofeng Yu, Zhiqing Guo, Dan Ma, Gaobo Yang, "When 2D Cues Fail: Improving Image Manipulation Localization with Reliable 3D Geometry," arXiv:2607.18040 (July 2026; venue not stated). It uses monocular reconstruction to obtain depth and surface normals and argues that reconstructed geometry on manipulated images is noisy. It therefore estimates reliability and fuses the geometry selectively with RGB features, propagated across scales. — [arXiv 2607.18040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18040)
  - Geometry backend: MoGe-2 by default, with Metric3D as an ablation alternative. MoGe-2 adds about 330M parameters. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
  - Reliability cues are hand-designed: depth confidence C_d = exp(-|∇²D|), normal confidence C_n = exp(-| ||N|| - 1 |), and depth-normal agreement A_dn = (N_p·N_d + 1)/2. Fusion is Z = F_rgb + G ⊙ (w_d F_d + w_n F_n) with a learned sigmoid gate. The authors call these practical cues, not calibrated uncertainty. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
  - There is no explicit physical test such as defocus vs depth. Physical cues (JPEG, CFA, lens distortion, lighting/shadow) appear only in its related-work discussion. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
  - Protocol and results: trained on CASIAv2 plus Coverage/NIST16 train splits, evaluated with F1@0.5. In-distribution average 0.592 at 20 epochs and 0.621 at 50 epochs (CASIAv1 0.627, Coverage 0.592, NIST16 0.556), vs the best baseline Mesorch at 0.565. OOD average (Columbia, In-the-Wild, Korus, CocoGlide, IMD2020) 0.490 at 20 epochs, with Columbia 0.797 and IMD2020 0.528. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
  - JPEG robustness on CASIAv1: average F1 0.372 vs IML-ViT 0.370 and Mesorch 0.347. Q=100 gives 0.537 and Q=50 gives 0.225. The geometry stream gives essentially no robustness gain under compression. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
  - The authors state that reconstructed geometry "has not been systematically explored for IML." Stated limitations: single-view reconstruction, indirect 3D representation, and noisy geometry on manipulated images. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
- **DR-Net:** "DR-Net: Depth-assisted refinement network for image manipulation localization," Neurocomputing (2026), ScienceDirect PII S0925231226011732. According to the search snippet, the RGB image and its depth map go through two parallel, non-weight-sharing Scale-aware Modulation Transformer backbones. A depth fusion module applies stage-wise attention, with boundary supervision on depth features. Authors, depth model and numbers were not retrievable (403). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925231226011732)
- **Face video:** "Exploring Depth Information for Detecting Manipulated Face Videos," arXiv:2411.18572 (2024). It estimates face depth from RGB and fuses it with backbone features through multi-head depth attention. This is detection of face forgeries, not general IML. — [arXiv 2411.18572](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18572v1)
- **Related cross-modal video work:** "CAM-VFD: Cross-Attention Multimodal Video Forgery Detection" (arXiv:2605.17133, 2026) fuses appearance, geometry and motion modalities for video forgery detection. — [arXiv 2605.17133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17133)
- **Generic "physical descriptors" for AIGI detection:** Qiu, Zhao, Qu, "Beyond Semantics: Uncovering the Physics of Fakes via Universal Physical Descriptors for Cross-Modal Synthetic Detection," arXiv:2604.04608 (2026). It selects 5 of 15 "physical" features (Laplacian variance, Sobel statistics, residual-noise variance, and others), converts them into text for CLIP, and reports 99.8% on GenImage Wukong/SDv1.4. Despite the name, these are image statistics, not optics or geometry laws, and the task is detection only. — [arXiv 2604.04608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04608)

### Inferences
- Key difference to emphasize: GFrame and DR-Net learn to use depth. Their gains come from supervision and they inherit dataset bias (training on CASIAv2). A thin-lens defocus residual is a training-free, interpretable hypothesis test. It produces a calibrated per-region anomaly score from physics, and could then be used as an extra input channel or as a prior for such networks.
- GFrame's depth-normal agreement cue A_dn is the closest thing to an internal-consistency check in this line, but it checks the depth estimator against itself, not against an independent optical measurement.
- GFrame's collapse under JPEG (Q50: 0.225) shows that depth streams do not buy robustness by themselves. A defocus-depth test may degrade differently, because blur radii of a few pixels survive moderate JPEG but are biased by resize and down-sampling. This needs measuring.

### Gaps
- The DR-Net full text (authors, depth estimator used, numbers) was not accessible.
- No paper was found that uses Depth Anything, Marigold, ZoeDepth or DPT specifically inside an IML/forensic pipeline. GFrame uses MoGe-2/Metric3D. More may exist under other keywords such as "geometry prior" or "3D-aware forensics."

## Q3. Chromatic aberration forensics after Johnson & Farid 2006 / Gloe 2010 / Yerushalmy & Hel-Or 2011: deep-learning-era revival? Does it work on generated or regenerated images?

### Takeaway
The main post-2016 contribution is Mayer & Stamm (IEEE TIFS 2018), a statistical local-vs-global lateral-CA (LCA) model for forgery localization. LCA anti-forensics (falsifying CA) also exists. Deep-learning work is limited to a 2025 FCN for detecting CA itself, not forensics on generated content. No paper was found that applies LCA consistency to localize diffusion or LLM-instruction edits, or that tests whether generators reproduce radially consistent LCA.

### Cited Findings
- O. Mayer, M. C. Stamm, "Accurate and Efficient Image Forgery Detection Using Lateral Chromatic Aberration," IEEE TIFS, 2018. It proposes a statistical model that detects tampered regions through inconsistencies between global and local LCA estimates. (Volume/issue/DOI quoted from memory as TIFS vol. 13, 2018, DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2018.2799421, not re-verified in this session.) — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322779654_Accurate_and_Efficient_Image_Forgery_Detection_Using_Lateral_Chromatic_Aberration)
- A 2025 AIGI-detection review lists chromatic aberration (Mayer & Stamm 2018) among the handcrafted spatial features used by early GAN-image detection frameworks. — [arXiv 2502.15176 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15176)
- Anti-forensics: "Anti-forensics of chromatic aberration" (about 2015, Mayer & Stamm) describes how to falsify the LCA in an image, so LCA cues can be forged deliberately. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282379864_Anti-forensics_of_chromatic_aberration)
- A deep-learning CA detector: "Chromatic Aberration Detection Using Fully Convolutional Networks" (Springer, 2025; DOI prefix 10.1007/978-981-96-6008-7_16) reports accuracy similar to state-of-the-art CA identification while running much faster. It is not aimed at AI-generated images. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-6008-7_16)
- An adjacent but different cue: CHROMA (arXiv:2606.08864, 2026) detects AI-generated images through inter-channel color-space correlations (RGB/Lab). These are channel statistics, not a lens-geometric radial CA model. — [arXiv 2606.08864](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08864v1)
- Classical baseline (context): Johnson & Farid, "Exposing digital forgeries through chromatic aberration," ACM MM&Sec 2006. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220942108_Exposing_digital_forgeries_through_chromatic_aberration)

### Inferences
- Diffusion inpainting of a region in a real photo probably destroys the local LCA displacement field (the VAE decoder does not reproduce the lens's radial channel misregistration). Mayer–Stamm's local-vs-global test should therefore in principle flag diffusion-inpainted regions. This is untested in the literature found. Full regeneration (img2img/LLM-instruction editors that re-decode the whole image) would erase global LCA too, turning the test into a detection cue ("no LCA anywhere") rather than a localization cue.
- LCA is tiny (sub-pixel), so it is expected to be very fragile to resizing and JPEG at Q<90, and to be removed by in-camera CA correction in modern phones. This likely explains the lack of a deep-learning revival and is a serious practical limit.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures LCA in Stable Diffusion, FLUX or GPT-image outputs, or in inpainted regions.
- Robustness numbers for Mayer & Stamm under JPEG and resize were not extracted.
- Yerushalmy & Hel-Or 2011 and Gloe 2010 follow-ups in the deep-learning era: nothing found.

## Q4. Perspective, vanishing-point and line-geometry consistency for AI-generated or edited images

### Takeaway
Farid (2022) showed with manual analysis that DALL·E 2 images violate perspective, shadow and reflection geometry. Sarkar et al. (CVPR 2024; arXiv 2311.17138) automated this with classifiers on perspective fields, line segments and object-shadow masks, reaching AUC 0.72-0.97 for detection. Localization is only qualitative, through Grad-CAM. Generators are now being fixed to enforce vanishing-point consistency (ControlVP 2025; Upadhyay et al. 2023), which threatens the cue's longevity. No automatic per-region vanishing-point or geometry consistency localizer for local diffusion or LLM edits was found.

### Cited Findings
- H. Farid, "Perspective (In)consistency of Paint by Text," arXiv:2206.14617 (June 2022; single author, UC Berkeley). This is an initial exploration of whether DALL·E 2 3D structures, cast shadows and reflections are consistent with perspective geometry. It finds consistent geometric inconsistencies that are not always visually obvious but "should prove forensically useful." The analysis is manual and qualitative. — [arXiv 2206.14617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14617)
- A companion paper, "Lighting (In)consistency of Paint by Text," arXiv:2207.13744 (Farid, 2022). — [listing](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2207.13744v2/similar?locale=en)
- A. Sarkar, H. Mai, A. Mahapatra, S. Lazebnik, D. A. Forsyth, A. Bhattad, "Shadows Don't Lie and Lines Can't Bend! Generative Models don't know Projective Geometry...for now," arXiv:2311.17138 (CVPR 2024). Three pixel-free classifiers: (1) Perspective Fields into a ResNet50, (2) DeepLSD line segments into a PointNet-style network, (3) instance object-shadow masks into a ResNet50. — [arXiv 2311.17138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17138); details from [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)
  - Data: generators were SDXL, Kandinsky-v3 (main training source), DeepFloyd IF and PixArt-α, with transfer tests on DALL·E 3 and Firefly. Real images were about 400k LSUN indoor and 125k BDD100K/Mapillary outdoor images. Generated images were "prequalified" to fool a signal-based ResNet50. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)
  - Results: AUC 0.72-0.97 overall. On the hard set where the signal detector is at chance, perspective fields reach 0.84 outdoor and lines 0.82 indoor. Generalization to DeepFloyd is poor (0.72). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)
  - Localization: Grad-CAM saliency only, with no quantitative localization evaluation. No dedicated JPEG or resize robustness test (only 5% JPEG augmentation in training). Whole-image generations only, no local edits. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)
- Generators are being corrected: R. Okumura, K. Shiohara, T. Yamasaki, "ControlVP: Interactive Geometric Refinement of AI-Generated Images with Consistent Vanishing Points," arXiv:2512.07504 (Dec 2025), which identifies vanishing-point inconsistency in Stable Diffusion outputs and fixes it. — [arXiv 2512.07504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07504). Also R. Upadhyay et al., "Enhancing Diffusion Models with 3D Perspective Geometry Constraints," arXiv:2312.00944 (2023). — [arXiv 2312.00944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00944). And "GeoEdit: Geometry-Aware Object Editing via Dual-Branch Denoising," arXiv:2606.30003 (2026), which notes that 2D diffusion editors "produce perspective violations" when moving or scaling objects. — [arXiv 2606.30003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30003)
- Benchmarks show multimodal LLMs are weak at perspective reasoning (MMPerspective, arXiv:2505.20426, 2025). This is relevant if one considers using an MLLM as a geometry judge. — [arXiv 2505.20426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20426)

### Inferences
- Existing geometry forensics targets whole-image generation (detection). Local edits made by instruction editors (moving or inserting objects) are exactly the case where a per-region test applies: does an inserted object's lines converge to the scene's vanishing points, and is its depth-scale and defocus consistent with its ground contact? GeoEdit's admission that editors violate perspective supports this.
- The cue has a shelf life because generators are adding VP and geometry constraints (ControlVP, Upadhyay, GeoEdit). Defocus-vs-depth consistency is less targeted by current editing research, although bokeh and refocus models (DiffCamera, GBSD) exist.

### Gaps
- No quantitative localization benchmark of geometry cues on edited (not fully generated) images was found.
- Farid's later peer-reviewed geometry work (for example in Journal of Vision or PNAS, if any) was not checked.

## Q5. What remains open or novel for an automatic, per-region optics/geometry consistency test on diffusion/LLM edits and under JPEG/resize?

### Takeaway
Open: (1) an explicit thin-lens defocus-vs-monocular-depth residual for localization, with nothing found; (2) LCA local-vs-global tests evaluated on diffusion-inpainted or instruction-edited regions, with nothing found; (3) automatic per-region vanishing-point consistency for inserted or moved objects, with only image-level detection published; (4) robustness studies of any of these cues under JPEG and resize on modern edit benchmarks, with only GFrame's depth-stream JPEG table found, showing little robustness.

### Cited Findings
- The only depth-for-IML paper with JPEG numbers (GFrame) drops from F1 0.627 to 0.225 at Q50 on CASIAv1, about equal to non-geometry baselines. — [arXiv HTML 2607.18040](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18040v1)
- Geometry-based AIGI detection (Sarkar et al.) offers only qualitative Grad-CAM localization and no robustness tests. — [ar5iv 2311.17138](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17138)
- Defocus forensics in the generative era (Jeon & Woo 2025) is image-level detection with a learned classifier on defocus maps, not localization and not physics-model fitting. — [arXiv 2509.23289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23289)
- Blur-based localization faces known anti-forensics: resizing removes splicing blur traces, and existing defocus methods fail when the image already contains natural blur inconsistency. — [Springer 978-3-030-69449-4_12 (snippet)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-69449-4_12); [ResearchGate, blur-type](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269294994_Image_tampering_detection_by_exposing_blur_type_inconsistency)
- Recent AIGI manipulation-localization work (for example GAP-SAM, arXiv:2608.20929, 2026) still relies on learned artifact priors and reports that OOD pixel-level localization lags image-level detection. This is a motivation for physics-based, training-free priors. — [arXiv 2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)

### Inferences
- A defensible novelty claim: "first training-free, per-region thin-lens consistency test between measured defocus and monocular-foundation-model depth, for localizing local diffusion/LLM edits, with robustness analysis under JPEG/resize, and use as a prior or feature for learned IML." The claim should be bounded by citing the 2008 defocus-consistency paper, Bahrami/Kot, Jeon & Woo 2025, GFrame 2026 and DR-Net 2026.
- Expected weaknesses that reviewers will raise: (a) many phone and AI images are all-in-focus, so the test is uninformative there; (b) depth foundation models produce affine-invariant (relative) depth, so the fit needs a scale/shift or inverse-depth parameterization, and the edit itself corrupts the depth estimate (GFrame's point); (c) blur estimators confuse texture-less regions with blur; (d) resizing scales the blur radius uniformly, which a per-image fit of a and b absorbs, but JPEG and down-sampling erase small radii. A combined test (defocus plus perspective plus LCA) with reliability weighting is still unoccupied territory.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether any 2025-26 workshop papers (CVPR WMF, WIFS, IH&MMSec) quietly propose blur-depth consistency. These venues are poorly indexed by the search tools used.
- No benchmark containing edits with known optical ground truth (for example, inserted objects with deliberately wrong DoF) was found. A controlled synthetic benchmark would itself be a contribution.
