# Region definition for region-wise forensic consistency analysis (grids, windows, superpixels, segments, content-matched sampling)

Scope note: ~15 tool calls. Primary sources actually read in full text: Noisesniffer IPOL 2024 (pp. 86-102), Chierchia et al. ICASSP 2014 (guided filtering for PRNU). Other sources were checked at abstract/metadata level only; this is flagged where it matters. Items marked **[Inference]** are my own reasoning, not claims from the literature.

## Q1. Content dependence of forensic statistics and how methods normalise for it

### Takeaway
Noise statistics in real images depend on intensity (Poisson-Gaussian / noise level function), on frequency and scale (demosaicing, JPEG), and on local texture/sharpness, so a raw "noise level per region" mixes trace with content. The established remedies are: (a) estimate only on homogeneous / weak-texture patches (lowest low-frequency energy or lowest PCA eigenvalue), and (b) compare only within intensity (and possibly frequency/scale) bins, i.e., against a noise level function, not a single scalar.

### Cited Findings
- Raw sensor noise is well modelled as a clipped Poissonian-Gaussian process (a signal-dependent Poisson part plus a stationary Gaussian part), with parameters tied to quantum efficiency, pedestal and analog gain; parameters can be fitted from a single image. Foi, Trimeche, Katkovnik, Egiazarian, IEEE TIP 17(10):1737-1754, 2008 — [DOI 10.1109/TIP.2008.2001399](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2008.2001399); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tip/FoiTKE08)
- After the camera pipeline, noise is intensity- and frequency-dependent; Colom, Lebrun, Buades & Morel propose a nonparametric intensity-frequency-dependent noise model (>1,000 parameters), validated against ground-truth noise curves on raw and JPEG images. IEEE TIP 24(10):3162-3175, 2015 — [DOI 10.1109/TIP.2015.2438537](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2015.2438537); [author PDF](https://mcolom.perso.math.cnrs.fr/download/articles/noise_estimation_TIP2015.pdf)
- Liu, Tanaka & Okutomi select low-rank, weak-textured patches (by gradient statistics) and estimate noise by PCA on those patches; ICIP 2012 and IEEE TIP 22(12):5226-5237, 2013. A follow-up (ICIP 2013) uses the same weak-textured patches to estimate signal-dependent noise parameters. Typical implementation patch size 7 — [TIP 2013 record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_pubmed_primary_24108465); [sunkit-image implementation](https://sunkit-image.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/sunkit_image.utils.noise.noise_estimation.html); [MATLAB code](https://la.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/36921-noise-level-estimation-from-a-single-image)
- Pyatykh, Hesser & Zheng estimate noise variance as the smallest eigenvalue of the covariance of overlapping B x B blocks, iteratively choosing the subset of lowest-variance patches; reported >=15x faster than equally accurate methods. IEEE TIP 22(2):687-699, 2013 — [DOI 10.1109/TIP.2012.2221728](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2012.2221728); [IPOL analysis/extension of the PCA method](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2016/124)
- Noisesniffer's related-work survey states that earlier blind noise forensics (Mahdian & Saic MAD-wavelet blocks; Ke et al. PCA + k-means; Lyu et al. kurtosis + k-means; Liu & Pun SLIC + 5 filters + graph cut) "share the same drawback": they assume a single constant AWGN level, which does not hold because noise depends on intensity; later methods use a noise level function (NLF) instead (Yao et al. edge/non-edge NLF+CRF; Julliand et al.; Pun et al. NLF via Wiener filtering; Zhu & Zhao NLF depending on local sharpness; Gardella et al. NLF that is also scale-dependent for JPEG images) — [Gardella, Musé, Colom, Morel, IPOL 14:86-115, 2024, Sec. 2](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- Noisesniffer's normalisation recipe: discard blocks containing saturated pixels (noise is clipped); group w x w blocks per colour channel into intensity bins of B blocks; in each bin keep the n-percentile with lowest low/medium-frequency DCT energy (set L = "homogeneous" blocks), and within L the m-percentile with lowest pixel std (set V); declare a bin invalid if >m% of L is perfectly flat (JPEG-flattened). The trace statistic is then the spatial concentration of V within L, not a noise level — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 3.1](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- PRNU localisation explicitly handles content dependence via a "predictor" that estimates the expected correlation under H1 from local image features; in dark, saturated or heavily textured areas the correlation can be low without any forgery, so those pixels are declared genuine regardless — [Chierchia et al., ICASSP 2014, Sec. 2](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)
- Lyu, Pan & Zhang estimate local noise from the near-constancy of kurtosis of natural images across band-pass channels, closed-form solution, accelerated with integral images, then segment by noise variance with k-means. IJCV 110, 2014 — [DOI 10.1007/s11263-013-0688-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-013-0688-y); [PDF](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~siweilyu/papers/ijcv14.pdf) (full text not retrieved: HTTP 403)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** DT-CWT detail-band energy, wavelet sparsity, residual energy and local spectra all contain a signal term that scales with local contrast/texture; without an intensity-binned and texture-gated comparison, a "noise inconsistency" map largely becomes a texture map. This is exactly the failure mode the NLF literature was created to fix.
- **[Inference]** For a 3-channel 8-bit JPEG, conditioning on intensity alone is insufficient; JPEG quantisation makes the residual also depend on frequency band and on 8x8 alignment, so conditioning should be (intensity bin, frequency band, maybe texture class).

### Gaps
- Exact block sizes used by Pyatykh et al. and Liu et al. in the TIP experiments not verified (only implementation default 7 seen).
- No full-text verification of Lyu 2014 window sizes and its reported performance.

## Q2. Is segmenting into texture/colour-homogeneous regions sound or a confound?

### Takeaway
Segmentation is a double-edged sword. Restricting estimation to homogeneous content lowers bias and variance, but hard segmentation (colour segments, SLIC) used as the *unit of comparison* is documented as a source of error, and in generative inpainting the edited area tends to coincide with an object/segment, so segment-level differences confound semantics and texture with the trace. The literature's better answer is to use content homogeneity to *select or weight samples* (soft weights, homogeneous-block selection within intensity bins), not to define the regions being tested.

### Cited Findings
- Chierchia et al. previously used preliminary segmentation to adapt the analysis window to candidate forgeries, but "segmentation, however, is itself a source of errors, and the experimental analysis proved the heavy impact of such errors on performance"; they replace hard segmentation by soft, content-adaptive weights (guided filter with the image as pilot) — [ICASSP 2014, Sec. 1](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)
- Same paper, the bias-variance argument: with a fixed large window, mixing forged and genuine pixels makes the statistic "strongly biased and basically useless"; including only predictors homogeneous with the target removes bias "at the cost of an increased estimation variance" — [ICASSP 2014, Sec. 3](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)
- Segmentation-based noise forensics exists and reports gains: Pun, Liu & Yuan (JVCIR 38:195-206, 2016) segment into multi-scale superpixels, fit an NLF (noise vs brightness per segment), flag segments off the NLF; Liu & Pun (Neurocomputing 2020) note noise estimation is inaccurate in small segments and add adaptive-SVD estimation plus SLIC+block multi-level maps; Li et al. 2019 use SLIC + PCA noise + clustering — [Pun et al. 2016 record](https://research.mpu.edu.mo/en/publications/multi-scale-noise-estimation-for-image-splicing-forgery-detection/); [Liu & Pun 2020](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/locating-splicing-forgery-by-adaptive-svd-noise-estimation-and-vicinity-noise-descriptor/812689668121296897-2492) (abstract level only)
- Noisesniffer uses homogeneity only to choose *which blocks* enter the test, and tests *arbitrary 4-connected unions of square cells* (polyominoes, side l_beta) grown greedily, rather than predefined semantic segments; this replaced the original rectangular macro-blocks — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 3.2-3.3](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- Noisesniffer limitations that show the cost of homogeneity gating: "Since the forgery is a textured zone, finding homogeneous blocks inside the forgery is more difficult when using bigger blocks"; the method "is only able to detect forgeries having lower noise levels"; n (fraction of blocks assumed homogeneous) is capped at 0.1 because larger values cause false positives in textured images — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 5 and 5.1.1-5.1.3](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- TGIF2 adds random, non-semantic inpainting masks and reports that this "reveals object bias": fine-tuned IFL methods' gains partly depend on edited regions being objects — [Mareen et al., arXiv 2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- COCO-Inpaint is built to emphasise "intrinsic inconsistencies between inpainted and authentic regions" rather than "superficial semantic artifacts such as object shapes", with four mask-generation strategies — [arXiv 2504.18361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18361). (A search-engine summary further claimed that object masks are easier to localise than random masks and that training on random boxes generalises best; I could not verify this from the abstract.)
- Mask-shape bias (the mask outline acting as a semantic cue) is also named in object-removal work, mitigated by reshaping masks to random/rectangular shapes — [arXiv 2509.10259](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10259v1)
- The researcher's prior method segments by colour and compares DT-CWT noise across segments; the abstract gives no quantitative metrics and evaluates on a self-built dataset — [Barglazan & Brad, arXiv 2408.06429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06429)

### Inferences
- **[Inference] Confound mechanism.** Text-guided inpainting replaces an object; colour/texture segmentation tends to return that object as one segment. Any between-segment difference (texture energy, sharpness, intensity-dependent noise, generator smoothness) then "localises" the edit even when no trace is measured, and equally lights up genuine objects with different texture. This yields optimistic results on object-mask datasets and failure on random masks (consistent with TGIF2's object-bias finding).
- **[Inference] Boundary mismatch.** If the edit mask cuts through a segment (random masks, background inpainting, mask dilation by the editor), the segment mixes edited and pristine pixels; its statistic is a weighted average, giving the "strongly biased" regime Chierchia describes.
- **[Inference] Unequal variance.** Segments vary in size by orders of magnitude, so per-segment estimates have very different variance; a fixed threshold on the difference flags small segments as outliers by chance unless the test is normalised by size (e.g., a z-score with variance ~2/N per segment, or an NFA that counts tests as Noisesniffer does).
- **[Inference] Verdict.** Homogeneity is a sound *sampling/weighting* principle (compare like with like), but an unsound *hypothesis-unit* definition for generative-inpainting localisation.

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly ablates "colour-segment regions vs grid vs content-matched sampling" on the same noise statistic for diffusion inpainting.
- COCO-Inpaint mask-type numbers not verified.

## Q3. Better designs: content-matched comparison, heteroscedastic normalisation, multiscale/multi-shape windows, learned nuisance-invariant descriptors

### Takeaway
The best-supported design is: compute a local trace statistic on small units, normalise it against an image-wide model conditioned on nuisance variables (intensity bin, texture class, frequency/scale), and test candidate regions of arbitrary shape built from small cells with an explicit false-alarm control; use content only to weight samples (soft). Learned pairwise "same-source" descriptors are the learned analogue of matched-pair comparison.

### Cited Findings
- Content-matched comparison in practice: Noisesniffer compares each block only with blocks of the same channel and same intensity bin (B = 20,000 blocks/bin optimal; 40,000 nearly identical), then tests whether the lowest-noise subset is spatially uniform under a Bernoulli/binomial null — a like-with-like design that needs no explicit noise level estimate — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 3, 5.1.2](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- Arbitrary-shape regions with false-alarm control: NFA(R) = w^2 * N_tests * binomial tail, where N_tests counts all polyominoes of all sizes up to half the image (p_n ~ 0.3169*4.0626^n/n); a region is accepted if NFA < 1; candidate regions are grown greedily from seed cells whose V/L ratio exceeds m — [IPOL 2024, Eqs. 3-11](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- Soft content-adaptive windows: guided filtering with RGB pilot raised PRNU AUC from 0.63 to 0.78 (48x48 forgeries) and 0.71 to >0.85 (64x64), versus 0.85->0.90 at 96x96 and negligible gain at 128x128 — [Chierchia et al. ICASSP 2014, Sec. 4, Fig. 2](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)
- Multiscale NLF: Gardella et al. estimate an NLF at multiple scales because JPEG noise is intensity- and scale-dependent; Pun et al. use multi-scale superpixels — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 2](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462); [Pun et al. 2016](https://research.mpu.edu.mo/en/publications/multi-scale-noise-estimation-for-image-splicing-forgery-detection/)
- Learned pairwise comparison: Forensic Similarity (Mayer & Stamm, IEEE TIFS 2020) uses a Siamese network to decide whether two patches share forensic traces; forgeries are detected as communities in the patch-similarity graph — [arXiv 1902.04684](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684); [IPOL 2024, Sec. 2](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)

### Inferences
- **[Inference] Recommended pipeline for the researcher's setting.** (1) Per small cell (e.g., 8-16 px, aligned to the 8x8 JPEG grid), compute residual statistics per DT-CWT subband. (2) Fit a robust image-wide conditional model E[stat | intensity bin, texture/gradient class, subband] using only weak-texture cells (Liu/Pyatykh-style selection). (3) Convert each cell to a standardised residual (or a rank/percentile within its stratum, as Noisesniffer does). (4) Aggregate over many candidate shapes (sliding windows at several scales and aspect ratios, or region growing over cells) with a multiple-testing-aware score (NFA or permutation null). (5) Validate on random/non-semantic masks to check for object bias.
- **[Inference]** A rank-within-stratum statistic (as in Noisesniffer) is preferable to a raw level difference for generative edits, because it is invariant to monotone content-dependent scaling of the statistic and does not require knowing whether the edit raises or lowers the trace (though Noisesniffer's one-sided test only finds lower-noise regions; a two-sided version would test both tails).
- **[Inference]** Learned "nuisance-invariant" descriptors can be trained with matched pairs (same intensity/texture, different source) to force invariance; but they inherit the object-bias risk if trained on object-shaped masks (TGIF2).

### Gaps
- No primary source found that formally compares stratified (intensity x texture) normalisation against SAM/object segmentation for diffusion-inpainting localisation; this is a testable gap.

## Q4. Region size vs statistical power (sample complexity) and smallest detectable edit

### Takeaway
Second-order statistics are relatively cheap (relative SE of a variance ~ sqrt(2/N)), but higher-order statistics are expensive (kurtosis SE ~ sqrt(24/N)), and correlation-based traces like PRNU need ~128x128 windows; spatial correlation of residuals and the need to keep only homogeneous pixels reduce the effective N further. Small cells (~40-50 px) give enough pixels for variance, but only if the cell is homogeneous and the comparison is stratified; higher-order and spectral statistics per cell will be dominated by estimation noise.

### Cited Findings
- Sampling variance of excess kurtosis under normality is 24/N (exact small-sample SE formula given by SPSS/Genstat) — [semTools docs](https://rdocumentation.org/packages/semTools/versions/0.5-6/topics/kurtosis); [IBM SPSS note](https://ibm.com/support/pages/node/421769)
- PRNU: because the signal is weak, correlation is computed over ~128x128 windows, which makes forgeries much smaller than the window undetectable; the original algorithm discarded detections smaller than 64x64 (a quarter of the window) — [Chierchia et al. ICASSP 2014, Sec. 2](https://perso.esiee.fr/~chierchg/articles/2014_icassp_prnu.pdf)
- Noisesniffer: w = 3 was best (MCC 0.357 vs 0.284 at w = 5 and 0.204 at w = 8 on Trace "endomasks"); w < 3 gives too few samples to estimate low/medium-frequency variance; detection cell size l_beta = 60-100 px had little effect (larger l_beta found larger zones, smaller ones better shapes); optimal m depends on forgery size (small forgeries better with small m) — [IPOL 2024, Sec. 5.1, Tables 1-6](https://doi.org/10.5201/ipol.2024.462)
- Liu & Pun 2020 explicitly target the inaccuracy of noise estimates in small segments — [record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/locating-splicing-forgery-by-adaptive-svd-noise-estimation-and-vicinity-noise-descriptor/812689668121296897-2492)
- Learned patch comparison also loses accuracy with smaller patches: Forensic Similarity 256x256 beats 128x128 at every JPEG quality (e.g., 91.83% vs 88.63% at Q95; 74.04% vs 68.83% at Q75) — [arXiv 1902.04684](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04684)

### Inferences
- **[Inference] Back-of-envelope (assumes i.i.d. Gaussian residuals; real residuals are spatially correlated so effective N is smaller).** For a variance estimate from N samples, Var(s^2)/sigma^4 ~ 2/(N-1), so relative SE ~ sqrt(2/N): N = 1,600 (40x40) -> ~3.5%; N = 256 (16x16) -> ~8.8%; N = 64 (8x8) -> ~18%. Comparing region (N1) vs rest (N2 >> N1) on log-variance, z ~ delta / sqrt(2/N1); to detect a 10% noise-std change (delta ~ 0.2 in log-variance) at z = 3 needs N1 ~ 2*(3/0.2)^2 ~ 450 effective samples, i.e., about 21x21 i.i.d. pixels, more in practice. A 5% std change needs ~1,800.
- **[Inference]** Kurtosis: SE ~ sqrt(24/N) -> 0.12 at N = 1,600; for heavy-tailed residuals the SE is larger (depends on 8th moment). Discriminating kurtosis differences < ~0.5 needs thousands of samples per region. Bispectrum / multifractal / covariance-spectrum estimates are worse: they spread N samples over many frequency or scale bins, so per-bin variance scales with number of bins / N. I found no forensic paper quantifying these specifically (gap).
- **[Inference]** Implication for the 24x24 grid (~40-50 px cells): each cell has enough pixels for a stratified variance-type statistic, but (a) only a fraction of each cell is weak-textured, (b) generative inpainted regions are often smooth so their cells sit in different strata than the rest, (c) edges of the edit cut cells, and (d) per-cell higher-order statistics are noise-dominated. Better: smaller cells (8-16 px) with stratified ranks, aggregated over multi-scale, multi-shape candidate regions with NFA/permutation control. The smallest detectable edit is set by how many homogeneous, stratum-matched samples fall inside it, not by its pixel area.
- **[Inference]** Unequal region sizes (segments) should be compared via size-aware statistics (z-scores with variance ~ 2/N_eff, or binomial-tail NFA), never raw differences.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying sample complexity of bispectrum, multifractal exponents or covariance spectra for forensic use.
- Effective sample size under demosaicing/JPEG correlation not quantified in retrieved sources.
- Smallest-detectable-size curves for diffusion-inpainting noise forensics not found.
