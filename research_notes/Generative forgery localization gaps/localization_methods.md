# Image Forgery Localization Methods for Generative Edits (2023 – Oct 2026): Catalogue and Critical Analysis

Scope: pixel-level localization (masks) of diffusion/flow inpainting, object replacement, instruction-based and MLLM-based editing. All IDs are arXiv unless stated. Numbers are as reported by the cited paper. Where a number was read from an HTML rendering of tables, it is noted as such. "(abs)" means the claim is from the abstract only, without table-level verification.

---

## Q1. What are the main method families?

### Takeaway
Six families coexist in 2025-2026: (A) dual-stream RGB+noise/trace localizers inherited from classical IML; (B) foundation-model adapters (SAM2/SAM3, DINOv2/v3, CLIP, MAE), which now hold most 2026 SOTA claims on AIGC edits; (C) diffusion-as-localizer (the mask is generated by a conditional diffusion/score model); (D) MLLM/explainable localizers (LLM + segmentation decoder); (E) reconstruction/inversion residual methods (VAE or diffusion reconstruction error used as the forensic cue); (F) AIGC-specific trace learners (generator fingerprints, low-level energy/phase statistics). The 2026 trend is hybrids: a hand-designed generative-trace prior (LAD energy, VAE-reconstruction token, local phase, DiffusionPrint features) injected into a frozen foundation segmenter.

### Cited Findings

**(A) Dual-stream RGB + noise/trace (classical lineage; the baselines)**
- TruFor (CVPR 2023) fuses RGB with a learned Noiseprint++ fingerprint, trained self-supervised on real data only. It flags anomalies against the camera/processing pattern and outputs a localization map, an integrity score and a reliability map — [2212.10957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10957)
- PIM (Pixel-Inconsistency Modeling, Kong et al.) assumes demosaicing-induced pixel correlations that manipulations break. It uses a global masked self-attention stream plus a local stream, Learning-to-Weight modules and PIDA augmentation, and benchmarks 15 models on 12 datasets — [2310.00234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00234)
- IML-ViT is a plain high-resolution ViT with multi-scale features and edge supervision, built on the premise that artifacts are "massive at the manipulation border". Code: SunnyHaze/IML-ViT — [2307.14863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14863)
- SparseViT (AAAI 2025) replaces handcrafted noise extractors with sparse, discrete self-attention that "breaks image semantics" to force non-semantic features. It claims up to 80% fewer FLOPs — [2412.14598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14598)
- Mesorch (AAAI 2025) runs a parallel Transformer (macro/semantic) and CNN (micro/low-level) at multiple scales, a "mesoscopic" representation — [2412.13753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13753)
- SAFIRE (AAAI 2025) reframes localization as point-prompted source-region partitioning (SAM-like), so the model learns uniform characteristics within each source region rather than memorizing traces — [2412.08197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08197)
- CAT-Net and MVSS-Net are pre-2023 classics, used as baselines in TGIF2, GIFTBench and PromptForge (see below). CAT-Net relies on JPEG DCT/compression artifacts, which explains its collapse on fully regenerated images (Q4) — [TGIF2 2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- "GIFL" (named in the assignment): I could not identify a 2023-2026 arXiv paper by this exact name. See Gaps.

**(B) Foundation-model adapters (SAM / DINO / CLIP / MAE)**
- MaskCLIP / SPM (OpenSDI, CVPR 2025) aligns CLIP with MAE through prompting and attending strategies ("Synergizing Pretrained Models"). It reports +14.23% relative IoU (+14.11% F1) over the second-best model on OpenSDID localization — [2503.19653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19653)
- FLAME (Jun 2026) pairs a LAD (local absolute-difference energy) map with a parameter-efficient adapter on a frozen SAM 3 encoder. A lightweight LAD-Net CNN produces a coarse mask that is used as the SAM prompt — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)
- GAP-SAM (Aug 2026) encodes the image together with its frozen-VAE reconstruction into one "global artifact token". The token is injected into SAM3's feature pyramid with zero-gated FiLM, using LoRA rank 8 — [2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)
- ForenScope (Oct 2026, the method in the GIFTBench paper): DINOv2 ViT-L/14 with registers, LoRA-adapted for image-level classification in blocks 8-23 and then frozen. Multi-depth features (blocks 8/11/14/17) are fused at three scales with learned layer weights, followed by CLS-conditioned bounded FiLM on coarse scales — [2610.01778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01778)
- SARIF (Jun 2026) is a "Segment Anything for Robust Image Forensics" adapter that targets cross-domain generalization (abs; no numbers verified) — [2606.21108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21108)
- FASA (Apr 2026) combines an adaptive dual-band DCT module with patch-level contrastive alignment on frozen CLIP, a semantic-frequency side adapter, and a prototype-guided, frequency-gated decoder. It is evaluated on OpenSDI and traditional benchmarks (abs) — [2604.12341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12341)
- CLUE (Aug 2025) uses LoRA to repurpose generative-model internals for forgery localization (abs snippet only) — [2508.07413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07413)

**(C) Diffusion-as-localizer (mask generated by a conditional diffusion/score model)**
- InpDiffusion (Jan 2025) treats inpainting localization as conditional mask generation. Denoising is conditioned on image semantics and edges with a dual-stream multi-scale feature extractor, and the stated motivation is to reduce overconfidence and recover subtle boundaries — [2501.02816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02816)
- SDiFL (Aug 2025) builds on SD3's multimodal latent space. It treats high-pass forgery residuals as an explicit extra modality fused into the latent and claims "up to 12%" gains over SOTA, with generalization to unseen real-world document and natural forgeries (abs) — [2508.20182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20182)
- CBDiff (Oct 2025) is a conditional Bernoulli diffusion: Bernoulli rather than Gaussian noise matches binary, sparse masks, Time-Step Cross-Attention injects semantics, and the model samples multiple plausible masks to represent uncertainty. It is evaluated on 8 public benchmarks (abs) — [2510.19597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19597)
- DiffIML (May 2026) learns the score function of the mask distribution with a lightweight mask-specific VAE, a small denoising UNet, edge supervision and an error prior. It uses 2 protocols over 8 non-generative and 3 generative benchmarks. Code: scu-zjz/DiffIML — [2605.16879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16879)
- Note: in family C, diffusion is used as a mask prior or decoder. It does not exploit the generative trace itself, so it inherits whatever signal the conditioning encoder captures (Inference).
- DiffForensics (CVPR 2024) and ForgDiffuser: not retrieved via arXiv search in this session. See Gaps.

**(D) MLLM / explainable localizers**
- FakeShield (ICLR 2025): a Domain-Tag-guided Explainable Forgery Detection Module (DTE-FDM) plus a Multi-modal Forgery Localization Module (MFLM, SAM-based), trained on the GPT-4o-annotated MMTD-Set. It covers Photoshop, DeepFake and AIGC-Editing. Code: zhipeixu/FakeShield — [2410.02761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02761)
- SIDA (CVPR 2025): an LMM with a mask decoder for detection, localization and explanation, trained on SID-Set (300K real, synthetic and tampered images) — [2412.04292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04292)
- LEGION (ICCV 2025): an MLLM that grounds and explains artifacts in *fully synthetic* images (SynthScars, 12,236 human-annotated images). It reports +3.31% mIoU and +7.75% F1 over the second-best expert on SynthScars. This is artifact segmentation, not edit localization — [2503.15264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15264)
- So-Fake-R1 (2025): an RL-trained (GRPO-style) VLM on So-Fake-Set (>2M images, 35 generators), with the 100K So-Fake-OOD set from excluded commercial models. It reports +4.5% localization IoU over the second-best method — [2505.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18660)
- ForgeryGPT (2024) — [2410.10238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10238); EditScout (an MLLM localizer for diffusion edits, Dec 2024) — [2412.03809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03809)
- 2025-2026 successors:
  - Propose-and-Rectify, a forensics-driven MLLM IML — [2508.17976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17976)
  - Training-free in-context forensic chain — [2510.10111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10111)
  - ForensicsTok, which drops the exogenous segmentation decoder in favor of tokenized mask modelling — [2606.24538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24538)
  - Agentic tool-augmented reasoning for explainable IFD — [2609.39066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39066)
  - Venus-DeFakerOne (InternVL2-2B + SAM2, unified detection + localization) — [2605.14091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14091)
  - Adversarial attacks on forensic VLMs (JECA²) — [2605.28609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28609)

**(E) Reconstruction / inversion residual based**
- ReGFLoW (Sep 2026) is the first *weakly supervised* (image-label-only) localizer for diffusion edits. The residual r = |I − D(E(I))| from the frozen SD1 VAE is used as dense spatial guidance in both feature and score spaces, with artifact-centric multiple-instance learning — [2609.36882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)
- GAP-SAM uses VAE reconstruction as a *global* token rather than a spatial residual. The paper argues that VAE-reconstruction artifacts "transfer poorly" to local inpainting when used as training data (Mask-VAE), yet are useful as a conditioning prior — [2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)
- In proactive variants, APT localizes tampering in *fully regenerated* images via embedded anchor-aligned perturbations. It is watermark-based, so it is not passive forensics — [2608.30656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30656)

**(F) AIGC-specific trace learners and dataset-coupled baselines**
- NFA-ViT (BR-Gen, 150K scene-level local edits of sky, ground and other background regions) mines heterogeneous regions through noise fingerprints. It then uses attention to "amplify" and propagate subtle traces image-wide — [2504.11922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11922)
- DiffusionPrint (Apr 2026) is a MoCo-style patch-contrastive CNN that learns a *generator fingerprint* robust to latent-decoder spectral distortion. Its output replaces Noiseprint++ as the secondary modality in TruFor or MMFusion. Code: mever-team/diffusionprint — [2604.12443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)
- ICL-Net (PromptForge-350k, Mar 2026) has a triple stream of SegFormer-B4 backbones: a noise/high-frequency stream, a trainable main stream, and a frozen copy of the main stream. An intra-image contrastive loss separates forged from real pixel features, combined with Dice and Focal losses weighted 1:4:20 — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386) (details from HTML)
- PANet (Impostor, Jun 2026) uses multi-scale Log-Gabor filters and a Riesz transform to build a monogenic signal, giving local phase features (cos φ, sin φ, log(1+A), φ), plus semantic-forensic consistency learning. Removing the phase block drops average IoU from 0.5978 to 0.5834 — [2606.04545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04545)
- EG-Mamba (Jul 2026) treats the mask as a state that is progressively updated using evidence, uncertainty and boundaries. It claims larger gains on unseen AIGC forgeries when trained only on conventional data (abs) — [2607.29156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29156)
- DiffSeg30k provides 30k multi-turn (up to 3 edits) images from 8 diffusion editors, with per-pixel *editor-ID* labels, and reframes localization as semantic segmentation. It found that segmentation models make strong whole-image classifiers but are fragile under distortions — [2511.19111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19111)

### Inferences
- Family A was designed around camera/ISP and compression traces. Modern editors decode the whole image through a VAE and regenerate it, so the "pristine reference" those models assume often no longer exists in the output. The 2026 papers that do best (FLAME, GAP-SAM, DiffusionPrint, PANet) all add an explicit *generation-pipeline* prior rather than a camera prior.
- Families C and D mostly change the *decoder*, not the *evidence*. Their generalization to new editors therefore depends on the encoder (SAM, CLIP, InternVL), which is semantic. This links directly to the object/semantic-bias failure mode (Q4).

### Gaps
- "GIFL" could not be identified. DiffForensics (CVPR 2024) and ForgDiffuser were not retrieved in this session. Their numbers are not reported here.
- CBDiff, SDiFL, SARIF, FASA, CLUE and EG-Mamba: only abstracts were read, so the per-dataset numbers are not verified.

## Q2. For each important method: core idea, forensic signal, training data, cross-dataset numbers, compute, code

### Takeaway
On modern instruction/MLLM edits, the 2026 SAM3/DINO-based hybrids report the strongest cross-generator numbers. Absolute OOD scores remain modest (IoU of roughly 0.2-0.5 on closed-source editors), and every leading method reports on a different benchmark and protocol, so cross-paper ranking is not possible.

### Cited Findings

**FLAME** — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178) (numbers from HTML Table 1)
- Signal: the authors argue theoretically that diffusion "suppresses local high-frequency variance", creating an energy gap against optical-imaging entropy. The LAD value per pixel is the mean of tanh(‖I(p)−I(q)‖²/τ²) over a hollow 3×3 neighbourhood.
- Training: MagicBrush plus a subset of SID. FLAME-F additionally uses only 500 EditStream images generated with Qwen-Image-Edit, with 20% replay.
- IoU/F1 by test set:

|---|---|---|
| MagicBrush (in-domain) | 0.538/0.650 | — |
| SID (in-domain) | 0.580/0.677 | — |
| CoCoGLIDE | 0.469/0.576 | — |
| AutoSplice | 0.501/0.624 | — |

### Inferences
- Compute and parameter counts are almost never reported for 2026 AIGC localizers (FLAME, GAP-SAM, ReGFLoW, DeFakerOne). Efficiency is an open, publishable axis, for example a SAM3-free LAD or phase-prior localizer.
- On LocateEdit-Bench, a vanilla SegFormer *beats* forensic IML architectures in-domain. Instruction-edit localization in-domain is therefore largely a semantic segmentation problem. The forensic contribution only shows up cross-editor, which is why cross-editor protocols are the only meaningful evaluation.

### Gaps
- No single paper evaluates FLAME, GAP-SAM, ICL-Net, PANet and ForenScope on a common test set.
- Code availability was not confirmed for GAP-SAM, ICL-Net, PANet, ForenScope and ReGFLoW.
- CBDiff, SDiFL, InpDiffusion and DiffIML numbers were not extracted (abstracts only).

## Q3. Robustness claims (JPEG/resize/social media, unseen generators) and size of drops

### Takeaway
Degradation robustness is reported in many different ways and is usually mild (≤1-5 IoU points for JPEG Q≥70). The largest drops come from *unseen generators* (2-3× IoU loss) and from *generative super-resolution* (−0.5 to −0.8 F1). Unseen closed-source MLLM editors (Nano-Banana) are consistently the worst case.

### Cited Findings
- ICL-Net (IoU 62.45 clean):
  - JPEG: QF90 61.92, QF80 61.37, QF70 60.23, QF60 58.82.
  - Random crop: 40% → 55.11.
  - JPEG QF80 + 20% crop: IoU 61.56 (<1-point drop).
  - Unseen editor: Nano-Banana falls from 51.43 in-domain to 18.95 held out — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
- FLAME (Table 3, which reports a clean average of 0.416/0.517 that does not match Table 1):
  - JPEG Q75 → 0.329/0.417; Gaussian blur k3 → 0.379/0.483; Gaussian noise → 0.270/0.371.
  - Resizing not tested — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)
- GAP-SAM claims it is best at every tested severity of JPEG, blur and resize. The numeric values are in figures only (not extracted) — [2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)
- LocateEdit-Bench: all models decline under JPEG and blur, with F1 dropping most — [2602.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577)
- TGIF2, generative super-resolution (Real-ESRGAN ×2) on spliced images:

| Method | Clean F1 | After Real-ESRGAN ×2 | After bicubic ×2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAT-Net | 0.87 | 0.06 | 0.76 |
| TruFor | 0.78 | 0.21 | 0.76 |
| MMFusion | 0.69 | 0.18 | — |

  ([2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613), HTML, column reading slightly ambiguous)
- TGIF2 cross-generator: SD2-trained TruFor scores 0.29 F1 on SDXL vs 0.49 in-domain — [2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- OpenSDI, from ReGFLoW's tables: MaskCLIP falls from 75.8 F1 (SD1.5) to 41.7 OOD (SD2.1/SDXL/SD3/FLUX). IML-ViT falls from 73.6 to 28.2 — [2609.36882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)
- DeFakerOne perturbation *accuracy* (detection): resize 69.23, noise 65.32, JPEG 76.16. No localization robustness is reported — [2605.14091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14091)
- DiffSeg30k reports "significant challenges ... particularly concerning robustness to image distortions" for segmentation baselines (abs) — [2511.19111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19111)
- So-Fake-OOD (100K images from excluded commercial models) is the main social-media-oriented OOD benchmark — [2505.18660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18660)

### Inferences
- Standard JPEG/blur robustness is close to solved for learned generative-trace localizers when such perturbations are seen as training augmentation. The unsolved robustness axes are:
  - (i) generator shift, especially to closed-source MLLM editors;
  - (ii) *generative* post-processing (SR, AI denoise or upscalers, re-encoding through another VAE), which overwrites the very trace being detected.
- Almost no paper evaluates real platform pipelines (e.g., WhatsApp/Facebook recompression plus resize) on *AIGC-edit* localization.

### Gaps
- No verified per-severity numbers for GAP-SAM robustness, or for SIDA/FakeShield localization robustness on modern editors.
- No found study of localization under social-network transmission for instruction edits (GPT-Image, Nano-Banana).

## Q4. Reported failure modes

### Takeaway
Six failure modes recur across independent papers:
1. Object/semantic bias and "boundary adhesion".
2. Fully regenerated images, where the whole image passes through the VAE.
3. Small edits and object removal.
4. Edits that leak beyond the mask, which makes ground truth unreliable.
5. Generative super-resolution and post-processing.
6. Recall-dominated misses on unseen sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Object bias / boundary adhesion:**
  - GAP-SAM names "boundary adhesion": fine-tuned segmenters snap predictions to semantic object contours rather than to true manipulation boundaries. The evidence is qualitative (Fig. 4) — [2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)
  - TGIF2: TruFor fine-tuned on semantic masks scores 0.50 F1 on SD2 semantic masks but 0.23 on random masks — [2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
  - DiffusionPrint lists "object bias" (firing on salient non-inpainted objects) among its failure cases — [2604.12443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)
- **Fully regenerated (FR) images:**
  - Off-the-shelf IFL F1 on TGIF2 FR images: TruFor 0.14, MMFusion 0.13, CAT-Net 0.02. Fine-tuning raises TruFor to 0.75 in-domain — [2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
  - DiffusionPrint is motivated by latent decoding "disrupting the camera-level noise patterns" — [2604.12443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12443)
- **Mask/ground-truth mismatch:**
  - ReGFLoW argues diffusion edits affect regions beyond the mask through VAE mapping and blending, so out-of-mask pixels are wrongly treated as authentic. The evidence is conceptual and qualitative — [2609.36882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)
  - "Edit spillover", where editors alter semantically related unspecified content, is documented on the generation side — [2603.17876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17876)
  - SIGMA notes that instruction-only grounding misses unintended editor side-effects — [2605.27924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27924)
  - PromptForge's DINOv3-difference masks fail on pure colour edits; a 200-sample volunteer check found 8 significant errors — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
  - LocateEdit-Bench masks come from ChatGPT + SAM3, i.e. object-level masks, which bakes in object bias — [2602.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577)
- **Small regions, removal, smooth areas:** FLAME's stated limitations are:
  - very small edits, weak boundaries and many similar objects;
  - object removal, where inpainting blends into the surrounding texture;
  - false positives on naturally smooth or repetitive authentic regions;
  - severe post-processing, ISP denoising and anti-forensic perturbations, which disrupt LAD statistics.

  ([2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178))
- **Removal and AI-edit transfer:** a splicing-trained SparseViT scores about 0.92 F1 on splicing but about 0.44 on removal and about 0.60 on AI-edit. Off-diagonal failures are mostly false negatives ("recall-dominated"). Background edits (larger areas, weaker boundaries) degrade differently — [2610.01778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01778)
- **Background/scene edits:** BR-Gen shows that object-centric datasets miss sky and ground edits. PANet reaches only 0.327 IoU on BR-Gen vs 0.786 on Impostor — [2504.11922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11922); [2606.04545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04545)
- **Generative super-resolution:** see Q3. Real-ESRGAN collapses CAT-Net, TruFor and MMFusion — [2603.28613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28613)
- **Pixel metrics inflate performance:** LocateEdit-Bench notes that pixel accuracy and AUC are dominated by the unedited background. GAP-SAM reports only a fixed 0.5 threshold — [2602.05577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05577)
- **Adversarial fragility of forensic VLMs:** [JECA² 2605.28609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28609); and of IFL models in general: [2506.12871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12871)

### Inferences
- Automatic mask generation is used by PromptForge (DINOv3 difference), LocateEdit (ChatGPT+SAM3), SIGMA (semantic difference) and BR-Gen (perception pipeline). It is a *shared* weakness: training and test labels are object-shaped by construction, so benchmarks reward object bias. This is a concrete opening for a paper on non-semantic ground truth, such as a pixel-difference-in-latent mask or a random-mask edit protocol like TGIF2's, for instruction editors.
- The tension between FR images and mask-supervision is fundamental. With MLLM editors (GPT-Image, Nano-Banana), *every* pixel is regenerated, so "edited region" must be defined semantically ("what changed meaningfully") rather than forensically ("what was generated"). Few methods state which definition they target.

### Gaps
- No quantitative boundary-adhesion metric exists in the literature found.
- No study reports localization failure as a function of edit-area fraction for MLLM editors, although FLAME and GIFTBench note small-region difficulty qualitatively.

## Q5. Current best cross-generator localization on modern AI edits (numbers and benchmarks)

### Takeaway
On truly unseen modern MLLM/flow editors, the best verified numbers are IoU of about 0.19-0.57 depending on the editor. Closed-source Nano-Banana is the hardest (about 0.19-0.39 IoU). Higher averages, such as GAP-SAM's 79.8 F1 and PANet's 0.85 F1, come from benchmarks containing older diffusion inpainting or from in-domain editors. There is no common leaderboard.

### Cited Findings
- **Nano-Banana, held out:**
  - ICL-Net leave-one-out IoU: 18.95; average over 4 held-out editors 41.5 — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
  - FLAME (trained on MagicBrush+SID): IoU/F1 0.216/0.295. FLAME-F (500 Qwen-Image-Edit samples added) reaches 0.391/0.454 — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)
- **FLUX Kontext, held out:**
  - FLAME 0.285/0.391; FLAME-F 0.446/0.548 — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)
  - ICL-Net leave-one-out IoU 53.53 — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
- **Step1X-Edit, held out:** ICL-Net IoU 57.12 — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
- **BAGEL, held out:** ICL-Net IoU 36.39 — [2603.29386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29386)
- **Diffusion/inpainting OOD mixes:**
  - GAP-SAM (trained on FLUX ControlNet COCO): 6-set average F1 79.8, including UltraEdit 49.8 and DiffSeg30K 69.0 — [2608.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20929)
  - OpenSDI OOD (SD2.1/SDXL/SD3/FLUX) best F1 is 41.7 (MaskCLIP, trained on SD1.5), per ReGFLoW's table — [2609.36882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36882)
- **Mixed-source OOD:** ForenScope averages 0.671 F1 over 9 OOD datasets when trained on GIFTBench — [2610.01778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01778)
- **GPT-Image / GPT-Image-2:** I found no paper reporting pixel-level localization. DeFakerOne reports detection only, on 71 samples — [2605.14091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14091)
- **Qwen-Image-Edit:** FLAME 0.321/0.408 (unseen); FLAME-F 0.482/0.603 is not unseen — [2606.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02178)

### Inferences
- A fair statement for a paper: "best reported zero-shot cross-editor IoU on closed-source MLLM editors is below 0.25 (Nano-Banana: ICL-Net 18.95, FLAME 21.6)". Few-shot adaptation with about 500 samples nearly doubles it (FLAME-F 39.1). Few-shot / continual adaptation to new editors (EditStream-style) is therefore a high-leverage research direction.
- Benchmarks for 2026 MLLM editors (PromptForge-350k, LocateEdit-Bench, Impostor, GIFTBench, DiffSeg30k) use different editors and mask protocols. A unified leave-one-editor-out protocol with non-object (random/attribute/background) edits plus generative post-processing would be a clear contribution.

### Gaps
- No pixel-level numbers were found for GPT-Image(-1/-2), Seedream/SeedEdit or Gemini-native editing beyond Nano-Banana.
- No cross-paper common evaluation exists. GAP-SAM, ICL-Net, FLAME and PANet were not compared head-to-head.
- Release status was not verified for the PromptForge-350k, LocateEdit-Bench and Impostor datasets (LocateEdit: "upon acceptance").
